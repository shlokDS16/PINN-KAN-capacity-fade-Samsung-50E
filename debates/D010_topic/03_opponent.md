## 0. Summary verdict
The topic, as written, is a semi-empirical stress-life model with spline stress factors and per-cell random effects, evaluated on one small-cell, single-temperature dataset, whose headline accuracy claim sits at (or below) the replicate noise floor and whose novel claim (identifiability) is tested mainly on synthetic data generated by the fitted model. It is not a PINN paper, the KAN is a GAM by construction at the proposed size, and the programme as scheduled does not fit one CPU week. **Verdict: REVISE (not accept); reject as written.** Three conditions in section 11.

## 1. Is it still a "PINN + KAN" paper? (bait-and-switch)

**1.1 [fatal for the brief, major for the science] There is no PINN in the headline model.**
- Evidence: proposal 3.5: "the 'residual' is identically zero and there is no loss weight to tune. That is physics-informed in structure ... not in the strict PINN collocation sense." The strict PINN is variant M_P, "reduced to a control" (9.1 A2/C3). The proposer's own open question D4.
- Consequence: the brief (PINN + KAN) is met by a mixed-effects ODE with a spline stress map. A JES reviewer who reads "physics-informed" will look for a physics loss; the author asked for a PINN; the title avoids the word ("physics-informed Kolmogorov-Arnold networks") while the method contains no network trained against a physics residual. Reading the abstract and the method will feel like a switch.
- Worse, the proposal itself gives the reason the PINN cannot be the headline: with 19 points per cell and a trivial scalar ODE, shooting through the ODE strictly dominates collocation (exact enforcement, no weight lambda_res). So the soft PINN M_P is dominated by design; the "does the residual add anything" control is expected to answer "no", which makes the PINN decorative by construction, a pure negative control.
- What would answer it: a setting where the PINN formulation is *needed* rather than merely allowed. The only candidate visible in the proposal is collocation at unobserved *stress* points (a solution surface y(Theta, s) with ODE residual enforced between the 63 measured conditions). That is not what M_P does (collocation is over Theta only, "self-referential" horizon Theta_hor). Unless M_P beats the hard ODE on Test B/C by a preregistered margin, the PINN claim is empty. Alternatively the author must explicitly accept a non-PINN headline (D4) before the topic is adopted.

**1.2 [major] Self-referential collocation in M_P.** Theta_hor is "where the current model's own forecast crosses y = 0.4" (3.5): the collocation region is defined by the model being trained, so the physics residual is enforced where the model already extrapolates; there is no external information in that region (the R is the model's own G). It can only regularise toward the model's own prior. Answer: the M_P versus hard-ODE contrast with a fixed, label-free collocation range.

**1.3 [minor] "Network".** At width [3,2] the KAN is six univariate spline functions plus a SiLU base term (see 2.1). "Physics-informed Kolmogorov-Arnold networks" in the title oversells a six-function additive model.

## 2. Is the KAN essential?

**2.1 [major, near-fatal for the "KAN" in the title] At the proposed size the KAN is a GAM by identity.** KAN [3,2] (3.3) is a sum of 3 univariate spline edges per output, i.e. a log-additive generalised additive model. The R4 control "additive-spline GAM" is therefore the same model class with a different basis; the proposal cannot distinguish "KAN" from "GAM" at [3,2]. Only [3,h,2] with h in {2,3} differs, and then identifiability (the paper's selling point) is lost because the hidden layer is not identifiable (sum of nested splines; permutation and scale symmetries).
- Evidence: proposal 3.3, 7 (ablation row), 1 (R4).
- Answer: a KAN-versus-spline-GAM contrast at the same edge basis in which the KAN uses a hidden layer and still wins on a preregistered endpoint; without it the title must not say KAN.

**2.2 [major] The R4 test is unfair in both directions.**
- "Log-polynomial (GAM) degree 2" has no cross terms; the only place a KAN with a hidden layer can win is on interactions (charge rate x DoD, i.e. plating depends on the electrode potential reached), and a degree-2 polynomial with cross terms (10 coefficients) is not in the ablation list. Conversely the additive-spline GAM will match the KAN on everything additive.
- S2 (hinge onset in ln C_chg, only in the ln A_2 term) is additive: an additive-spline GAM represents it exactly as a KAN [3,2] does; only the quadratic GAM loses. S2 therefore tests "spline versus quadratic", not "KAN versus GAM". It is a straw man for the polynomial and says nothing about KAN superiority.
- The one extrapolation test (Test C) favours the polynomial: B-spline KAN edges (pykan, fixed grid on the training range) carry no information outside the grid; power-law or polynomial extrapolate. The proposal says the hinge law "predicts the hardest extrapolation" for Test C but the KAN has no mechanism to extrapolate a hinge beyond its grid. Expect the KAN to lose Test C to the log-linear model.
- Where could a KAN genuinely win? Only with a multiplicative or compositional interaction in the generator that is not additive and not low-order polynomial (e.g. A_2 depends on a product of C_chg and a function of DoD through anode potential). The proposal has no such generator (S1 additive, S2 additive, S3 differs in the *knee*, not the stress map, S4 GP draw). Answer: a generator with a non-additive stress law, and the KAN beating both the interaction polynomial and the additive spline GAM on recovery and on Test B.

**2.3 [minor] Symbolic extraction on a 3-input, 63-condition map** with library {linear, quadratic, ln, exp, power, hinge} is a model-selection among 6 forms on 63 points; "rounding survival <= 1.10" is nearly guaranteed for a 2 to 3 coefficient formula and says nothing about the KAN. A contrast with a symbolic fit of the GAM would give the same ratio. Answer: report the same rounding test for the GAM and the polynomial.

## 3. Data

**3.1 [major] ISU-ILCC is a weak primary dataset for a JES physical claim.**
- 250 mAh 502030 NMC/graphite Li-polymer pouch cells from a single manufacturer (README 3.1), 30 C only (README 3.2), two cyclers/incubators at two sites (README 3.2), roughly 19 capacity points per cell (scratch median 19). Strong JES PINN/KAN papers (see the audit's Wang-family and PIKAN/PGKAN rows) report on 4 to 8 datasets, EV-format cells, several chemistries.
- Actual cycle protocol per README 3.3: "constant-current/constant-voltage charging step and a constant-voltage discharging step", DoD "determined by estimating the voltage cutoffs". So "DoD" is an *estimated* quantity, tied to an unreported SOC window position (mean SOC) which is not in the stress vector; DoD and window position are confounded, and the "mean DoD" is a measured group property (proposal 3.1) not a design variable.
- RPT schedule differs by group: groups 20 to 64 have an extra RPT at 0.5 week and groups 30 to 64 an extra pulse cycle (README 3.4). Cells in groups 1 to 19 get a different amount of early information and different extra ageing from the RPT itself. The window information (and the identifiability of delta_c) therefore changes systematically over the design.

**3.2 [major] The ISU authors already did this, on this data, with a hierarchical model, and the proposal has not read them.**
- arXiv 2307.08382 (abstract only, my reading): 225 NMC/graphite cells, widely varying charge rate, discharge rate and DoD; lifetime prediction from the first <= 15 % of data; 15.1 % MAPE in-distribution, 21.8 % MAPE out-of-distribution with a *hierarchical Bayesian regression*, features from Q-V degradation-mode information.
- The proposal's "hierarchical window-MAP" is the same idea, and the proposal's own noise floor (within-condition sd of ln t80 = 0.19; leave-one-replicate-out condition-median error 0.151, scratch) is the same size as 15.1 % MAPE. That is: published early-life EOL accuracy on this design is already at the replicate noise floor.
- The proposal chooses a different endpoint (retention RMSE, Test B) from the authors' MAPE on EOL, so no direct comparison with 15.1 / 21.8 is available; a reviewer will ask for it. B8 (the authors' own method) is listed "unread".
- The Q-V curves (`Q_interpolated`, on disk) that give LLI/LAM degradation modes are ignored, although they are the one available *electrochemical* check of the "SEI-like" versus "knee" mechanisms (see 4.3).
- Answer: full-text read of 2307.08382 and a comparison on its metric (EOL MAPE, in- and out-of-distribution), plus use of the Q-V curves to test the mechanism labels.

**3.3 [fatal as scheduled] Dependence on 10.2 GB `Cycling_json` (D1).** The time coordinate Theta (equivalent full cycles) needs per-cycle Ah; the whole model and all claims in "throughput units" depend on it (3.1). INVENTORY records Zenodo throughput of about 50 KB/s at times and DNS failures, and the data steward only attempted files under 300 MB. At 50 KB/s 10.2 GB is about 57 hours; the proposed pilot P0 range-fetches 12 cells and the full set is a "yes" request. The fallback F1 (weeks plus an analytic cycles-per-week) removes the coordinate that is the proposal's rationale (3.1 (i)), and restricts claims to "per week of continuous cycling". Answer: a measured fetch of the full per-cycle data before the topic is adopted, or a dataset where per-cycle throughput is already on disk.

**3.4 [major] Is Theta even the right coordinate on this dataset?** Cycle duration scales with DoD / C-rate, so cumulative throughput per week is set mostly by the C-rates and CV steps, only weakly by DoD (my reasoning; to be verified on the 12 pilot cells). The "DoD exponent" in throughput units then absorbs the number of cycles and the calendar/RPT-driven loss of low-DoD cells (e.g. group 26 at 4.16 % DoD, t80 = 29.3 weeks, scratch output). Per-Theta rate exponents on C-rate differ from per-week exponents by a trivial shift of about one unit. The "stress laws" are then partly a coordinate artefact; the proposal itself lists "no calendar-cycle separation" as a limit (5), yet its model has no calendar term and treats all fade as throughput-driven. Answer: show that the learned exponent ranking is invariant to the coordinate (weeks versus Theta) and that low-DoD cells are not fit by absorbing calendar loss.

**3.5 [major] Selection on the outcome.** Cells with t80 <= W are excluded from the W-window evaluation (5.2 % at W = 4, scratch). These are the worst cells, concentrated at high stress (G9: 5.24 weeks; Test C's high-charge-rate range). Excluding by the outcome biases every metric and especially Test C (charge > 2 C) toward survivors. Answer: report the excluded cells by condition and a sensitivity analysis that includes them (e.g. scoring them as failures).

**3.6 [major] A better primary dataset exists on the proposer's own list: Samsung INR21700-50E (279 cells, 71 conditions, temperature x DoD x C-rate, calendar and cycle ageing, space-filling design; INVENTORY A2).** It is the only dataset in INVENTORY in which an Arrhenius term is testable with more than 3 levels, calendar and cycling can be separated, and the cells are an EV format. The proposal relegates it to "reserve" (5) mainly on a download-size argument that evaporates because ISU also needs 10.2 GB (3.3). Against Samsung: 10.3 GB in 278 zips, not yet inspected, the cadence and the noise floor unknown, 21700 cell with possible fewer points. The net is that the proposal chose a dataset because it is on disk (summaries only), not because it best supports the claim. Answer: a one-day look at the Samsung per-cell schema (cadence, number of RPTs, replicate structure); if it has weekly or cycle-level capacity for 70 conditions it should be the primary.

**3.7 [major] The secondary dataset cannot carry what it is asked to carry.** Sandia mirror: 61 cells in 3 chemistries (LFP 18, NCA 22, NMC 21), temperatures 15/25/35 C (9/34/18 cells), DoD only 0-100 % (57) and 20-80 % (4) in the mirror (INVENTORY A6). So: no DoD law, only 3 temperature levels (no curvature of Arrhenius, as the proposal concedes in 9.3), 4 cells in the second DoD level, and per chemistry the replicate structure (names suffix a/b) is unverified. Within a chemistry the stress matrix has perhaps 6 to 9 conditions. The Arrhenius result will be a point estimate per chemistry. The file is also truncated (111.6 of 115.2 MB). This is a footnote dataset, not a second dataset.

## 4. Electrochemistry

**4.1 [major] The two-mechanism backbone is not identifiable as written, and the mechanism names are unsupported.**
- dy_1/dTheta = A_1/(y_1 + y_0) with y_0 > 0 and dy_2/dTheta = A_2 exp(gamma y), observed only through the sum y = y_1 + y_2 (and a single capacity channel). Fade is the only observation; two mechanisms are separable only through curve shape, and the shapes (decelerating then accelerating) can be produced by many pairs of (A_1, y_0) and (A_2, gamma). The proposal tests this only with synthetic data generated by the same form (S1).
- The first four weeks (the window) contain the break-in/formation-type transient, which the SEI term with a small y_0 (infinite initial rate as y_0 tends to 0) must absorb; y_0 is "bounded below by an initial prior from the first reference test" (3.6) i.e. set partly by the data it then explains.
- The window of 4 weeks is about a third of the median life, mostly in the regime where mechanism 2 has not begun. delta_c2 (the late acceleration offset of the unseen cell) is then determined by the prior N(0, Sigma), not by the cell. The "forecast from the window" reduces to a stress-law forecast plus one cell-level offset, a point 3.7's "hostile reading" already worries about; Test B removes the replicate median but does not show the window carries information on the knee.

**4.2 [major] What an electrochemist would say.**
- sqrt(t)-type SEI growth is a *time* (calendar) law driven by SEI electron-tunnelling/solvent diffusion and by the SOC held, not by throughput; using throughput as the clock for the SEI term while the dataset cannot separate calendar and cyclic loss mislabels the mechanism. Cyclic SEI repair/growth on a graphite surface that cracks is throughput- and DoD-linked, but then A_1 depends on the SOC window (position), which is not in the stress vector.
- exp(gamma y) as "plating / knee / active-material loss surrogate" lumps at least three mechanisms with different stress dependencies (plating: charge rate, anode potential, temperature; LAM: DoD/volume change; electrolyte consumption/pouch effects in a 250 mAh pouch). Calling it plating is not testable on capacity alone. The proposal says "without claiming mechanism identification, which is exactly what C2 tests", but the model, the figures and the title talk about "laws", "SEI-type" and "knee", and a JES reader will take that as a mechanism claim.
- Plating onset is threshold-like in anode potential, a function of rate, SOC and temperature jointly, not a power law in C-rate; a global exponent on C_chg from 63 conditions is a local sensitivity over a designed range, not a constant of nature.
- Cell self-heating at 3 C charge in a 250 mAh pouch means the cell temperature is not the incubator's 30 C at high rate; the "30 C" is an assumption and the C-rate effect carries a hidden temperature effect.
- Learned stress exponents from 63 conditions have no external physical comparison in the proposal. Without a measured quantity (LLI/LAM from Q-V, post-mortem, resistance from the pulse tests the groups 30-64 include) they cannot be called physical. Answer: compare the two mechanisms' contributions with degradation modes from `Q_interpolated` and with resistance growth on groups 30-64, at least qualitatively and with a preregistered sign test.

**4.3 [minor] Mean DoD is a group-level measured property** (proposal 3.1) rather than a design level; condition-to-condition variation in realised DoD is therefore measurement of a property of the cycler's cutoff estimation, not an independent variable.

## 5. Identifiability programme

**5.1 [major] Circularity.** Truth is anchored on fits of the same backbone to the real training cells (4.1), S1's generating model equals the fitted model, and the pass criterion is recovery of the three principal slopes. In S1 the true stress law is log-linear, i.e. inside the model class of a regularised KAN (which can collapse to a line). A consistent estimator recovering a parameter of a well-specified, low-dimensional model from simulated data at the design's own noise level is expected statistics (consistency, Cramer-Rao), not a result. Using a different step size in the generator avoids the "inverse crime" only numerically.
- The informative cases are S3 and S4 (misspecified, free); for them the proposal has no pass/fail criterion ("measures how recovery degrades").
- The threshold language ("proposal: median relative error above 25 percent" for kill, 10 percent for pass "fixed after pilot P3") is arbitrary and set after seeing the pilot, so it is not preregistration in the sense of the CONTRACT. Relative error of a slope is ill-defined when the true slope is near zero, and anchoring truth on real fits can produce a near-zero exponent (e.g. discharge rate). Replace-by-interval criteria (coverage of the true value by bootstrap/profile-likelihood intervals) are absent from the primary criteria, although Fisher information and profile likelihood are listed as secondary.
- Answer: coverage of the truth by the interval, S3/S4 thresholds fixed in advance, truth drawn from ranges not fitted on the same real data.

**5.2 [major] The free-G contrast is a straw man.** PIKAN's G has no stress input (PIKAN.md section 1 and 7: "No temperature, C-rate, DOD or protocol inputs"); the proposal creates a "PIKAN-style free-G" with s as an input and then shows that an unconstrained G(y, Theta, s) is not unique. That is (i) a statement about unconstrained function classes (any universal approximator with 19-point trajectories and a 3-D condition space is non-unique) and (ii) not a statement about PIKAN or PGKAN, whose actual defect (unstable parabolas across datasets, rounding collapse, PIKAN.md 6.5 and 6.6) is on four datasets with no stress input. A fair contrast would include a flexible-but-constrained alternative (e.g. the backbone with free shape in y only, or the structured backbone with a non-KAN stress map), which would show where the identifiability actually comes from: the backbone, not the KAN. The proposal's own R4 and R2 are only evaluated against the two extremes.
- Also, R2 reads "paired difference not above the 2-fold margin": a 2-fold margin is large and arbitrary; with 20 draws per cell the test is under-powered in the small designs.

**5.3 [major] The result in 4.2 (design-size sweeps) multiplies compute beyond the CPU week.** (number of conditions 4) x (replicates 3) x (cadence 2) x (noise 3) x (window 3) = 216 cells x 20 draws = 4,320 full structured fits, and again for the free-G and the GAM, each with a KAN-ODE integrated by RK4 over 400 steps, L-BFGS, per-cell offsets and nested hyper-parameters. Even at one minute per fit this is 72 h for one model family. Add 200 bootstrap resamples x 7 folds on real cells, Optuna 60 trials per family, 5 seeds, 10 baseline families. See 7.1.

**5.4 [minor] Design orthogonality** (pairwise correlations 0.00, -0.10, -0.34, condition number 1.44, scratch) concerns main effects only. The interactions that [3,h,2] is meant to find are not orthogonal in this design (about 15 conditions sit at DoD around 97 %, 23 at discharge rate 0.5; hand-transcribed Table A1 in `isu_profile.py`, not yet rechecked against the PDF).

## 6. Novelty and positioning

**6.1 [major] "Identifiability" is overclaimed as novel.** The audit's NOVEL rating (0.65) rests on KAN/PINN-battery searches and on abstracts. Gasper et al. 2021 (JES 168, 020502), by its title as quoted in the audit's table and the DOI, is about statistical validation and model identification of algebraic stress-life models, which is the same question for the semi-empirical family (audit rows: "identifiability-adjacent", abstract only). Structural and practical identifiability, profile likelihood and sloppiness are standard in battery-model parameterisation literature (physics-based models); the audit did not search that literature, and the proposal cites only Raue (2009), "from memory, to verify". PIKAN/PGKAN full texts, JECS addd4e, and arXiv 2607.16864 are unread. A reviewer who knows the semi-empirical aging literature (Schmalstieg, Wang 2011, Gasper) will say the stress-law structure and its parameter confidence intervals are the usual content of those papers.
- Against PiDDM (arXiv 2607.29095): the same family of rate law (SEI Arrhenius t^alpha + LLI DoD^b1 Ah^b2) is public; the proposal's backbone differs by details and by a KAN stress map. The comparison "ours versus PiDDM-style" cannot separate backbone from KAN.
- Against Kuzhiyil (UDE, Applied Energy): "known state equation plus learned correction" is public (audit).
- The remaining novelty is the combination of synthetic recovery and a free-G contrast, which is a statement about the authors' own constructed baseline (5.2).

**6.2 [major] An editor's reading.** The abstract-level claim is "identifiable stress laws ... from physics-informed KANs", supported mostly by synthetic experiments and a design-size sweep. JES publishes testing/test-procedure papers and applied modelling; it expects experimental validation of a physical claim. The editor's likely reading: ML-methods paper with a new training regime and synthetic benchmark, whose real-data headline is non-inferiority to LightGBM. The "design guidance" argument (smallest design at which the exponents are recovered) is a statistics-of-experiments result, not an electrochemistry result, and is conditional on the model class being correct (the proposer's own 9.2). Risk of desk rejection: real. Answer: an experimentally verifiable physical claim (see 4.2) plus real-data identifiability reported with intervals.

## 8. Results risk

**8.1 [major to fatal for the accuracy claim] The primary endpoint is probably at the noise floor.**
- Replicate floor (scratch): leave-one-replicate-out condition-median |ln error| of t80 = 0.151 median (mean 0.196); within-condition sd of ln t80 = 0.19 (median over 63 conditions). The published early-life EOL MAPE on this design is 15.1 % (in-distribution) and 21.8 % (out of distribution) (arXiv 2307.08382, abstract). Retention RMSE over the forecast set is dominated by knee-timing error, which is the same cell-to-cell scatter for all models.
- Test B has no replicates in training, so the condition median is unavailable, but a tuned GP with ARD on (ln stress, window summary) with 56 training conditions in a smooth 3-D stress space is a strong baseline; ln life is smooth in stress (between-cell sd of ln t80 0.68 vs within 0.19 means stress explains most variance, scratch). A 2.5-parameter physics model cannot beat the floor; the best it can do is match a GP. The proposal anticipates this: P1 "if the best flexible baseline already sits at the replicate noise floor (headroom ratio below about 1.3), the accuracy claim R3 cannot be won; reframe to identifiability-first". The gate threshold of 1.3 is arbitrary and the proposal does not state a prior probability of tie.
- If it ties, the paper is a non-inferiority result ("at least as accurate as LightGBM") plus a synthetic study, which fails the brief ("results at the level of the strongest recent JES papers") and then 6.2 applies.

**8.2 [major] Contamination of the preregistration.** P1 (day 1) runs tuned LightGBM/GP on Test A/B at W = 2, 4, 6, i.e. on the test folds, and the non-inferiority margin is "set after P1 (headroom pilot) and before confirmatory runs" (6). The margin and the headroom gate are thus set after the baselines' test performance is seen, on the same folds that become confirmatory. Test folds are touched twice (contradicts CONTRACT 6: "test cells are touched once per frozen configuration"). Answer: run P1 on training folds only, with the margin derived from inner CV.

**8.3 [minor] The primary statistics** (Wilcoxon on 63 condition-level units with cluster bootstrap, Holm over 5 contrasts) are sound, but the units for Test B are 63 conditions in 7 folds of 9; the folds are "stratified over the standardised stress space", i.e. every test condition has a close neighbour in training, and the effective number of independent conditions in a 3-D design with 63 points is lower than 63. The CI is likely too narrow if neighbour correlation is high.

## 9. What would make it look weaker than the best JES PINN/KAN papers

- One real dataset of small 250 mAh pouch cells at one temperature, plus a 61-cell, 3-chemistry footnote (3.1, 3.7). The audit's strongest comparators (PI-EKAN, 8 datasets; PGKAN, 4 datasets, 387 cells; Wang-family, 4 datasets) have more datasets and cells.
- Headline errors likely several percentage points of retention over the forecast range, versus the 0.3 to 1 % MAPE regime that JES readers see in the PIKAN/PINN4SOH family (PIKAN.md section 5). Those numbers are estimation-with-features, not forecasts, but readers compare tables.
- A comparison table mostly of the proposer's own re-implementations (PiDDM-style, PIKAN-style, Wang-style, all task-adapted), disclosed as such; a reviewer will discount them. No code for PiDDM; PIKAN has no licence.
- No uncertainty (excluded by design, retired topic), no EV-format cells, no impedance/diagnostic link.
- Figures: `FIGURE_FORMS.md` is empty, the figure forms were not checked against any JES exemplar (proposal 9.3), CONTRACT 6a requires it.

## 12. What result would force rejection of the claim
- Median slope error above 25 % on S1 (R1) with the real noise; or free-G identified as well as the structured model (R2) (both are already in the proposal's kill list).
- The additive-spline GAM or interaction polynomial matching the KAN on Test B and Test C (R4): then there is no KAN claim.
- Primary endpoint within the replicate noise floor for GP/LightGBM and ours (tie), with identifiability intervals wider than the between-condition spread of the exponents on the real cells.
- Exponent ranking changing between weeks and Theta coordinates.

## 13. The three changes without which I would recommend rejection
1. **Settle what the paper is.** Either a PINN headline that wins (collocation in stress space or a measured advantage of the residual), or the author explicitly accepting a hard-physics ODE headline (D4) and dropping "PINN" from the brief and the claims; and a KAN-versus-GAM test at the same basis in which the KAN has to win on something real (interaction, non-additive law), else the KAN leaves the title.
2. **A primary dataset and a coordinate that support the claim, verified before adoption.** Per-cycle throughput on disk (or Samsung 50E checked), the ISU authors' paper read with a comparison on their EOL metric, coordinate invariance shown, and a mechanism check against measured degradation modes/resistance so that "SEI" and "knee" are not labels.
3. **A realistic, honest plan with a tie case.** Measured per-fit compute, a compute budget that fits the week, P1 run on training folds only (margin and thresholds fixed before any test fold), interval-coverage identifiability criteria with thresholds fixed in advance, and a stated paper if the accuracy endpoint ties (including whether that paper is publishable in JES).

## 14. Verdict
**REVISE.** Not acceptable as written (the brief is not met, the KAN claim is not testable at the proposed size, the accuracy claim is likely a tie, and one week of CPU does not cover the programme). The identifiability question is the one part with potential, but only if it is reformulated around interval coverage on real data and an electrochemical check, and if the data and compute dependencies (3.3, 7.1) are resolved first. Evidence that would re-open an accept: a completed per-cycle fetch (or Samsung schema check), a measured seconds-per-fit, the ISU authors' full text with a metric comparison, and a training-fold-only headroom pilot showing a margin of at least the proposer's own threshold over a tuned GP.
