## 1. Findings

### F1. The expected result leaves "PINN" and "KAN" without a demonstrated benefit (fatal)
- Evidence: PREREG_v3 section 1 "Expected endpoint": H1b "N or none (KAN tie)", H2 "unresolved", H1c "gate closed or unresolved", title T3. D011 judge 3.1 says the same. Pilot P5 (`runs/pilot_P5/RESULT.md` line 25): with the prespecified collocation the soft PINN beat the hard ODE by median ratio 0.96 (4 of 5), a 4 % gain at an MDE of 9-22 % (PREREG section 1 Power). P4: KAN 0.081 vs GAM 0.103 on a generator the author designed, GAM floor 0.05-0.07. P3: best data-only learner already at the replicate reference (ISU 0.90).
- Attack (supervisor): the paper's two named methods are, on the design's own forecast, neither shown necessary. Title T3 ("... with physics-informed neural networks and Kolmogorov-Arnold networks") names both. The only expected win is H6 (against a parametric model) and H1a (against a residual-free network). A reader of the abstract cannot say what PINN+KAN bought.
- The PINN is redundant by construction: D013 (a) chose one shared state factor "needed for the closed form" so that the law integrates exactly (Eq. 5). The hard ODE A2 is then the exact solution of the same law, and F is slack. The design makes the PINN's only role (integrating the law) unnecessary, then tests whether the slack helps (H2). The KAN's hidden layer `[5,h,1]` with h up to 8 and G up to 12 on 32 training conditions per fold is a spline MLP; the KAN-specific selling points (symbolic discovery in PIKAN/PGKAN, readable edge functions) are not used: no symbolic extraction is in the plan except an appendix mention, and Fig. 5 shows additive partial dependences of a non-additive map, not KAN edge functions.
- Alternative explanation of any H1a/H6 win: the baselines are weak by design (see F6, F7), so a tuned parametric neural model wins regardless of PINN or KAN.
- Forces rejection of the "PINN+KAN is what works" reading: H2 not A and H1b not S (the stated likely outcome).
- What answers it: a statement, before the freeze, of what contribution survives if H1b and H2 are null, written into the claim contract and accepted by Shlok in a ledger row (D011 told him; no row records his answer; `checklist 17.2` has the told-on date still open).

### F3. Median estimand: acceptable in form, risky in effect (major)
- Evidence: D012; PREREG section 1 Estimand, 9.3, 9.10; Fig. 4 and Fig. 3 demos.
- The exact sign test on per-condition log ratios is defensible and level-safe. Problems: (a) it is blind to catastrophic conditions. The Fig. 3 demo itself shows A1 at 24.02 pp against A10 at 7.46 pp in one panel, and the Fig. 4 demo shows H6 with the median ratio favouring A1 while the mean paired difference is -1.65 pp [-8.39, 0.79] (comparator better). Under the rules the abstract still says "typical held-out condition". A JES reader of a forecasting paper cares about the worst case and the mean. (b) The sign test discards effect size: 28 of 40 conditions with a 0.1 % gain pass the 2 % margin only if the median does; the test is on L - m, so this is partly handled, but there is no tail estimand at all (worst-condition error, share of conditions with error above a stated tolerance). (c) D011 itself chose the mean-targeting rule first and fell back to the median only because the test failed its level; the estimand changed to suit the available test.
- JES acceptability: median-of-condition ratios with exact order-statistic intervals and tiers S/N/none are unfamiliar. Reviewers expect RMSE, MAE, MAPE and R^2 on test cells with a stated mean. These are secondary here.
- What answers it: the abstract states the mean pp difference and the worst-condition error beside the median whenever their signs disagree, as a fixed rule.

### F4. Independence, dependence structure and power of the sign test (major)
- Evidence: PREREG 9.1 ("Neighbour dependence and fold-shared fits are not modelled"), T1 power table (assumed error correlation 0.95 / 0.90 / 0.75), 4 ("holds its size for any continuous distribution").
- The size guarantee needs independent conditions. The 40 conditions share folds (one tuned configuration per fold), share the same 5-D design with deliberately spread neighbours (section 4: neighbourhood members go to different folds), and share labs (35 C all INT, 45 C 66 of 75 HM). Positive dependence between conditions' signs inflates the type-I rate; the simulations in T1 drew independent conditions. The exact test's size at n = 40 is 0.019 or 0.0083, not 0.025; the claimed "family-wise 0.025" is not what is achieved and the Holm levels are applied to p-values ordered by the **N-tier** statistic while the S-tier test (a different null) is run at the rank's level (section 9.5). Holm validity needs the ordering by the p-values of the hypotheses actually tested; ordering by another statistic can exceed the family level when two S nulls are true.
- Power: the correlation 0.95 for H1a is called "same-model contrasts" (section 1) but A9 has no law and no residual; it is not the same model. At 0.75 the MDE is 17-22 % and the pilots show gains of 4 % (P5) to about 20 % on a synthetic KAN/GAM recovery metric. H1b and H2 will end "unresolved" with near certainty; the preregistration cannot adjudicate the KAN.
- Alpha accounting: H2 (three tests), H5, H1c, NC-2, X1, H3, ISU, SNL each at 0.025 outside the Holm family. "Family-wise" covers three tests only.
- Forces rejection of the stated size: a simulation in which condition errors share fold-level and lab-level random effects with the left-skew shape of ISU gives a sign-test rate above 1.2 x nominal.

### F5. H1a is a low bar (major)
- Evidence: PREREG 7.1 (A9: "F, same z_c, inputs, window inversion, no residual, no law"), H1a "residual does work".
- A9 is a tanh network extrapolating in time from a window of about 4 check-ups (weeks 1-5 for cycling cells: D010 re-audit item on cadence; P0). Without the law, the only thing that shapes the extrapolation is the training cells' shape. H1a at tier S says "a network told the functional form of fade beats a network not told it", which is the expected result for any structured model. It is not evidence that the physics residual helps in the sense of the PINN literature, which would be a comparison against the same law without F (that is H2, not in the family). The family contains the easy PINN contrast and excludes the hard one.
- Cheapest alternative explanation of a H1a win: A9's extrapolation is poor; the residual is a regulariser on the extrapolation region (collocation to 1.1 x the longest record).
- Also (E1 design): A9 gets a different (larger) tuned space than A1's F, "conservative", yet the data-only learners that a reviewer would use as the data-only reference (A11/A12) are excluded from the family.

### F7. The comparator set is thin for JES and the data-only learners are gated out (major)
- Evidence: PREREG 7.1 ("LSTM, Transformer, PiDDM reimplementation, BatLiNet/FlowBatt: out"); BLUEPRINT standards; section 1 claim contract.
- A JES reviewer of a PINN/KAN paper expects LSTM, GRU or Transformer baselines, and at least one published PINN (Wang et al.) and one KAN model (PIKAN/PGKAN-style) run on the same cells. ### F8. Temperature-laboratory confound: honestly stated, incompletely handled (major)
- Evidence: PREREG 3.3(7), 4 (Test C), 9.8(f), 11 Test C wording; D011 section 2.
- Credit: no temperature claim, Test C descriptive, title never mentions 35 C, lab/stage rows, surface-temperature offsets by lab. This is more careful than the exemplars.
- Gaps: (1) **Test B is aliased too.** All 35 C cells are INT and almost all 45 C cells are HM; held-out conditions at 35 and 45 C sit among training conditions of the same lab. "Untested conditions" in the T1 title is then interpolation within lab, in a design that puts neighbours in other folds on purpose (section 4). (2) The lab-indicator sensitivity row covers A1, A9, A12 **but not A10**, the arm of the key contrast H6; A10, which has no lab term, is disadvantaged exactly where labs differ, and the row cannot show whether the H6 win is a lab artefact. ### F9. Calendar and cyclic terms are collinear inside each cycling cell; "joint law" interpretation is assumption (major, electrochemist)
- Evidence: draft Eq. (2)-(5); PREREG 7 "single state-dependent modulation"; 11a item 4.
- For a cycling cell the throughput rate is nearly constant, so E_n(t) is proportional to t_n and Phi = (a_cal + a_cyc nu) t_n: only the sum is identified from that cell. The split is identified only if the calendar rate of a cycled cell equals the storage rate evaluated at (T, SOC_mid), which ignores the SOC swing, charge-hold time at high SOC, and rest-period differences. The "calendar share of fade" (11a item 4, Fig. 6c) is a model-defined quantity, not a measurement; the counterfactual a_cyc = 0 is not physical when one h(y) couples both terms. A single shared h also forces calendar SEI growth (decelerating) and cycling mechanisms (plating or LAM knees at 1.2 C charge, accelerating) to share one state dependence; the separate-h variant is an appendix row only (D013, (n)).
- The "physics" in the residual is a phenomenological rate law with free KAN-valued coefficients; any F that can be fitted by some parameter set satisfies it. There is no electrochemical content (no SEI diffusion, no plating onset), no EIS, DVA or resistance fade, and capacity fade only. "Physics-informed" will be read narrowly by an electrochemist.
- 35 C calendar twins and Latin-hypercube points in a 5-D hull of 32 training points impose the author's own extrapolated law as pseudo-data where no cells exist; this is self-consistency, not validation (the prereg says so for twins only).

Readers will want mean RMSE and worst case.
