## 0. Recommendation (single)

**Do not change A1, A15, G15 or the cap before the freeze. Put the KAN-side methodological contribution into an identifiability-audited extraction protocol (candidate E below), which changes no scored arm.** The pre-fixed synthetic rule adopted none of the architectural candidates: shape-constrained edges (M) lost in my implementation, library-aware training (L) improved accuracy slightly at m = 32 but missed its false-structure criterion and is not better at m = 8. M's loss is at least partly an initialisation artefact (section 4), so M and L are queued as a pre-specified second round (exploratory appendix or revision), not as pre-freeze changes. The approved wording ("Kolmogorov-Arnold stress functions", "readable Kolmogorov-Arnold stress laws") stays true as it is.

I know this is less than the author asked for ("if possible, include the novelty in KAN"). It is what the evidence supports at this date; section 6 says what would change it.

## 1. What the author is asking for, and where the D021 findings leave us

The KAN today is a spline map from stress to log-rate, used as a black box and then read by a post hoc symbolic step. D021 showed that the post hoc step is the weak link: BIC on the KAN's grid picks a 3-coefficient form on 92 % of truly linear edges (cap 3), the temperature edge has only 3 training levels, and the cap that stops the false structure (2) leaves {zero, linear, log} and makes D016 3.5 unanswerable. A methodological novelty "inside the KAN" would have to attack that: make the edges simpler during training (L), or constrain them by physics (M), or type them (B-candidate b). Each is tested or argued below.

## 2. Candidates

| id | idea |
|---|---|
| (a) M | monotone spline edges by construction for inputs with a physics sign (T +, DoD +, C_ch +; SOC_mid and C_dch free), outer edges increasing; unconstrained KAN as the ablation |
| (b) | physics-typed bases: T entered as the Arrhenius abscissa, power laws on throughput, so the "extracted" law is exact |
| (c) L | library-aware training: add to the parent's loss lambda x the squared distance of every edge (on the extraction grid) from its best {linear, log} fit, so that extraction loses nothing |
| (d) ML | M and L together |
| (e) E (mine) | identifiability-audited extraction: per edge report the number of training levels, the identifiable shape parameters, the BIC margin, the false-structure rate from a synthetic calibration, and label a form "readable" only where the data can identify it; no change to any scored arm |
| (f) (mine) | raise the BIC floor (rms_tol_rel 1 % to about 3 %) and keep cap 3: untested; changes the frozen rule and hash |

## 3. Per candidate: novelty, effect, fairness, cap, cost, tests, title

**Prior work to check (all unverified, from memory).** Monotone KANs: "MonoKAN" (certified monotonic KAN with cubic Hermite splines, 2024-2025 preprint). Shape-constrained additive models: Pya and Wood, SCAM, Statistics and Computing 2015. Monotone networks and lattices: Sill 1997; Gupta et al., JMLR 2016. KAN and its symbolic snapping: Liu et al., arXiv 2404.19756 and KAN 2.0, arXiv 2408.10205. Library-based equation learners: Martius and Lampert, EQL 2016; Brunton et al., SINDy, PNAS 2016; relaxed discrete choice as in DARTS. Identifiability: Raue et al., Bioinformatics 2009 (profile likelihood); stability selection, Meinshausen and Buhlmann 2010. In our target journal the precedent for KAN inside an Arrhenius law is Koenig and Deng, J. Energy Storage 158 (in `notes/PRIOR_ART_SCAN_lead.md`). Whether any of these (or a 2026 battery paper) already does what is proposed is for the novelty auditor.

**(a) M, shape-constrained edges.** Novelty: the method (monotone spline coefficients, monotone KAN) is prior art, MonoKAN and SCAM at least; what remains is the application with physics signs to a joint calendar-plus-cycle law, and the argument below. One real argument for it: cycling temperature is lab-confounded (35 C all INT, 45 C all HM), so a free T edge can absorb a laboratory offset as a non-monotone temperature effect; a sign constraint forbids that. Against: the physics fixes the sign for T and DoD, probably for C_ch (not verified from the dataset paper), not for SOC_mid (the dataset authors report non-monotone behaviour above 80 % SOC) nor C_dch. Effect at g1: uncertain. In the committed check M was far worse (G0 m = 8: E_par 0.169 against 0.121; section 4) but the check's M was under-initialised; a post hoc diagnostic (2 seeds, GAM, G0, m = 32) gives 0.060 and 0.052 for M with a better start, against 0.079 and 0.061 for B, i.e. about 0.014 of range better. So M could help where the sign prior is right and the data are scarce, and could hurt where it is wrong (G3 was meant to test that, and could not, because M was under-trained). K1/K2: no direct effect. Fairness: every law-based arm (A1, A2, A4, A5, A13, A15, G15) would need the same signs, or K2 stops isolating composition; the data-only comparators cannot be given them (LightGBM accepts monotone constraints, the GP does not), so P1 and P2 would then mix the law with a sign prior and the attribution of the gain to the physics law (explanation E1) is muddied. Two checks become tautologies: PC-2 (calendar rate monotone in T) if E(SOC) were constrained, which it must not be. Cap: monotone edges are simpler: false structure at cap 3 was 37.5 % (M) and 29 % (ML) against 94 % (B), still far from 0, so M does not replace the cap. Cost: about 2 days (parametrisation with a reliable initialisation, a certified-monotone test, wiring into all seven arms, a new tuned choice or a fixed one) and A1 is the primary arm: the pilots P4/P5 that support the design used the unconstrained KAN. Title: "Kolmogorov-Arnold stress functions" stays true; a new word ("physics-constrained") would be needed to claim it and the freeze has no sentence for it. Test plan: monotonicity certificate on a dense grid for every fit; G0/G2/G3 recovery with a corrected init and 10 seeds; lab-aliasing synthetic (a laboratory offset on the middle temperature level) as the case where M should win.

**(b) typed bases.** Novelty: none (the calendar term already uses the Arrhenius abscissa; KA-CRNN uses Arrhenius coefficients). Effect: the edge study (D021) shows 1/T and linear-in-T differ by 0.5 % of range on the 23-45 C hull, below the 1 % floor, so exactness buys nothing numerically; it changes the library and its hash and the seven-family unit test. What it does give, at no cost, is an activation-energy readout (the slope in the Arrhenius abscissa) at report time, with the 3-level caveat. Not a contribution; do as a readout only.

**(c) L, library-aware training.** Novelty: modest. The idea is quantisation-aware training transplanted to symbolic libraries; EQL, SINDy and KAN's own snapping are the nearest. What may be new is applying a projection-residual penalty on the very grid that the frozen BIC rule reads, with one library for a KAN and its matched GAM. Effect (committed check, 6 seeds, section 4): at m = 32 the parent's error is lower by 0.0055 of the truth range (upper bound -0.0035, better in 6 of 6 seeds), the cap-3 extracted law of L is better than the cap-2 extracted law of B by 0.0055 on G0 and G3 and by 0.0088 on G3 alone (the U-shaped C_ch edge that cap 2 cannot hold), but false structure at cap 3 only falls from 94 % to 79 % (criterion: 25 %), and at m = 8 L is slightly worse than B (+0.003 to +0.008). So L helps where there is data, does nothing for the scarce cell, and does not fix the BIC problem. K1/K2: applied to the parents of A15 and G15 only and with the same lambda and code path, K2 still isolates composition; it narrows the A15-minus-G15 gap on additive truth from 0.0250 to 0.0196. A1 would be untouched, so K1 would compare an L-trained closed form with a plain A1; that favours K1 slightly and must be disclosed. Cap: L1 failed, so D021 stands; the replacement rule I had specified (cap 3 plus lambda_lib) is not justified by the data. Cost: about 60 lines plus tests (1 day), +15 to 40 % per parent fit (measured 20 s to 23 s KAN and 15 s to 21 s GAM at m = 8), and lambda would be one more number (it replaces the unused sparsity choice for A15/G15). Title: unchanged.

**(d) ML.** Tracks M (E_par at G0, m = 32: 0.149 against 0.152 for M and 0.072 for L) and is worse than L alone in every cell of the check. Not a candidate.

**(e) E, identifiability-audited extraction.** Novelty: moderate, in the analysis of KAN laws rather than the network: KAN papers read symbolic forms off trained edges; D021 shows with ground truth that this over-reads edges (92 % false structure at cap 3, 0 % at cap 2), that the design fixes how many shape parameters an edge can carry (3 temperature levels give 2 contrasts; 137 of 400 random 3-level data sets are fitted exactly by at least three different 3-coefficient forms), and what the cap costs on curved edges (up to 19 % of edge range). A protocol that reports, per edge, levels, identifiable parameters, BIC margin and the calibrated false-structure rate, and withholds the word "readable" where the data cannot support it, is a methodological contribution a JES reviewer can use. Prior work: identifiability and profile-likelihood analysis (Raue et al.), stability selection; neither is for KAN edges to my knowledge (unverified). Effect on scored results: none (it adds a table and a figure panel to the Fig. 5 / Table 2 material and reuses `runs/D021_cap_synth`). Fairness: A15 and G15 get the same audit. Cost: 0.5 to 1 day, no change to arms or to the hash. Test plan: the audit reproduces the D021 false-structure rate on the same synthetic seeds; a unit test that an edge with L levels never gets a family with more than L - 1 coefficients flagged "identifiable"; per-edge label stability across the five seeds and the five folds (already planned in 11a item 6) is added as the stability-selection column. Title: "readable Kolmogorov-Arnold stress laws" is then supported rather than assumed, and the K+ sentence's "readable" can cite the audit.

**(f) larger BIC floor with cap 3.** Untested; touches the frozen rule and the library hash; no novelty. Mentioned because it is the cheapest route to cap 3 if D016 3.5 must be answered (together with a calendar-only cap, D021 alternative 3).

## 5. Why this and why not each alternative

1. **Why not M now.** Primary arm change two steps before the freeze, an unmeasured optimiser sensitivity, sign validity partly unverified, fairness to the data-only comparators not achievable, PC-2 must be protected, and the method is prior art (MonoKAN, SCAM). If the diagnostic's 0.014 survives a proper round it is a real g1 candidate.
2. **Why not L now.** All accuracy criteria passed, but the gain is 0.0055 of range at m = 32 and nothing at m = 8, and it does not fix the problem it was designed for (false structure 79 %). It is also a change to the K-chain parents that favours K1 slightly.
3. **Why not (b).** No numerical effect on this hull; changes the hash.
4. **Why not ML.** No better than M, and worse than L alone in every cell.
5. **Why E.** It is the only candidate that adds a defensible methodological result without touching a scored arm, and it uses evidence already on disk. Its weakness is that it is a protocol result, not an architecture; I do not claim more.

## 6. Is this the best result possible? What are we lacking? What would a reviewer reject?

Room for improvement: yes. (i) M with a robust parametrisation (I-splines or Hermite as in MonoKAN, with a start that does not depend on a magic constant) and a lab-aliasing synthetic where it should win; (ii) L with lambda in {1e3, 1e4} and a larger floor (f); (iii) both on Samsung training sides with inner CV only. Each synthetic round is about one hour on two processes; the Samsung-side step needs the lead.
Lacking: any real-data evidence; a verified physics sign for C_ch; the novelty audit of every name above; a tuned lambda; refit results (the check stops before the end-to-end refit); more than six seeds.
A reviewer would reject: "monotone KAN is MonoKAN" for (a); "this is EQL/KAN snapping with a penalty" for (c); "synthetic only, 0.5 % of range" for (c); "the protocol is bookkeeping, not a method" for (e) unless the calibration table and the identifiable-parameter rule are shown clearly and the label discipline changes what the paper claims.
