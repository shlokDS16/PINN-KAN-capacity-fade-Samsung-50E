## FATAL

### F1. The calibrated quantity is not the error rate of the label it licenses (unit mismatch, dependence, effective n)
- **Where.** `extraction_audit.py:142-166, 215`; `extraction_calibration.py:36, 47-53, 110, 138`; `calibration_rule.md` sections 2-3.
- **Mismatch of units.** Condition (iii) bounds the *per-law* rate "one extracted law chooses F when the truth is another family". The label "identified: F" is awarded on an *aggregate of 25 laws* (5 folds x 5 seeds, condition ii: at least 20 of 25 agree). The calibration contains one fit per (fold, dataset); it has no seeds and never applies the 25-law rule to anything. The false-label rate of the actual rule is never estimated. With the laws sharing one truth and 60 % of their training conditions (measured: mean pairwise Jaccard of the five m = 32 training sets = 0.60), the aggregate error is governed by systematic bias, not by a per-law rate, so the per-law number can be either too high or too low. Nothing says which.
- **Effective sample size is about 4 per cell, not 20.** `truth_families(block, j)` has no fold argument (`extraction_calibration.py:47-53`), so dataset j has the same truth family, sign and amplitude in all 5 folds; the parent fit seed is j (`:138`), so the initialisation is also the same. The "20 synthetic edges per (input, m, arm, truth family)" are 4 distinct truths (2 with + sign, 2 with - sign) x 5 correlated replicates. Seed and truth assignment are perfectly confounded. The Clopper-Pearson intervals of `calibration_tables` treat 20 or 40 as independent draws.
- **Quantisation makes the threshold a coin flip.** One failed truth replicate costs 5 of 40 pooled edges = 0.125 > 0.10 (abstain); a replicate that fails in 4 of 5 folds costs 0.10 and passes. The point estimate, not an upper bound, is compared with 0.10 (`:215`). Clopper-Pearson 95 % upper bound of 4 of 40 is 0.237 (computed); of 0 of 40 is 0.088. So "calibrated at <= 0.10" is compatible with a true rate of about 0.24.
- **Failure scenario.** A cell is labelled "identified: log" for c_dch because the 4 synthetic truths happened to be separable; the real law gets the same label in 22 of 25 laws; on a fresh set of 4 truths the same cell would give 0.20. The paper's table says "calibrated false-structure rate <= 0.10" and a reviewer who re-derives n_eff finds 4.
- **Result that forces rejection of the claim "calibrated".** Any held-out synthetic replication in which the full 25-law label rule returns a wrong "identified: F" in more than 10 % of datasets for a cell the table marked as passing; or a re-run with different truth-to-j assignment that flips a pass to a fail in more than one of the inputs.

### F2. No pre-set statement of what a useful audit is; "always abstain" satisfies every pre-registered condition, and the failure path is already written as a result
- **Where.** `03_judge.md` section 7 item 5 ("calibration shows that no input can reach 'identified' at g4 ... the rule stays; the paper reports it, and the contribution becomes the negative audit finding"); `extraction_audit.json` (only upper bounds on error: `max_false_family_rate`, `max_sign_error_rate`; no lower bound on recall or on the share of inputs named); PREREG 7.3 item 6 (c); 17.4 (build-or-strike).
- **Why empty.** Every threshold limits false positives. Nothing limits false abstention. The label set `{pruned, identified, sign, composed, ambiguous}` with abstention as the default (`label_input`, `:236`) is perfectly calibrated by a rule that labels everything "ambiguous". The pre-registration contains no minimum number of inputs that must be nameable, no minimum true-label recall in the calibration, and no statement of what outcome would count as the audit failing. Section 7 item 5 turns the failing outcome into a "negative audit finding", so no calibration result can falsify the contribution. A contribution that cannot fail is reporting.
- **Prediction from code and geometry (not a result).** At m = 32 the five fold training sets have 3 temperature levels and 20-23 (soc_mid), 10-11 (dod), 9-10 (c_ch), 17-20 (c_dch) levels (computed from `subsets.json`). On the temperature hull (296-318 K) the best straight line fits log(T) with an RMS residual of 0.27 % of the range (computed), far below the extraction tolerance `RMS_TOL_REL = 0.01` (`symbolic.py:36`), so linear and log tie and the tie is broken by list order (see M4). T therefore cannot reach "identified" for either family and ends at "sign identified" at best. For c_ch, dod and c_dch the linear-versus-log residuals are 5.5 %, 7.4 % and 7.1 % of range (computed), so they separate by shape alone. The audit will therefore name roughly three inputs and abstain on the one input (T) that the paper's physics story needs. That is a plausible honest result, but nothing pre-registered says whether it counts as success.
- **Failure scenario.** The calibration returns no passing cell for A15 h = 2 (composed inputs, M5) and two for G15; the paper then prints "G15 has more identified inputs than A15" as if it were a finding about the laws (it is an artefact of the label set, M5).
- **Result that forces rejection of the claim "contribution".** Calibration in which fewer than a pre-stated number of (input, arm) cells reach "identified" at m = 32, with the number fixed before the run; the current files fix none.

## MAJOR

### M1. Pooling over the "other truth" classes dilutes the 0.10 bound; point estimate; no multiplicity
- `extraction_audit.py:157-161`: the false-family rate of F is `sum over T != F of count(T -> F) / sum of n(T)`. For F = linear the denominator pools truth = zero (separable at amplitude 0.5, near-zero errors) with truth = log (the confusable class). The bound is then effectively 0.20 against the class that matters: 4 of 20 log truths read as linear, 0 of 20 zero truths read as linear, rate 4/40 = 0.10, label awarded. Clopper-Pearson for 4 of 20 is [0.057, 0.437] (computed). The judge's text (2.2a item 3 (iii)) says "the calibrated rate of choosing that family when the truth is another eligible family", which does not say "pooled"; the code chooses the weaker reading.
- Three inputs x three families x three arms x two m give 54+ cells, each tested at a point estimate; the winner's curse is not addressed; the table is read cell by cell.

### M2. Closed-world calibration; "pruned" and "sign identified" are not calibrated on the cases that matter
- Truth families are the same three the extractor can output (`{zero, linear, log}`), with one amplitude (A = 0.5 for every non-zero input, `synth.py:67`). The curved block (quadratic, hinge) is report-only by rule (`calibration_rule.md` section 2) and never enters a label. The false-family rate for "identified: linear" given a quadratic, hinge, power or Arrhenius truth is therefore not bounded, yet the real stress dependences that the paper otherwise discusses (the dataset authors' non-monotone SOC dependence, PREREG 7.3 item 1 Limitations sentence; A10's power laws) are outside the library. The approximation flag (`:123-127`) is the only guard; by rule it "changes neither the cap nor any label", so a label "identified: linear" and a flag "the cap-2 form is an approximation of a curved learned effect" can be printed on the same input.
- `extraction_audit.py:152`: the sign-error rate is computed only over non-zero truths. The label "sign identified" is therefore unconditional on null inputs. A truly null input that the extractor reads as a consistent spurious linear term (stable across folds, since the folds share 60 % of the conditions) fails (iii), then passes the sign path (`:232`) because its sign error is measured on other inputs' truths. Failure scenario: c_dch has no effect; aliasing with c_ch (design correlation up to |0.32| at m = 8, `subsets.json`) gives a consistent spurious slope; output "sign identified" for a null input.
- "pruned" (`:210-212`) needs only 80 % zero laws and no calibration at all. Pruning is relative: `tol = RMS_TOL_REL * max(ranges)` over the whole layer (`symbolic.py:374-375`), so an input is pruned when its edge is under 1 % of the strongest edge, not when it is absent. The calibration gives all non-zero inputs the same amplitude, so the false-zero rate for a real but weak effect (the realistic case next to Arrhenius temperature) is never estimated. "Pruned" will be read as "no effect".

### M3. "Design-matched" is true for the stress vectors only
- Matched: the 32 or 8 training cycling identifiers (from `subsets.json`) and the 19 storage identifiers (`extraction_calibration.py:92-94`). Not matched (all fixed round numbers, `synth.py:6-8,20-21,132`): effect amplitude (one value), noise (0.5 pp), per-cell offset (iid, sd 0.15), median fade (20 pp), calendar-to-cycle ratio (`c_cal = c_cyc - 2`), check-up cadence (one hard-coded schedule read from a figure; the real cells differ in length and end of test), no cell attrition, no heteroscedasticity.
- Laboratory aliasing is absent. PREREG 3.3 item 7: all 35 C cycling cells were run by one laboratory, 45 C by two others, and temperature is aliased with laboratory. Temperature has exactly 3 levels. The sign and level of the T effect are, by design, partly indistinguishable from laboratory offsets; the synthetic offsets are iid, so the calibration reports a T sign error rate that cannot occur in the real design. "Sign identified" for T is the label most likely to be wrong and the least calibrated.
- The calibration uses `default_hp` (the middle value of each categorical: grid 5, sparsity 1e-3, and so on) for A15 and G15 (`extraction_calibration.py:138`), while the real A15 and G15 are tuned by inner CV over a six- and four-dimensional space that includes `sparsity` in {0, 1e-4, 1e-3, 1e-2} and `grid` (`arms.py:75-79`). Sparsity directly sets the pruning behaviour that the label rule reads. The cell that gets looked up (`A15h1`, `A15h2`, `G15`) is therefore not the pipeline that produced the real laws.
- Signal-to-noise direction: the synthetic effect range is 1.0 in log-rate against a noise that is about 2.5 % of the final fade. If real scatter is larger, the real false rates exceed the calibrated ones, which is the optimistic direction for the label. There is no sensitivity grid over amplitude, noise or offset; a single operating point is reported as "the" rate.

### M4. The family choice is decided by a floor and a tie-break, so the stability condition and the calibration partly re-measure a deterministic geometric rule
- `symbolic.py:207-223`: BIC is evaluated on the KAN's edge function on a 101-point grid (not on data) with `rss/n` floored at `tol^2` where `tol = 0.01 * max range` of the layer. When two families fit within the floor, their BICs are exactly equal (equal number of coefficients for linear and log) and `key = (round(bic, 9), N_COEFS, order)` falls through to list order, where linear precedes log (`FAMILIES`, `:37`). The choice then depends on nothing in the data.
- Consequences: (a) condition (ii) (same family in at least 80 % of laws) is satisfied trivially for any edge that both families fit within 1 % of the layer range, which is the case for T (residual 0.27 % of range, computed); stability is not evidence of identification. (b) The false-family rate for "identified: log" is structurally near zero (log can win only when strictly better) while that for "identified: linear" is structurally high on short hulls; the two rates are not exchangeable and a single threshold on both is not a single standard. (c) Whether linear can be told from log reduces, for a well-trained KAN, to the hull ratio hi/lo against a hard-coded 1 %: a quantity computable from the design in one line, with no fit. A reviewer will ask why 600 pipeline fits are needed.
- The "BIC margin" printed in the per-edge table is a margin on an interpolated spline grid with a tolerance floor, and it is not used in any label (`label_input` only stores its median). The column looks like statistical evidence and is not.

### M5. A15 and G15 are not treated identically in what the labels can express
- G15 has h = 1 and no outer edge, so `additive` is always True (`extraction_audit.py:109` with `h == 1`) and "composed" can never occur; A15 h = 2 can be "composed" when the input feeds both hidden nodes, which is redundancy of the architecture (two linear edges sum to one linear effect) and not lack of identification by the data. The side-by-side sentence "of the 5 inputs, the design identified the form of j" will count A15 lower for a structural reason.
- The additivity gate (iv) is the only protection against interaction. It exists for A15 h = 2 only; G15 has none. The `noadd` calibration block runs `A15h2` only and is report-only (`extraction_calibration.py:66, 213-219`): the calibration never measures how often G15 (or A15 h = 1) names a family for an input whose true effect interacts with another input. The project's premise (G2 truths, "additive GAMs cannot represent it") is a non-additive reality, so G15 will name more inputs than A15 exactly where the GAM is mis-specified.
- Mixed-variant handling is asymmetric: when A15 folds were tuned to different h, `_worst_cell` (`:174-180`) takes the largest false-family rate and the smallest n over variants present, even when a variant appears in one law of 25; G15 always gets its single cell.
- Calibration defaults and search spaces differ between arms (A15 grid {2,...,12}, G15 grid {3,...,30}, `arms.py:76-79`), so "one code path" (test `test_a15_and_g15_go_through_one_code_path...`) is true for `law_record` only, not for the objects audited.

### M6. Code bug: A15 with h = 1 is declared additive whatever its outer edge is
- `extraction_audit.py:109`: `additive = bool(h == 1 or all(f in ("linear","zero") for f in outer_fams))`. For A15 the KAN is [5, h, 1]; with h = 1 there is still an outer edge, `outer_fams` has one entry, and a log outer edge makes the law `log(sum_i f_i(x_i))`, non-additive in every input. The code (and the judge's wording, read literally) skips the test for h = 1. Only the G15 assertion in the unit test checks `outer_families == []`; nothing asserts it for A15 h = 1. The calibration inherits the same behaviour, and `calibration_tables` ignores the `additive` field entirely.
- Failure scenario: A15 h = 1 fits an outer log edge; every first-layer edge is linear; the label is "identified: linear" for every input of a law whose effect on the output is concave in each.

### M7. The guard is a discipline, not a control (authenticity, pins, scope)
- `extraction_audit.py:336-354`: authenticity rests on (a) a hash record written next to the file it describes, (b) `written_unix` stored inside that unsigned record, and (c) file mtimes. The repository's own test helper `_calibration` (`test_extraction_calibration.py:129-140`) fabricates a table, rewrites `written_unix` and `os.utime` into the past, and the guard accepts it. No check of `rule_sha256`, `commit`, `n_records`, `plan` or `cells_below_minimum_n` is made against the FROZEN commit or the config pins. `calibration.json` is not in `PIN_FILES` (`guard.py:24`) and the stats step has no `calibration_sha256` argument, unlike item (f) (`item_f_sha256`). The thresholds pin is optional (`stats_step.py:528, 558`, default None); without it the thresholds file and the record can be edited together.
- `mtime` ordering is the whole "before any outer score" claim. A copy, an unzip or a checkout resets mtimes. The check also applies only to files named `condition_scores*.json`, which `run.py:524` writes last, after `predictions.csv` (`rec.add_preds`, `run.py:467`); the calibration script uses a different definition (`SCORED.jsonl`, `extraction_calibration.py:164`). Two definitions of "outer score exist" in one pipeline.
- `edge_level_wording` is "the only function that produces" edge wording only by comment: `audit_arm`, `label_law` and `results_sentence` are public and take a calibration dict as an argument. The banned-claims list (judge section 4) is enforced by no linter on `paper/draft/`.
- The calendar KAN is extracted by the same pipeline (`symbolic.py:432-435`) and its edge families are never audited or guarded; 11a items 1 and 2 and any "calendar law" sentence are outside the guard.
- Scope of the claim in the paper must therefore be "ordering by file time and operator discipline", not "enforced".

### M8. The level-based part of the rule is inert on the Samsung design, so the claimed difference from Sovrano et al. has no operational content here
- Condition (i) is `L_e >= 3` and "identifiable parameters = min(1, L_e - 1)" (`extraction_audit.py:60-65`). On the real folds every input has at least 3 levels (T exactly 3; the others 9 to 23 at m = 32; 4 to 8 at m = 8; computed from `subsets.json`). So (i) never binds, the "identifiable parameters" column is 1 in every row (a constant), and L_e = 2 never occurs. The level-based, Raue-style element that the novelty note offers as the distinction from Sovrano et al. ("no calibrated rate and no level-based rule", PREREG 7.3 (f)) is not exercised by the data it is applied to. What remains is the calibrated rate (F1, M1-M4) and a stability vote.
- Structural identifiability in the Raue sense (a profile-likelihood or rank argument on the parameters) is not performed; "levels minus one" for a one-slope family is a counting rule. A reviewer familiar with identifiability analysis will call the table's column headings borrowed.

## What a sceptical JES reviewer writes (KAN-side contribution only)

- **Summary.** The authors add an "identifiability audit" to closed-form extraction from a KAN and from a matched GAM: a table of training levels, a BIC margin and a stability vote, with labels licensed by false-family rates measured on synthetic truths over the same training conditions, and abstention otherwise.
- **Strengths.** Honest about cap and library; pre-specified thresholds; a published false-structure counterexample (37 of 40 at cap 3) motivating the cap; abstention as default; matched A15/G15 treatment in the reporting.
- **Weaknesses.** (1) Calibrated against a generator that contains the answer (library = truth family), with one amplitude and iid noise; the real design has laboratory-aliased temperature. (2) The rate that is calibrated is not the rate of the label. (3) No baseline: the paper does not show that the audit changes any conclusion relative to "stable across folds and seeds" alone or "report the closed form with its BIC margin". (4) No independent ground truth: no benchmark with known equations; no real data case where the audit was right and a naive reading was wrong. (5) Reads as simulation-based calibration of a selection rule (a standard practice) applied to one model class on one cell type; the novelty is the application. (6) Thresholds (0.8, 0.10, 20 edges) are asserted, not justified. (7) The audit is not used downstream except in a descriptive 11a item.
- **Questions.** What fraction of synthetic datasets receive a wrong "identified" label under the full rule? What happens when the truth is a power law or has an interaction? Why 20 edges and why pool over folds that share 60 % of their conditions? Does T ever get a family name?
- **Score and confidence.** As a stand-alone method contribution: reject or borderline (4/10, confidence 3/5). As a clearly scoped reporting protocol inside a battery paper: acceptable if the claims are narrowed to "reported with a synthetic false-structure table".
- **The one experiment whose absence causes rejection.** The end-to-end operating characteristic of the full label rule (25-law aggregate) on held-out synthetic datasets, including out-of-library and non-additive truths, against the stability-only baseline.

## Single change that would most raise its value without delaying the freeze

Calibrate the label, not the edge: in `extraction_calibration.py` and `calibration_rule.md`, replace the per-law confusion table by the full label rule applied to 5 folds x 5 seeds on each synthetic dataset, report the confusion of the printed label against the truth (rows: zero, linear, log, quadratic, hinge, non-additive; columns: pruned, identified F, sign, composed, ambiguous), and state in the same file, before the run, how many (input, arm) cells must reach "identified" for the audit to count as useful. The calibration runs after FROZEN from the frozen commit, so this is a pre-freeze edit of the script and the rule file only.
