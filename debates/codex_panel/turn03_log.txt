## 2. Where the PINN does real work, stated plainly, and how it is tested

**The honest premise (ruling).** For a scalar ageing ODE with a fully trusted rate law, a soft PINN
residual cannot enforce anything that exact integration (hard ODE) does not enforce more exactly; the
proposer, the opponent and Codex all say so, and I agree. A PINN differs from a hard ODE only where the
law is **not** fully trusted. Real cells are such a case. So the PINN is the headline formulation, and the
paper tests, with a pre-stated outcome, whether its soft coupling helps. This is not a straw man: the hard
ODE with the same KAN law is a full-strength competitor.

**Architecture (Wang-2024 F/G form, with G made physical and stress-conditioned).**
- **Solution network F:** y_hat = F_psi(t_tilde, s, z_c), retention loss y = 1 - Q/Q0,c, with
  t_tilde = time since the cell's first check-up (days, global scaling only, never per-cell life
  fraction: this is the PIKAN defect, PIKAN.md 4), s = stress vector, z_c = low-dimensional per-cell
  latent. Hard initial condition y_hat(0) = 0 by construction (y_hat = t_tilde * g(.)). F is a small MLP
  (the KAN's role is the stress map, so the two parts stay separable in ablation; F = KAN is one ablation
  row).
- **Dynamics G (the physics):** dy/dt = r_cal + r_cyc * nu, where nu = equivalent full cycles per day
  (from the cycling log; zero for storage cells),
  r_cal = exp(a(SOC) - E(SOC) (1/T - 1/T_ref)/R) * h_cal(y),
  r_cyc = exp(f(T, SOC_window, DoD, C_ch, C_dch) + delta_c) * h_cyc(y).
  a(.), E(.) (prefactor and activation energy as functions of storage SOC) and f(.) are the KAN stress
  functions (section 3). h_cal, h_cyc are state factors chosen by a small backbone tournament on training
  cells only (self-limiting 1/(y + y0); constant; accelerating exp(gamma y)), ties to the simpler.
  Terms are named **time-driven** and **throughput-driven**, never SEI or plating (opponent 4.2 accepted).
- **Residual:** R = dF/dt_tilde - [r_cal + r_cyc nu] by autograd. Loss = data term at check-ups +
  lambda_res * mean R^2 over collocation points + prior on z_c/delta_c. lambda_res by inner grouped CV on
  training conditions (log grid of 5; an edge optimum extends the grid).
- **Collocation points (fixed, label-free, not self-referential; opponent 1.2 accepted):**
  (a) along every training cell's time axis from the end of its observed record to a fixed horizon
  t_max set from the design's longest test duration (known before data); (b) at **unobserved stress
  points**: a fixed Latin-hypercube sample inside the convex hull of the training design;
  (c) at the **calendar twins** of cycling conditions: (T, SOC) with nu = 0, including T = 35 C, where the
  Samsung matrix has cycling cells but no storage cells (counted from `experiments_meta.csv`: storage at
  10/23/45 C, cycling at 23/35/45 C). Points are resampled from these fixed sets each step.

**What the PINN does that data and a hard ODE cannot.**
1. **Soft coupling under misspecification.** F is a flexible surface over (t, s) shared by all cells;
   where data exist and the law is wrong, F may depart from the law (paid for by the residual); where no
   data exist (beyond the window, at 35 C storage, between conditions) F is held to the law. A hard ODE
   has no such trade-off: its forecast is the law. A data-only F has no law outside the data.
2. **A residual map over stress space.** The trained residual |R| on a stress grid shows where the
   learned law and the data disagree (for example a storage-cycling superposition failure at high
   temperature and high SOC). A hard ODE has no residual; this diagnostic exists only in the PINN and is
   one figure (section 6, Fig. 7).
3. **Physics-regularised test-time inversion.** For an unseen cell, z_c is fitted to its window points
   plus the residual over the forecast horizon (causal: no label after the window).

**Fair hard-ODE vs soft-PINN comparison (preregistered).** Same G, same KAN, same backbone, same per-cell
offset, same inner-CV tuning budget, same data; hard ODE = vectorised RK4 through G with delta_c MAP from
the window. Endpoint: the primary endpoint. Outcomes, stated now:
- **(A) PINN better** (paired, margin as in section 6): the soft residual is a contribution; the
  headline stands as titled.
- **(B) tie:** the paper says plainly that the residual is equivalent to exact integration for
  forecasting at this design; the PINN remains the formulation because it is the only one giving the
  residual map and solver-free training; the claim rests on the calendar-cycle KAN law and on contrast (i)
  of section 1. Title 3.
- **(C) hard ODE better:** using the PINN as headline would knowingly present the weaker tool. The lead
  must then **put the choice to Shlok**: (C1, judge's recommendation) headline becomes the hard-constrained
  physics-informed KAN, title 2 with "physics-informed Kolmogorov-Arnold network", the PINN reported as
  the soft variant; or (C2) keep the PINN headline with the hard ODE reported as stronger in the ablation
  (a weaker paper, but the brief's wording kept).
The same contrast is run on synthetic data with a misspecified generator (section 6, G1), where theory
predicts (A) is most likely; a PINN that does not beat the hard ODE even there is evidence for (B)/(C).

## 3. Where the KAN does real work, and the fair GAM test

**Placement.** The KAN is the stress-to-log-rate map, nowhere else:
- calendar: KAN [1, 2] from storage SOC to (a, E); this is univariate and therefore a spline GAM in KAN
  basis; I rule that **no superiority is claimed for this part** (opponent 2.1 accepted). Its value is
  the shape of the SOC dependence of prefactor and activation energy, read from the edges;
- cycling: KAN [5, h, 1] over (T, SOC window, DoD, C_ch, C_dch), **with a hidden layer**, h in {2, 3, 4}
  by inner CV. This is where a KAN differs from a GAM: it can represent interactions (for example a
  charge-rate effect that changes with temperature or with SOC window) without the interaction set being
  written down. Identifiability is judged at **function level** (response surfaces and partial
  dependences), never at edge level, because hidden-layer edges have permutation and scale symmetries.
- B-spline KAN (cubic, grid 3 to 6 by inner CV, sparsity L1 + entropy weights by inner CV). Temperature
  enters G through the Arrhenius form; Test C (35 C) is inside the 23-45 C grid, so the B-spline
  extrapolation weakness (opponent 2.2) does not bite there; stress extrapolation outside the hull is not
  claimed.

**Fair contrast (Codex 4 and opponent 2.2 accepted in full).** Same G, same splits, inputs and
training-only scaling, equal TPE budget, comparable effective degrees of freedom (reported):
additive-spline GAM (= KAN [5,1]); GAM with prespecified pairwise interactions (tensor-product splines
on T x C_ch, T x SOC, DoD x SOC); degree-2 log-polynomial with all cross terms; parameter-matched MLP;
parametric Arrhenius/power-law factors. Symbolic rounding (proposal 3.8) is applied to the GAM and
polynomial as well as to the KAN (opponent 2.3), and moves to the Appendix.

**Pre-stated KAN outcomes.**
- KAN better than every contrast on the primary endpoint (paired, Holm) and on synthetic interaction
  recovery (G2): KAN superiority is claimed.
- **Tie** (within the margin) with the best GAM: the claim made instead is "KAN stress functions reach
  the accuracy of the best prespecified-interaction GAM without specifying the interactions, and their
  response curves agree with it"; no superiority wording; the title keeps "Kolmogorov-Arnold" because it
  names the model used.
- KAN **worse** than a GAM by the margin: using it would make the paper wrong; KAN leaves the headline,
  and the lead puts the retitle to Shlok.

## 6. Experiments, endpoints, tie plan, reduced identifiability

**Task (identical for every model).** Inputs: s_c, Q0,c (first check-up), check-ups inside the window.
Output: retention at every later check-up. SOH = Q/Q0,c of the cell's own first check-up.
**Primary window:** the first k check-ups after the initial one, k fixed in `runs/prereg/` from the
cadence alone (after P0, before any model sees a test label). Secondary: k-1 and k+2.
**Tests.**
- **Test B (primary):** grouped folds over the 40 cycling conditions (5 folds of 8, stratified by
  temperature); all storage cells are in training (they are a different experiment; no test cycling cell
  enters training or tuning).
- **Test C (secondary):** all 35 C cycling conditions held out (13 conditions counted from the metadata);
  train on 23/45 C cycling plus all storage. This is a physical interpolation test in temperature, not a
  protocol comparison; no ratio between Tests B and C is reported (retired topic).
- **S2 storage transfer (secondary, physical check):** grouped folds over the 19 storage conditions;
  forecast held-out storage cells from their window.
- ISU and SNL: Test B analogue only (held-out conditions).
**Primary endpoint:** cell-level retention RMSE (percentage points) over the forecast set, averaged within
condition then over conditions, Test B, primary window, 5 seeds, frozen configuration.
**Secondary:** error at fixed horizons after the window (CONTRACT 6); RUL error in days where the cell
reaches 80 % inside the record; MAE, MAPE, R^2 with correct argument order; Test C; S2; ISU EOL MAPE
for comparison with the ISU authors; wall clock and parameter count.
**Arms (9, each with its own TPE budget, same for all learned arms):** PINN-KAN (main); hard-ODE-KAN;
PINN with parametric stress factors (PiDDM-style: fixed law, parameters by a small MLP of s); PINN with
additive GAM; PINN with interaction GAM; PINN with interaction polynomial; PINN with free G (stress
inputs); data-only F (lambda = 0); semi-empirical calendar + cycle model (parametric, least squares);
plus data-only GP-ARD and LightGBM on (s, window summary, t). LSTM/Transformer, PiDDM reimplementation
from its paper, BatLiNet/FlowBatt: dropped (discussed, no numbers).
**Tuning:** inner grouped CV by condition inside the training folds only; 20 TPE trials x 3 inner folds
per arm per outer fold, unless P2 shows fits under 20 s (then 40). Test folds touched once per frozen
configuration.
**Statistics:** paired Wilcoxon on condition-level differences, cluster bootstrap (10,000) by condition
for mean differences, Holm over the three primary contrasts; effect sizes always shown.
**Margin (fixed by rule now, value computed before confirmatory runs):** the non-inferiority / superiority
margin is the median, over training conditions, of the leave-one-replicate-out RMSE of the condition-mean
trajectory (replicate floor), computed by inner CV inside training folds only.
**Tie plan (stated now).** If the PINN-KAN beats (i) and (ii) but not (iii) the data-only learners: the
paper claims parity in accuracy plus physical consistency (S2 storage transfer, Test C, residual map) and
uses title 3; this is a JES applied-modelling paper with a weaker results section, and the lead tells
Shlok before writing. If it fails (i) (the residual does not help) or (ii) (KAN does not help): the
corresponding part is reported as unnecessary and section 2 outcome C or section 3 "worse" logic applies,
with the choice put to Shlok.

**Reduced identifiability programme (one figure + Appendix; not circular).**
- Design: the real Samsung design (conditions, replicates, cadence from P0); the ISU design in tonight's
  pilot.
- Generators, written before any real-data fit: **G0** well-specified (sanity only, never cited as
  evidence); **G1** misspecified time law (storage loss proportional to t^z with z depending on T, and a
  logistic knee in throughput whose onset shifts with stress; neither is in the model's state form);
  **G2** non-additive stress law (a charge-rate hinge whose threshold shifts with temperature, plus a
  DoD x SOC product term). Optional G3: storage and cycle terms not additive (cycling scales the storage
  rate), to test whether the PINN residual map flags the superposition failure.
- Truth: functional forms and coefficients from a grid fixed in normalised units in `runs/prereg/`;
  only the **overall fade level** at the last check-up and the **noise level** are matched to summary
  statistics of the training cells (median fade and replicate spread), never to fitted model parameters.
- Metrics: nRMSE of the recovered log-rate surface on a fixed 500-point Latin hypercube in the design
  hull, divided by the true log-rate range; Kendall tau of variance-based stress importance with truth;
  forecast RMSE on held-out conditions.
- **Thresholds (fixed here):** pass nRMSE <= 0.15 on G1 and G2; fail > 0.30; in between "partial".
  Structured (any structured G) vs free G: structured median nRMSE <= 0.67 x free-G median and paired
  Wilcoxon p < 0.05 over 20 draws. KAN vs additive GAM on G2: KAN lower nRMSE, paired p < 0.05; on G0
  the KAN must not exceed the additive GAM's nRMSE by more than 10 % (no penalty for flexibility).
  Interval widths are reported as a diagnostic only; no coverage or calibration claim (retired topic).
- Size: 3 generators x 20 draws x 4 models (PINN-KAN, hard-ODE-KAN, additive GAM, free G) = 240 fits.

**Figure/table plan (forms to `debates/D-figures.md` against FIGURE_FORMS.md, CONTRACT 6a):** 1 method
schematic (F/G, KAN stress functions, storage + cycle arms, collocation sets); 2 design map (59
conditions, storage vs cycling, replicate fade); 3 forecast overlays at held-out conditions incl. 35 C;
4 forest plot of paired differences (primary endpoint) for all arms; 5 learned stress functions (storage
SOC prefactor and activation energy; cyclic partial dependences) with seed/fold envelopes; 6 storage
transfer and storage share of cycled cells' fade; 7 PINN residual map over stress space; 8 synthetic
recovery (G1, G2): structured vs GAM vs free G; Table 1 main results (Samsung B, C, S2; ISU; SNL);
Table 2 ablations. Replication detail, sensitivity rows and symbolic rounding go to the Appendix (JES
supplementary policy).

## 1. Claim contract

- **Claim (JA1, unchanged).** On held-out Samsung 50E cycling conditions (Test B, primary window) the PINN-KAN has lower
  condition-level retention RMSE than (i) the same network without the physics residual (lambda = 0), (ii) the same PINN with the
  best alternative stress map, (iii) the best tuned data-only learner.
- **Mechanism.** (1) The soft residual holds a shared surface F to a time-driven plus throughput-driven law where there are no
  data (beyond the window, between conditions, 35 C storage twins). (2) A hidden-layer KAN stress map represents interactions of
  (T, SOC window, DoD, C_ch, C_dch) without prespecifying them.
- **Closest methods and exact distinction.** Stroebl et al. (dataset authors; parametric calendar + cycle superposition fitted to
  this data; no learned stress maps; arm A10) and their JES 142, 119552 (states the cycle part is inadequate). PiDDM
  (arXiv 2607.29095; fixed law, parameters from a small MLP of stress; arm A7; no KAN, no joint storage-cycling training, no
  held-out-condition protocol as far as the re-audit read). KA-CRNN (JES 158, 121853; KAN-valued Arrhenius parameters of SOC in a
  neural ODE on thermal-abuse data). PIKAN/PGKAN (SOH estimation, PINN4SOH sets, per-cell life-fraction scaling; not forecasting).
  Zhang JES 182, 125097 **[c]**: positioning unchecked.

1. Capacity per check-up = mean of charge (step 21) and discharge (step 22) capacity from Coulomb counting of the CU, exCU or ET
   file (dataset paper). A check-up without a complete step 21/22 pair is dropped, not its cell.
2. Reference capacity Q0,c: the ET capacity if ET members exist for every analysed cell, otherwise the first CU for every analysed
   cell (one reference for all cells; chosen once, by availability) **[b]**. Retention = Q / Q0,c.
3. Incident handling, from the paper's "Known incidents" list (file names as printed there). Dropped at file level: TP_z25_01_01_ET_T45
   (undefined load), TP_z01_03_11_CU (concatenated files), TP_z17_03_13_exCU and TP_z17_03_17_CU (incorrect SOC_max),

   ET files (the failed capacity cycle was replaced by a repeated measurement; only the charge OCV may be affected). Because the paper
   asks to drop "potentially consecutive files", every later check-up of a cell whose cycling segment shows undefined load or a wrong
   SOC_max (TP_z24_01_02, TP_z25_01_04, TP_z07_03_16, TP_z20_03_16, TP_z17 from segment 18) is dropped from that point. Segments with
   only missing log data ("cycling itself was performed correctly") keep their check-ups and use the designed throughput for that
   interval **[b]**. Mapping of each pattern to (stage, serial) cells follows 3.2.
4. Pauses (chamber downtime 15 and 20 days, lab downtime 4 days, stage 2) are kept on the real calendar axis.
5. Storage cells 01-03 of a test point (66 cells) have intermediate check-ups and form the storage trajectories. Cells 04-06 have
   entry and final tests only: **excluded** from training and scoring in the primary analysis; a sensitivity row adds their
   two-point records. The final test (AT) is excluded from the forecast set (dataset paper: AT capacity at 23 C may deviate because
   of preceding 10 C and 45 C tests); an AT-included row is a sensitivity.
6. No cell is excluded because of its capacity trajectory. Cells whose record ends inside the window are scored in a sensitivity

7. Stage (batch) and lab are recorded covariates. **Confound found in the metadata:** all 39 cells at 35 C were run by lab INT;

## 5. Task, window rule, forecast set

- **Task (identical for every arm).** Inputs: stress vector s (cycling: T, SOC_mid, DoD, C_ch, C_dch, with SOC_max recoverable;
  storage: T, SOC), Q0,c, the window check-ups, throughput rate nu. Output: retention at every later check-up time.
  Time t_tilde = days since the reference check-up, global scaling only (never per-cell life fraction; JA2).
- **Throughput.** Inside the window: logged equivalent full cycles per interval. After the window: the cell's own mean rate over
  the window (causal; no log after the window is used). Oracle-throughput row (logged, all arms) is a sensitivity. **[b]**
  (needs ZYK segments; P0 plans an analytic estimate to be checked on a few cells; if it fails ZYK of all cycling cells is needed).
- **Window rule, in check-up counts, fixed from the schedule alone [R].** Count intermediate check-ups (CU + exCU; ET and AT
  excluded) per cell from the zip member listings (identifiers only, before any capacity is computed). n_med = median over the
  cycling cells; k_cyc = round(n_med / 3) (the first third of the median record); likewise k_sto from the storage cells 01-03.
  Primary window = first k check-ups after the reference; secondary windows k-1 and k+2 (JA6). **[b]** needs all 279 listings (a few
  KB each by range request). Expectation, not a computation: the Samsung figure gives cycling check-ups at weeks 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9,
  13, 17, 21, 25 then every 4 weeks, storage at about weeks 5, 9, 17, 25 then every 8; the two listed cycling cells in P0 have 9 and
  16 intermediate check-ups, the storage cell 7, so k_cyc is expected in 3 to 5 and k_sto near 2. Count-based because paused
  intervals make weeks unreliable.
- **Go/no-go from the schedule (JA8-3).** If fewer than 3 post-window check-ups exist for most cycling cells at the primary
  window, Samsung becomes secondary (storage transfer only) and the fallback of 3.4 applies.
- **Forecast set.** For each scored cell: all CU and exCU after the window that survive 3.3, in time order. A cell is scored if it
  has at least one such check-up; the number of forecast points per cell is reported; a sensitivity row restricts to cells with at
  least 3 forecast points.

## 6. Endpoints

- **Primary (one):** cell-level retention RMSE (percentage points) over the forecast set, averaged within condition (mean over the
  condition's scored cells) and then over conditions; Test B, primary window k_cyc, 5 seeds (per-condition value = mean over seeds
  of that condition's RMSE), frozen configuration.
- **Secondary:** (1) the same under windows k-1 and k+2; (2) error at fixed horizons after the window (first post-window check-up
  and the later ones, horizons defined in check-up counts); (3) RUL error in days for cells whose record crosses 80 % retention
  (n reported; none otherwise); (4) MAE, MAPE, R^2 (correct argument order); (5) Test C, S2, Test A, A10 contrast; (6) ISU EOL MAPE
  and SNL analogue; (7) wall clock and parameter count; (8) share of non-finite or diverging forecasts per arm (a non-finite forecast
  is scored with the persistence error and counted).
- **Metrics where a regression is expected** (stated now so that they are not read as a failure): wall clock and parameter count
  (PINN-KAN is slower than LightGBM and A10); error at the first post-window check-up (a data-only learner can be locally better
  next to the window); RUL error (few cells reach 80 %).

## 7. Arms, tuning, budget

## 9. Statistical analysis

1. **Unit = condition** (Test B 40, Test C 13, S2 19, Test A 40; ISU 63; SNL 23). Condition score s_{a,c} per section 6.
2. **Contrast** d_c = s_comparator,c - s_PINN-KAN,c (positive favours PINN-KAN), paired on condition and on the same seeds.
3. **Margin delta [R].** Per outer fold f, delta_f = median over the fold's training conditions of the leave-one-replicate-out RMSE
   of the other replicates' mean trajectory (the judge's replicate floor; implementation = `replicate_floor` in `p3_headroom.py`,
   time-interpolated), computed by inner CV inside the training conditions only, with the same window and forecast rules. Pooled
   delta = weighted mean of delta_f by test conditions per fold. Test C, S2 use their own training side. No test label enters delta.
4. **Primary tests.** One-sided exact Wilcoxon signed-rank on d_c - delta (superiority by at least delta), Holm over H1a, H1b, H1c at
   family-wise 0.05; a contrast **passes** only if the Holm-adjusted p <= 0.05, the mean of d_c >= delta, and the 10,000-resample
   cluster bootstrap (by condition) 95 % interval of the mean of d_c excludes 0. For H1c under non-inferiority **[a]**: test
   median(d_c) > -delta (shift d_c + delta), same Holm family and same bootstrap check on the lower bound. Two-sided unshifted p
   values are given in the appendix.
5. **Feasibility check of the superiority margin [R] [OPEN-4].** JA6 fixes delta = replicate floor and JA7 P3 calls superiority
   winnable when the best flexible learner exceeds 1.2 x floor. A superiority margin of one floor cannot be reached unless the best
   comparator's inner-CV error exceeds about 2 x floor (the model cannot be below the floor). Before the freeze the ratio r_f =
   (inner-CV error of the best of A11/A12 and the per-cell power law) / floor_f is computed on every training side. If the weighted
   mean of r_f is below 2, the default replacement (chosen now, before any outcome) is: superiority tested at margin 0
   (Holm-adjusted p and bootstrap excluding 0) and non-inferiority kept at delta. The statistician may overrule at sign-off.
6. **H2 (hard vs soft).** d_c = s_hard - s_PINN. A: mean d >= delta with p <= 0.05 (one-sided, shifted) and bootstrap excluding 0.
   B: the 90 % bootstrap interval of mean d inside (-delta, +delta) and both shifted one-sided Wilcoxon tests at 0.05 (equivalence).
   C: mean d <= -delta with p <= 0.05 (reverse direction). Anything else: "unresolved", worded as B, direction reported. Separate
   alpha 0.05, not in the Holm family (JA1: it does not refute the claim).
7. **KAN wording (H3).** Against the best interaction-capable GAM (inner-CV-best of A4, A5): win = H1b passes and G2 passes (section
   13); tie = equivalence within +-delta as in 6; worse = mean d <= -delta, p <= 0.05 reverse direction; otherwise "unresolved".
8. **Effect sizes (always shown):** mean d in pp, relative reduction, matched-pairs rank-biserial r, share of conditions won, per-fold
   means (heterogeneity), the cluster-bootstrap interval. Neighbour correlation between conditions is not modelled (stated).
9. **Other families.** Test C: the three contrasts of H1 with Holm inside Test C (n = 13, low power, stated). H6: one test, A10
   vs A1, shifted at margin 0. S2: non-inferiority of A1 to the best of A3, A9, A10 at delta_S2. Everything else descriptive;
   exploratory results are labelled and unadjusted.
10. **Sensitivity rows (appendix):** lab-and-stage one-hot added to z_c prior for A1, A9, A12 (E3); AT included; cells 04-06 of
    storage included; cells with at least 3 forecast points; forecast-failure rule of 3.3(6); oracle throughput; backbone fixed to
    self-limiting; all window variants.

## 10. Controls

- **Negative control (effect must vanish).** In each Test B outer fold the stress vectors (with their design throughput) are permuted
  among conditions, separately in training and test sets, window data untouched; A1, A9, A4 are re-run with the same budget.
  Required: contrast A9 - A1 has mean d < delta and unshifted two-sided Wilcoxon p > 0.05; A1 does not beat R2 (window-linear
  extrapolation) by more than delta. A violation means leakage through something other than stress: stop and audit (section 12).
- **Positive controls (known effect the pipeline must recover).** (P1) Synthetic G0 (well specified): the structured arms recover the
  log-rate surface with nRMSE <= 0.15 (J pass line) and beat A11/A12 on G0 forecast RMSE (paired Wilcoxon p < 0.05, 20 draws).
  (P2) Real storage cells: the fitted calendar rate increases monotonically with temperature at every SOC inside the storage hull
  (a deterministic check of the learned law, computed from training fits). (P3) A1 beats R1 (persistence) with margin 0 in Test B;
  a physically trivial improvement the pipeline must show.

## 13. Identifiability protocol (one figure + appendix; synthetic only; JA6)

- **Design:** the real Samsung design (40 + 19 conditions, replicates, cadence and window from section 5) **[b]**; ISU design as in P4/P5.
- **Generators** written before any real fit, truth never from model fits: G0 well specified (positive control); G1 misspecified time law
  (t^z with z depending on T, plus a logistic knee in throughput whose onset shifts with stress); G2 non-additive stress law (charge-rate
  hinge with a temperature-shifted threshold, plus DoD x SOC product); optional G3 storage and cycle terms not additive. Pilot grids
  are in `runs/pilot_P4/config.json`. **G2 strengthened:** P4 found the additive-GAM class floor on the true surface at 0.05-0.07,
  below the 0.15 pass line, so G2 could not discriminate. The confirmatory G2 uses the upper values of the pilot grid for the hinge
  H = 1.2 and the product P = 0.8 (no new numbers); the pilot-strength G2 is a sensitivity. **Validity rule:** computed before fitting
  any model, a G2 draw is discriminating only if its additive-GAM class floor exceeds 0.15; if the median floor stays below, KAN
  superiority cannot be claimed from G2 and H3 "win" is unavailable.
- **Constants:** fade level, noise and offset spread follow the pilots (round numbers: median final fade 20 pp, noise 0.5 pp, offset
  sd 0.15), not matched to Samsung summary statistics as JA6 asked, because matching needs label-derived scalars (OPEN-6).
- **Metrics:** nRMSE of the recovered log-rate surface on the fixed 500-point hull Latin hypercube divided by the true range
  (absolute level included, as defined in J, centred reported alongside because P4 showed free-G carries a level offset: KAN/free 0.61
  absolute but 0.76 centred); Kendall tau of variance-based stress importance with truth; forecast RMSE on held-out conditions.
- **Thresholds (JA6):** pass nRMSE <= 0.15 on G1 and G2, fail > 0.30, between = partial. Structured median <= 0.67 x free-G median and
  paired Wilcoxon p < 0.05 over 20 draws. KAN lower than additive GAM on (strengthened) G2, paired p < 0.05; on G0 KAN not above GAM by
  more than 10 %. Because P4 passed "any structured" only on the absolute metric (GAM/free 0.76), the claim is stated for the
  KAN-structured model; it extends to "structured" generically only if the GAM also meets 0.67. Interval widths: diagnostic, no claim.
- **Hard vs soft on G1:** the H2 contrast on synthetic data (20 draws, paired); P5 saw PINN below hard in 4 of 5 draws with a small
  median ratio (0.96 primary, 0.84 whole-axis collocation) and one reversal. Collocation placement (record end vs whole time axis) is
  decided on training-condition inner CV, not on P5. Residual map: optional G3; a residual-map claim needs a rank-correlation threshold
  with the true violation surface that the statistician sets before G3 runs; without it Fig. 7 carries no claim.
- **Size:** 3 generators x 20 draws x 4 models (A1, A2, A4, A8) = 240 fits at the pilot tuning effort (8 trials x 2 inner folds, equal
  across arms), plus G1 hard vs soft 20 draws.

## 1. Still viable for JES?

**Yes, but as a narrow, conditional advance—not a claim that PINN-KAN beats data-driven models.** P3 says tuned learners are already near the replicate floor on ISU and Sandia. There, the honest result is parity in accuracy plus any demonstrated physical consistency. Samsung remains the decisive primary test; its corresponding headroom result is still pending.

The strongest honest headline is: **a joint calendar-and-cycle model uses storage and cycling experiments to predict held-out ageing conditions, while controlled ablations test whether its residual and learned stress maps add value.** Novelty remains “as a combination” with only moderate confidence in the re-audit; Zhang et al. and Hallinger et al. are still unresolved, so don’t overstate priority.

A JES reviewer is most likely to call this a real advance if the model **predicts held-out conditions and a held-out storage condition from early check-ups**, and the result survives comparison with Stroebl’s semi-empirical model and matched data-only, hard-ODE, and GAM/MLP controls. A tiny RMSE gain alone will not do it. If the soft PINN ties or loses to exact integration, report that plainly and drop the claim that the residual improves forecasting.

## 2. What to improve first, by impact per day

1. **Resolve the closest-prior-art and comparator question.** Read Zhang et al. (*JES* 182, 125097), Hallinger et al., and Stroebl et al. (*JES* 142, 119552) in full. Zhang is specifically flagged as a PINN on Samsung 50E; its dataset and task must be checked, not inferred from its title. Stroebl is the mandatory same-data calendar-plus-cycle comparator. A novelty overlap or an unfair baseline could sink the paper outright.
2. **Make the temperature claim statistically defensible.** The 35 °C cells all come from one lab; that is not identifiable as a temperature effect. See section 4.
3. **Simplify the confirmatory question and analysis.** The draft has many arms, tests, gates, and figures. Preserve the primary held-out-condition forecast and a small set of diagnostic ablations; treat the rest as supporting or exploratory.
4. **Add a restrained physical plausibility check.** Compare the fitted calendar temperature dependence with activation-energy ranges from relevant literature only after matching chemistry, SOC, and test protocol. Report differences and limits. **Unverified:** the relevant literature values have not been checked here; a numerical “agreement” claim is premature.
5. **Only then add cross-dataset panels.** ISU and Sandia are useful evidence of transportability, but P3 supports non-inferiority there, not superiority. Keep those claims separate from the Samsung result.

A well-resourced group would likely have more independent cells, replicated temperature-by-lab combinations, and validation on another cell design. The immediate deficit is not model scale; it is the lack of independent evidence that separates temperature from lab and validates the learned law.

## 3. Is there a better use for the PINN residual?

The strongest use is the draft’s proposed **soft coupling of a flexible solution surface to a calendar-plus-throughput rate law**, with collocation in the unobserved forecast interval. This tests whether a soft residual helps under model mismatch. It should be judged against exact integration of the same law, not presented as inherently better.

Collocation at untested temperatures or “calendar twins” can make a forecast obey the assumed law there, but it **cannot validate** that temperature response. It is a model-imposed extrapolation. Coupling storage and cycling arms is scientifically useful because storage informs time-driven fade and cycling informs throughput-driven fade; however, if both arms are already encoded in the ODE, the residual adds no independent constraint beyond that law. Likewise, a residual enforcing consistency between check-up capacity and measured throughput can be valuable as a diagnostic, but exact integration enforces the same consistency more directly.

So keep these as diagnostics or ablations. A residual map is informative only if its error can be compared with observed departures or an independent validation set; otherwise it visualizes model disagreement without establishing which model is right.

## 4. Temperature–lab confounding

Do **not** call the 35 °C result temperature extrapolation or attribute its error or success to temperature alone. All 35 °C cells being from lab INT means temperature is aliased with lab; stage is also partly entangled. Adding lab and stage covariates cannot identify separate effects where the design has no overlap.

Frame the 35 °C holdout as **transfer to a combined temperature–laboratory condition**, and state the confound beside the result. Show the design map colored by both lab and stage. Run a sensitivity analysis on the overlapping temperature/lab combinations where such overlap exists, and report whether the learned temperature response is stable there. If the overlap cannot support that analysis, present the 35 °C result as a bundled domain holdout and remove temperature-specific language from the abstract and title. Collocation at 35 °C storage does not cure the confound; storage at 35 °C was not experimentally observed.

## 5. Three biggest preregistration weaknesses

1. **The primary claim still overreaches the observed headroom.** H1c switches between superiority and non-inferiority based on a training-side floor rule; Samsung’s P3 result is pending. Freeze that decision using Samsung training folds before scoring outer test folds. Given ISU/Sandia P3, parity with flexible learners is plausible, not a fallback failure.
2. **The temperature claim and residual mechanism are underidentified by the design.** The draft acknowledges the 35 °C/lab alias but leaves Test C as a conditional temperature claim. Reword it as joint domain transfer now; specify which overlapping strata can support a temperature-specific sensitivity analysis. Treat unsupported “calendar twins” as extrapolative constraints, not evidence.
3. **Too many confirmatory contrasts and decision branches for the available condition counts and compute budget.** The draft has a three-test Holm family, secondary families, multiple gates, synthetic generators, and 10–14 arms, while the condition is the statistical unit. This risks low power and makes the conclusion hinge on a complicated outcome tree. Choose one primary endpoint and the smallest set of essential contrasts: PINN vs same model without residual, KAN vs strongest GAM/MLP stress map, and the Stroebl comparator. Predefine all other analyses as secondary or exploratory.

Finally, the draft should resolve its open items before freeze: stage-aware cell identity, the Stroebl implementation details, MLP stress-map arm, model-capacity matching, Samsung-specific headroom rule, and whether Stroebl belongs in the primary multiplicity family.
