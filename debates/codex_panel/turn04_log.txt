## 2. Why the current design produces F1 (diagnosis, for the judge)

- PINN role "integrate the law": removed by the closed form (D013 (a)); A2 is the exact solution of the PINN's own law.
- PINN role "regularise F where data are absent" (collocation, twins): F is a surrogate of the law; with the law fixed, collocation adds no information about the cell, only consistency with the law's own stress map. It cannot beat the law-integrator unless the law is wrong.
- KAN role "accuracy": a hidden-layer KAN [5,h,1] with h up to 8 on 32 training conditions is a tuned spline MLP; accuracy over GAM/MLP is E2-sized.
- KAN role "interpretability": the KAN literature's selling point; the frozen design uses it only as partial-dependence plots of a non-additive map (D015 F1).
- Comparators: H1a (A9) is a low bar (F5), H6 is a win over a model its authors call inadequate (F6), H1c is gated and probably unwinnable (E1).

So the redesign changes the regime (scarce data) and the KAN claim (readable law, tie accepted), and demotes everything that cannot be won to a qualifier with fixed wording.

## 3. Options

Common conventions: unit = held-out condition; primary test = exact one-sided sign test on L_c = ln(S_comp,c / S_A,c) with margins ln 1.02 (tier S) and -ln 1.10 (tier N), as D012. "Chance" is a qualitative judgement from E1-E8, not a probability; no pilot on Samsung exists.

### Option 1. Knowledge transfer across arms (storage <-> cycling)

- **Hypothesis H-T1.** Training jointly on 132 storage cells lowers the forecast error of held-out cycling conditions relative to cycling-only training (A1 vs A13), because the shared calendar term r_cal(T, SOC) is identified by storage cells. Dose-response sub-hypothesis: the gain grows as cycling conditions become scarce (m = 8 vs 32) and with the calendar share. Converse H-T2: cycling cells lower the forecast error of held-out storage conditions (A1 vs A14, S2, n = 19).
- **Endpoint.** Condition-level retention RMSE (pp), log ratio. Dose-response: Spearman correlation between L_c and a **label-free** design covariate (designed EFC per week, E7), n = 40.
- **Test.** Exact sign test A13 vs A1, tier S/N, n = 40, level 0.025. Dose-response: Fisher-z approximation, 80 % power for |rho_s| about 0.43 at n = 40 (textbook value, not simulated). H-T2: n = 19, sign test (15 positives needed at 0.025); no tabulated MDE below about 17 % at n = 13 and above 12.5 % at n = 27 suggests above 15 %.
- **Power.** Same-model contrast (rho about 0.95): MDE about 9-12 % at n = 40. Any stratified subset (for example the 20 lowest-throughput conditions) has n = 20, not tabulated; interpolating the n = 13 and n = 27 columns puts the 80 % MDE above 13 % even at rho 0.95 (to be simulated by the statistician).
- **Risk.** (i) The calendar share of fade is probably modest in every cycling condition (E7: throughput 23-112 EFC/week, factor 5 only), so the transfer gain is probably below 9-12 %. (ii) Storage cells also feed the backbone tournament; the gain may be a backbone effect, not a calendar-term effect. (iii) The carrier is the shared law, which A2 has too; the soft residual is not the carrier. (iv) Temperature is lab-aliased (35 C INT, 45 C HM); storage cells at 10 C are all SIE. (v) H-T2 is expected null: storage conditions are measured directly.
- **Chance of a demonstrable effect.** Low to moderate for the main effect at n = 40; moderate for the scarcity version (m = 8), where the cycling-only model has too few conditions to separate the calendar part.
- **Title and claim if it carries the paper.** "Calendar-ageing tests as informative priors for cycling forecasts: ..." Claim: "joint training lowered the typical held-out cycling error by X % (interval) and the gain was larger where cycling conditions were scarce."
- **Verdict.** Keep as a **qualifier** (Q-T) at two grid cells and as a figure panel; not strong enough alone.

### Option 2. Data-poor regimes (learning curves)

Two axes, both available from the data and the code base.

**2a. Early check-ups k (window length).** k in {0, 2, k_full}, k_full = k_cyc by the frozen rule. k = 0 is the window-information reference demanded by D015 F7 (condition-only forecast, no cell information); k = 1 descriptive.
**2b. Ageing tests m (number of training cycling conditions per fold).** m in {8, 16, 32}; the 8 held-out conditions of each fold are identical for all m, so contrasts are paired across the grid. Training subsets are a deterministic max-min (space-filling) selection among the fold's 32 training conditions in standardised stress space (identifier-only, label-free, reproducible; the logic of the dataset's own conditioned-Latin-hypercube and pi-OED designs); random subsets are a sensitivity row. All storage cells stay in training in the base grid.

- **Hypothesis H-P (the PINN as a prior).** In the most data-poor cell the physics-informed family (law + F + residual, stress map chosen per fold by inner CV among A1 KAN, A3 parametric, A4 additive GAM) has a lower median condition error than the best data-only learner (inner-CV best of A9, A11, A12 with physics-motivated features, see fairness below). Prediction: the advantage is largest at (m = 8, k = 2) and shrinks as m and k grow (E1 says it is about nil at (32, k_full)).
- **Endpoint.** Condition retention RMSE (pp) at each cell; primary quantity L_c per cell.
- **Test.** Fixed-sequence testing at 0.025 per step (no multiplicity cost): g1 = (8, 2), g2 = (16, 2), g3 = (32, 2), g4 = (32, k_full); stop at the first step that fails. Tier S one-sided; reverse tier S evaluated at g1 regardless (safety finding). Result = number of consecutive passes J (0 to 4) and the sign at g1.
- **Power.** Different model families, rho about 0.75: 80 %-power gain 17-22 % at n = 40 (E4). That is large for E1's regime but small for a regime where a learner with 8 training conditions in a 5-D stress space must extrapolate; whether the true gain at g1 reaches it is **not measured** (Samsung blinded). The pre-freeze pilot P6 (section 5.6) measures the direction on ISU training sides.
- **Risk.** (i) At m = 8 every model may collapse to the same near-constant forecast (ratios near 1). (ii) Inner CV with 8 conditions is noisy (2 inner folds; equal for all arms). (iii) Straw-man risk: a reviewer will call a data-only learner without Arrhenius-type features a weak baseline; fairness condition: A11/A12 get a tuned choice between raw stresses and physics-motivated features (1/T, log C-rate, log DoD, SOC and SOC squared, the A3 abscissae) and the data-only family includes A9. (iv) Subsampling conditions changes the hull; report lab and temperature composition per cell. (v) "Physics beats data-only at small n" is a known pattern; the novelty is the quantified crossover on a designed dataset with a semi-empirical reference, not the pattern.
- **Chance.** Moderate for g1 (textbook regime, unmeasured here); low for g4 (E1).
- **Title and claim.** P+: "Physics-informed neural networks ... forecast lithium-ion capacity fade from few ageing tests and early check-ups"; claim: "with m = 8 training conditions and two check-ups the typical held-out condition error was X % lower than the best data-driven learner (interval), and the advantage extended down to cell g_J."
- **Verdict.** Adopt as the PINN's primary role.

### Option 3. KAN as readable, physically checkable stress laws

- **Hypotheses.** H-K1 accuracy tie: the closed-form law extracted from the trained KAN (A15) is non-inferior to the unsimplified KAN (A1) within 10 %. H-K2 stability: the selected function family per stress input is the same across outer folds. H-K3 consistency: the direction of each extracted stress effect agrees with the sign of the corresponding exponent in the dataset authors' (verified) model A10, and the effective activation energy of the calendar term is reported with its fold spread against the authors' own fit and against **verified** literature only. Optional H-K4: the extracted law is more accurate than A10 (superiority), as a qualifier.
- **Procedure (A15, to be specified in full by method-architect after the opponent round).** Per outer fold, on training conditions only: tuned KAN with hidden width limited to {1, 2}; sparsify (existing L1 plus entropy penalty, sparsity level from the tuned grid); prune edges below a pre-set importance; fit each surviving first-layer edge by a **pre-registered library** (zero, linear, power, log, exponential/Arrhenius in 1/T, quadratic, hinge) with a fixed BIC rule and a complexity cap; refit the free coefficients end to end under the same loss. The extraction rule is frozen before any outer prediction.
- **Endpoints.** (K-acc) sign tests of A15 vs A1 at +-ln 1.10 (TOST) at g4. (K-stab) number of the 5 stress inputs whose family label (pruned counts as a label) is identical in at least 4 of 5 folds; context: the same count on the NC-2 permuted-stress fits. (K-con) number of inputs whose extracted direction matches the sign of A10's exponent; needs FB-2 closed. (Q-A10) A15 vs A10 tier. Thresholds "4 of 5 inputs" are the proposer's design choices, flagged for the statistician; they are not derived.
- **Power.** TOST at n = 40 with same-model rho about 0.95: 0.71-0.95 at Holm step 1 (E4), higher at 0.025. K-stab and K-con are counts over 5 inputs and 5 folds: no formal power; they are pass/fail descriptors with pre-stated rules. This is the honest limit of interpretability claims.
- **Risk.** (i) A15 loses accuracy through the width cap or the library (then the KAN role is "not shown"). (ii) With 3 storage temperatures (10 C all SIE, 45 C all HM) E_a is lab-confounded; the comparison is only to A10's fit on the same data, and a lab-indicator row is mandatory. (iii) The 35 C level exists in cycling only. (iv) The BIC library can pick different families fold to fold when the edge is nearly linear (stability fails for a benign reason; report "equivalent over the hull" as a second label, pre-defined as max difference below 5 % of the edge range, a proposer's choice). (v) Reviewers may call 5 inputs a thin test of "interpretability"; the claim is worded as "stable and consistent", not "discovered physics".
- **Chance.** Accuracy tie: moderate to high. Stability: moderate, unknown. Consistency with A10's signs: moderate (monotone effects expected, but non-monotone SOC-window effects may appear). Superiority over A10 (K-D): unknown.
- **Title and claim.** K+: "... with readable Kolmogorov-Arnold stress laws ..."; claim: "a closed-form law extracted from the KAN reached the accuracy of the full KAN within 10 % (interval), the same functional family was selected in k of 5 folds for n of 5 inputs, and the directions agreed with the dataset authors' model for n of 5 inputs."
- **Verdict.** Adopt as the KAN's primary role. It converts "KAN = spline MLP" into a deliverable (a formula plus its stability), with the tie pre-stated.

### Option 4. What the soft residual can do that the closed-form ODE cannot (and an honest statement if nothing)

Honest statement first. With a fixed law and a closed-form (or RK4) integral, the hard ODE uses every piece of information the law carries. The soft residual adds **no** information about the system; it can only (a) relax the law where the law is wrong (F can bend, lambda_res tunes how much), (b) interpolate between the law-driven limit (lambda_res large, A2-like) and the data-driven limit (lambda_res small, A9-like), (c) expose where the law is violated (residual map), and (d) accommodate laws without a closed form, which RK4 also handles. Anything beyond (a)-(c) would be a claim without a mechanism. If the data show a tie with the better of A2 and A9 everywhere, the PINN's contribution is robustness (not having to choose), and the paper must say so.

Testable forms:
- **4a. Regime adaptivity ("no regret").** Using the same (m, k) grid: A1 is non-inferior (tier N) to the inner-CV best of {A2 hard law, A9 data-only} at each cell, and strictly better (tier S) at any cell where the two limits cross. Test: sign tests per cell, N then S; no extra data. Power: A1 vs A2 and A1 vs A9 are near-same-model contrasts (rho 0.90-0.95): NI at equality 0.40-0.95 (E4); the S tier at a crossing cell needs 9-16 %. Chance: NI moderate; S at a crossing cell low (E3: 4-16 %). Wording: "tracked the better of ... within 10 %" or "was not shown necessary".
- **4b. Controlled misspecification (backbone dose-response).** Fix the state factor h to each of {constant, self-limiting, accelerating} for A1 and A2 (existing `backbone` config), order the three by the hard ODE's training-side inner-CV error (training data only, computed before any outer scoring), and test A1_b vs A2_b in descending dose, fixed sequence. Chance: low to moderate (E3 is the only evidence). Risk: reviewers read a deliberately wrong law as a straw man; mitigated because the three backbones are the tournament's own candidates, but the result is a robustness statement, not an accuracy claim. Adopt only as an appendix row.
- **4c. Residual map as a law-violation detector.** Does |R| from A1 (training points) rank-correlate (n = 40) with the hard ODE's held-out error by condition, and does it beat the hard ODE's own training misfit mapped over stress as a predictor? Expected: equal to training misfit, i.e. no PINN-specific value. Chance: low. Descriptive figure only.
- **4d. Collocation where data are absent / calendar twins.** No mechanism beyond (a); keep as a mechanism description, not a test.
- **Verdict.** Adopt 4a as the qualifier Q-H (H2 restated on the grid, fixed wording), keep 4b-4c as appendix rows, state in the Discussion that the soft residual is not shown necessary unless Q-H says otherwise. This is the honest answer to F1 for the residual itself.

### Option 5. Other ideas

- **5a. k = 0 window-information reference (adopted).** Answers D015 F7: if the window adds nothing at k_full, "forecast from first check-ups" is empty. It is free for the law-based arms (no window inversion).
- **5b. Design-stage reading of k = 0 and m (adopted as the framing).** "Predict a planned ageing condition from the tests already run" is what an ageing-test planner does; it ties the study to the dataset's own subject (optimal experimental design).
- **5c. KAN-discovered term added to A10 (A10+).** Test A10+ vs A10 (same family, rho high, MDE about 10 %). Not adopted: it needs a second model-building step and A10 is still unverified (FB-2); possible follow-up.
- **5d. More condition-level units from further cycling datasets (for example BatteryLife).** Would lower the MDE (n = 126 pooled units from Samsung, ISU, SNL is already available but ISU/SNL are "not blind"). Not adopted: data volume (31 GB), CONTRACT preference and one-week limit; ISU and SNL are used as "also holds" replications of the same grid.
- **5e. Pooled units, cycling plus storage.** One sign test over 40 + 19 = 59 conditions of "held-out ageing condition". Heterogeneous estimand; secondary only.
- **5f. Hierarchical prior for the per-cell offset from replicate structure.** Would help law-based arms at k <= 2 but adds a tuned dimension. Not adopted.

## 5. Recommended design ("Design R")

### 5.1 Question and claim
"How few ageing tests and early check-ups does a physics-informed neural network with learned stress laws need to forecast the capacity fade of unseen cycling conditions, and are the learned stress laws readable and consistent with the dataset authors' model?" The claim contract is the outcome table of section 6; no sentence of the paper is chosen after the data.

### 5.2 Grid, arms, tuning
- Grid cells (m, k): m in {8, 16, 32}, k in {0, 2, k_full}; k = 1 descriptive. Primary path (fixed sequence): g1 = (8, 2), g2 = (16, 2), g3 = (32, 2), g4 = (32, k_full). If k_full = 2 then g3 = g4 and the sequence has three steps; if k_full = 3, g4 = (32, 3).
- Held-out set: the unchanged Test B (5 folds of 8, 40 conditions, `samsung_splits_v2.json`, hash pinned); only the training side is thinned.
- Arms: physics-informed family A1, A3, A4 (stress map by inner CV); A2 hard ODE; A9 data-only F; A10 semi-empirical (with per-cell factor, and "as published" row); A11, A12 data-only learners with physics-motivated feature option; A13 cycling-only (Q-T); A15 symbolic (K role; g4 only, plus g1 descriptive); R1, R2 and the k = 0 population forecast (reference). Dropped from Test B: A5, A7 (their only contrast, H1b, is replaced; A7 may stay at g4 as the "PiDDM-style" comparator), H3, G2-based KAN "win".
- Tuning: equal for every arm; same N of TPE trials, inner folds 2 for m < 32 and 3 for m = 32 (a stated rule, equal across arms); training-side only; comparators chosen per fold and per cell by inner-CV score, never by test error. The k axis costs inference only for A1-A4, A9, A10 (window inversion at test time); A11/A12 retrain per k (cheap).

### 5.3 Statistics
- Primary tests: exact one-sided sign tests (D012), tiers S (ln 1.02) and N (-ln 1.10), median estimand, exact order-statistic interval.
- P role: fixed-sequence over g1-g4 at 0.025 each (tier S); reverse S at g1 tested regardless.
- K role: TOST at g4 (two sign tests at +-ln 1.10, 0.025 each); K-stab and K-con as pass rules; Q-A10 tier.
- Qualifiers (Q-A10, Q-H, Q-T) at g1 and g4, each labelled secondary, interval reported, tier word by the shared template of section 6; none changes a title.
- Holm is not needed inside the primary roles (fixed-sequence for P; K's two components are a conjunction of independently stated pass rules). The two roles are tested at their own 0.025 and are not combined into a family claim; the abstract says so. This replaces the unclear alpha accounting of D015 F4.
- Rules from D015 adopted here: (i) whenever the sign of the mean paired pp difference differs from the median log ratio at a primary cell, the abstract adds the mean difference with its BCa interval and the worst-condition error; (ii) a lab-stratified, lab-indicator (including A10) or retain-all sensitivity row that lowers a primary tier lowers the tier word in the abstract automatically (other sensitivity rows carry no consequence); (iii) Holm ordering is by the statistic actually tested (moot here); (iv) a type-I simulation with fold-level and lab-level shared effects is still required before the freeze.

### 5.4 Fairness conditions (to be checked by red team)
1. Data-only family sees the same training cells, equal tuning budget, and may use Arrhenius-type features.
2. A10 uses the verified form (FB-2) both as published and with the per-cell factor; the weaker of the two sets the wording.
3. Subset selection is label-free and deterministic; random subsets are a sensitivity row.
4. All storage cells stay in the base grid; storage-off is Q-T.
5. Lab and temperature composition of every thinned training set is tabulated; the lab-indicator row exists for Test B including A10.

### 5.5 What the retained freeze conditions still require
FB-1 to FB-6 and FB-8 to FB-10 are unchanged; the new code items are: the subset selector, the k = 0 population forecast and k = 2 window path, the symbolic extraction (A15), data-only learner feature option, the fixed-sequence assembly in `stats_step.py`, the sentence templates, and a unit test for each limiting case (m = 32 reproduces the unthinned run bit for bit; k = full reproduces Test B; A15 with an untruncated library reproduces A1 to float tolerance on a noiseless synthetic law; subsets are identical for identical identifiers). README, stats step and draft 5.5 must be aligned with the new document (D015 F12).

### 5.6 Pre-freeze pilot P6 (training sides only, ISU, non-binding)
Learning curves on ISU (63 conditions, cyclic term only): m in {8, 16, 32, all} and the weeks-window analogue of k, for A1, A2, A9, A11, A12, A10-analogue, inner CV on training conditions only (the P3 enforcement). Purpose: show whether the ordering "law-based better than data-only at small m" exists at all on a dataset that is already "not blind". Pre-set reading, fixed now: if on ISU the physics-informed family is not lower than the best data-only learner at the smallest m in a majority of the five outer training sides, the lead tells Shlok before the freeze that P0 is the likely outcome; the design is not changed on the basis of the pilot's size. The pilot uses no Samsung number.

### 5.7 Compute (arithmetic from PREREG section 16, not measured)
Baseline Test B core at N = 20 is about 9.4 CPU-h expected (35.8 ceiling) for 8 neural arms over 5 folds. Design R drops two arms (A5, A7) from Test B and adds m = 8 and m = 16 refits for A1, A2, A3, A4, A9 with smaller training sets and 2 inner folds; the k axis adds inference only. Order of magnitude: the extra work is comparable to the dropped work, so the core total stays near 10-20 CPU-h expected with a ceiling near 40-70 (three single-thread processes: about one day wall clock expected). A15 adds a few refits per fold. The timing run at the freeze commit fixes N; the Partial plan of D008 (a) applies to every arm equally if triggered.

### 5.8 Power summary for the recommended design
- P role per step: a gain of about 17-22 % (assumed rho 0.75, n = 40) is detectable at 80 %; smaller gains would end as "none" at that step. Whether the gain at g1 reaches it is the open risk.
- K-acc: TOST detectable at equality with power 0.71-0.95 or higher (rho 0.95).
- Qualifiers: Q-A10 and Q-H are same-model-like contrasts (MDE 9-16 %); Q-T as in option 1.
- No simulation was run for fixed sequences, for the thinned-training correlation structure, or for n = 20 strata; the statistician must supply them (a freeze condition).

## 1. Replicate floor, a better reference, and D007

### 1.1 What the floor is and is not
`R_LOO` (judge, P3 `replicate_floor`): for cell j of condition k, RMSE of the mean trajectory of the other replicates against j at j's
forecast times; mean over replicates, median over training conditions. It is a valid lower bound only for an *oracle that knows the
condition's other replicates and not the held-out cell*. It is **not a bound for Test B** for three reasons, two of them mechanical:
1. Learners see the held-out cell's own window; `R_LOO` does not (P3 caution 1; ratios 0.82-1.04 on ISU).
2. The mean of r-1 other replicates carries its own noise: under iid replicate noise `R_LOO` is inflated over single-cell noise by
   sqrt(1 + 1/(r-1)) = 1.15 for 4 replicates (ISU) and 1.22 for 3 (Samsung's 35 of 40 conditions).
3. A model pools 39 other conditions to denoise a condition's curve; the replicate mean cannot.
Conversely it is not "reachable" either: for a held-out *condition* a model also pays a condition-generalisation error that no replicate
quantity measures. So no replicate-based number is a floor for Test B. It is a floor-like yardstick only for Test A (held-out replicates
of seen conditions).

### 1.2 Reference definitions (both descriptive, computed on training sides only, same window and forecast set as the endpoint)
Loss `l_j(t) = 1 - r_j(t)` (retention relative to the cell's own reference check-up). `m_{-j}(t)` = mean of `l_i(t)` over the other
replicates that reach t (linear interpolation in time, as P3). Window times `t_1..t_w` exclude the reference check-up; `t_w` is the last.
- `R_LOO`: as P3 (unchanged, kept for continuity). Named **replicate spread**.
- `R_WARP` (window-aware replicate predictor): forecast `l_hat_j(tau) = m_{-j}(tau) + [l_j(t_w) - m_{-j}(t_w)]` (additive form) or
  `l_hat_j(tau) = kappa_j m_{-j}(tau)`, `kappa_j = clip( sum_i l_j(t_i) m_{-j}(t_i) / sum_i m_{-j}(t_i)^2 , 0.25, 4)` (multiplicative form);
  no label after j's window is used for j. Condition value = mean over replicates of cell RMSE (pp); `R_WARP` = median over training
  conditions with >= 2 replicates, **for the form with the lower training-side median in that outer fold** (rule, ties to additive).
Report `E/R_LOO` and `E/R_WARP` (E = the arm's pooled score) in Table 1 as context. Neither enters a test or a margin.

### 1.3 Evidence (training sides, 5 outer folds of the P3 splits, W = 4 weeks ISU / 100 cycles SNL; `warp_*.json`)
| Dataset | `R_LOO` | `R_WARP` mult. | `R_WARP` add. | best tuned learner (P3) | ratio / `R_LOO` | / WARP mult. | / WARP add. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ISU | 5.75 pp | 5.60 | 5.24 | 5.13 pp | **0.90** | 0.92 | **0.98** (folds 0.89-1.15) |
| SNL | 2.62 pp | 3.38 | 1.45 | 2.97 pp | **1.13** | 0.89 | **2.06** (folds 1.60-2.60) |

ISU: the window barely helps a replicate predictor (5.75 to 5.24 pp): the learners are at the reference under every definition.
SNL: the additive window offset almost halves the reference (2.62 to 1.45 pp), the multiplicative form worsens it. The *same* learner
errors give 1.13, 0.89 or 2.06 depending on the definition. The reference quantifies noise plus window information; it does not say
how much a model can still gain at unseen conditions. A gate built on it cannot be trusted to decide the test type.

### 1.4 Does D007 stand?
D007 (contrast (iii) = non-inferiority on ISU and SNL, from the 1.2 gate) is **re-opened in part, as its own clause allows**.
- *ISU*: the NI conclusion stands under all three definitions (0.90, 0.92, 0.98; all below 1.2). Consistent with the data.
- *SNL*: the verdict is definition-dependent (1.13 / 0.89 / 2.06); the data do not support a pre-decided test type either way.
- *Samsung*: "decided by the same rule once data are in" is **withdrawn**. The gate is retired for all datasets.
- The draft margin `delta = R_LOO` could never be met as a superiority margin: on ISU it exceeds the comparator's own error (5.75 > 5.13 pp),
  on SNL it is 88% of it (2.62 of 2.97 pp). As a non-inferiority margin it would be 112% of E on ISU: vacuous. OPEN-4 is therefore answered: **the floor is not used as a margin**.
- Replacement: the **hierarchical rule** of section 2.3, applied to H1c on every dataset. It contains the NI outcome D007 wanted and
  allows superiority only if the interval earns it. D007's consequence stands unchanged: *the abstract cannot say "beats all
  data-driven models" unless H1c reaches tier S*. This change uses no Samsung data and no test fold; it is fixed before confirmatory runs.
  The lead should log it as a supersession row of D007 (not an edit).

## 2. Margins and the hierarchical test

### 2.1 Quantities (all from training-side data)
For comparator arm c and outer fold f: `E_f^c` = inner-CV condition-mean window-forecast RMSE (pp) of c's *selected* configuration on f's
training conditions (the same inner partition and metric used for tuning and for comparator selection; A10 and A3-type fixed-form arms get
the same 3-fold inner CV). Pooled `E^c = sum_f n_f E_f^c / sum_f n_f` with `n_f` = test conditions in fold f. For Test C, S2 use their own
training side. `margins.json` (E^c, delta_S, delta_N per contrast) is written and hashed after tuning ends and before any final refit
is applied to a test condition.

### 2.2 Margin values (rule)
- **Superiority margin `delta_S = 0.02 E^c`** (relative reduction of at least 2%). Purpose: exclude differences of no consequence while
  staying below the effect sizes the pilots suggest (P5: 3-14% per draw). Not zero, per the judge ("effect below the margin").
- **Non-inferiority margin `delta_N = 0.10 E^c`** ("no worse than 10% above the comparator"). Judgement, not derivation; stated as such.
  Sensitivity rows (descriptive): `delta_S` in {0, 0.05}, `delta_N` in {0.05, 0.15}.
- The replicate spread is **not** in the margin: with E = R on the pilots a margin of R is the whole error, and on Samsung E/R is unknown
  and may be far from 1.
- Illustration (pilot E, not Samsung): ISU E = 5.13 pp gives 0.10 and 0.51 pp; SNL E = 2.97 pp gives 0.06 and 0.30 pp.
- Contrast direction: `d_c = S_comp,c - S_PINN,c` (condition score in pp), positive favours PINN-KAN.
- Heteroscedasticity: condition-error CV is 0.45 on both pilots (training sides), so the pp-scale mean difference is dominated by hard
  conditions. The primary stays in pp (judge's endpoint). Mandatory robustness row: the same rule on `log(S_comp/S_PINN)` with margins
  `log(1.02)` and `-log(1.10)`; if the tier differs from the primary the abstract states both.

### 2.3 Hierarchical test for one contrast at Holm step level `a`
A contrast **passes margin m** iff (i) exact one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank p of `d - m` (H0: pseudo-median <= m) is <= a, AND (ii)
the one-sided BCa lower limit of mean(d) at confidence 1 - a (10,000 condition resamples) exceeds m. Tiers: **S** passes `delta_S`; **N**
passes `-delta_N` but not S; **none** otherwise. Implementation: `stats_rule.tier`. The null of N is nested in the null of S, so testing
N then S in sequence at the same level needs no extra correction. "Equivalent" wording needs a two-sided TOST at `+-delta_N`; N alone is
"non-inferior".
Behaviour of the rule, from simulation (family level 0.025, section 7): rejection at the null boundary is below nominal for the skewed
shape measured on ISU (max 0.0045 / 0.0050 / 0.0155 at nominal 0.00625 / 0.00833 / 0.025) and about nominal for normal errors
(0.0075 / 0.0105 / 0.0275); for a strong **left** skew of d (a few conditions where PINN-KAN fails badly), it is liberal, up to
0.0145 / 0.017 / 0.0395 (1.6-2.3 times nominal). That is the price of using a mean-based bound with n = 40; the share of non-finite
forecasts and the trimmed-mean/log-ratio rows are therefore mandatory beside any tier claim.

## 3. Holm family (OPEN-5)

Family, one-sided level 0.025 (two-sided 0.05 for a directional claim; the 95% intervals in tables then agree with the decision):
1. H1a: A9 (lambda = 0) vs A1 - hypothesis S.
2. H1b: inner-CV-best of A3-A7 vs A1 - hypothesis S.
3. H1c: inner-CV-best of A11/A12 vs A1 - **enters with the N p-value**; its S claim is a fixed-sequence follow-up at the same
   multiplicity level (compare `p_S x (Holm multiplier of H1c)` with 0.025).
4. **H6: A10 (Stroebl semi-empirical) vs A1 - hypothesis S. Included.**
Why H6 joins: it is the closest prior art and the abstract will say how PINN-KAN stands against the dataset authors' model; any contrast
named in the abstract/title/highlights must be in the family or be labelled exploratory. Cost: if A10 is as inadequate as its authors
state, its p is tiny and it takes Holm step 1, costing the others nothing; worst case it raises the first threshold from alpha/3 to
alpha/4 and the 80%-power effect by at most 0.5 points of relative improvement (section 7). The judge's claim (the conjunction of the
three model-part contrasts) is unchanged; H6 failing triggers outcome row K, not refutation.
H2 (hard vs soft) is **not** in the family. Outcome A (soft better) is tested only if the family passes (gatekeeping, no extra error
cost). Outcome C (hard better) is tested regardless (safety finding). Order of evaluation: A (tier S on `d = S_hard - S_PINN`, margin
`delta_S` of the hard ODE's E), then C (reverse S at `delta_S`), then B (TOST at `+-delta_N`); otherwise "unresolved", worded as B.
H4 (Test C): Holm-3 within Test C, secondary, n = 13. H5/S2 and the rest: exploratory unless the abstract uses them, then labelled
secondary with their own N rule at `delta_N`. Windows k-1, k+2 and all other rows: descriptive.

## 4. Unit of analysis, paired test, lab and stage

- **Unit = held-out condition** (Test B 40, Test C 13, S2 19, ISU 63, SNL 23). Condition score = mean over seeds, then mean over the
  condition's scored cells, of cell RMSE (pp). Equal weight per condition (estimand: the designed region of stress space, not the
  replicate count; 5 centre conditions have 6-12 cells and are not up-weighted). Sensitivity: weights by scored cells; a two-stage
  bootstrap (conditions, then cells within).
- **Estimand** per contrast: `psi = (1/N) sum_c [S_comp(c) - S_PINN(c)]` over the held-out conditions for the frozen pipeline, expected
  over initialisation seeds. Inference is to conditions drawn from this design hull for this cell type; not to other chemistries and not to
  stress outside the hull.
- **Pairing:** by condition (both arms scored on the same cells and windows). Seeds are not paired across arms; they are averaged first.
- **Dependence not modelled:** neighbouring conditions share training information and conditions in one fold share one fitted model;
  the standard errors treat conditions as exchangeable. Report per-fold means and the number of folds where the sign agrees; this is
  descriptive. Design effect: section 7 gives n = 27 as a "design effect 1.5" scenario.
- **Lab and stage.** From the metadata (identifiers only): of the 40 cycling conditions 37 are single-lab (INT 19, HM 14, SIE 4) and 3 are mixed
  (23 C is INT 6, SIE 3, HM+INT 1; 35 C is INT 13; 45 C is HM 14, HM+SIE 2, SIE 1)
  and every condition lies in one stage (stage 1: 25, stage 2: 15). Temperature is almost a function of lab, and the Test B folds are already stratified by temperature, so
  lab/stage cannot be blocking factors without removing the temperature contrast. Ruling: **not** blocking in the primary. Mandatory
  sensitivity rows: (a) bootstrap resampling conditions *within lab strata* (stratum = majority lab) and the post-stratified mean; (b) the
  mean of d per lab and per stage, with n; (c) leave-one-lab-out mean. If the stratified tier is lower than the primary tier the abstract
  must say so (disclosure, not an automatic downgrade). For Test C and S2 the temperature contrast is confounded with lab by design and
  no statistical device removes that; the wording rule is in section 6.

## 5. Seeds

- 5 final refits per arm and outer fold (seeds 0-4), as CONTRACT 6. Per-condition score = mean over seeds of the per-seed condition RMSE.
  The seed SD of the cross-condition mean is shown beside every score in tables; it is a reproducibility statement, not a confidence interval.
- Seeds are not units and are not pooled with conditions in any test. Never report the best seed or best fold; the selection rule for the
  comparator is the inner-CV rule of section 3 and nothing else.
- Why 5 suffice: seed noise adds `2 sigma_s^2 / S` to Var(d_c). With a per-seed SD of 5% of E (assumed; no pilot measured it) this is 0.001 E^2
  against `sigma_d^2` of 0.02 E^2 (same-model contrasts): +5% variance, +2.5% on the standard error; 10 seeds would save 1.3%. At 10% seed
  SD it would be +20% variance (+10% SE); still not worth more than 5. Cost of a change to 10 seeds is only the final refits (5 of 65 fits per
  arm and fold), so it can be adopted if the lead prefers, but the ruling does not need it. No post-hoc seed addition is allowed.
- Limitation to state: the tuned configuration comes from one TPE search per outer fold (as P3); search-to-search variation is not in the
  standard errors. A second search seed on the primary contrasts is advisable if compute allows and is an appendix row, not a decision input.

## 6. What goes into the abstract

For each primary contrast (n, estimand and tier stated): mean paired difference in pp with the 95% BCa interval (`stats_rule.effect_summary`),
the relative reduction `mean(d)/mean(S_comp)` with its interval from the same resamples, and the tier. Also PINN-KAN's own score as mean
over conditions with the seed SD. Not in the abstract: p-values, rank statistics, results of unadjusted tests, the sensitivity margins.
Wording by tier:
- **S**: "lower by X pp (Y%; 95% CI a to b; n = 40 held-out conditions)". "Outperforms"/"beats" only at tier S.
- **N**: "no more than 10% worse than [comparator] (one-sided lower bound ...)" plus the observed difference with its interval. "Comparable",
  "on par", "equivalent": only after a two-sided TOST at `+-delta_N`; "matches" is not allowed.
- **none**: "could not be distinguished"; give the interval; no direction language.
Scope words: "held-out cycling conditions of one cell type in three laboratories"; "an untested temperature" only if H4 reaches N or S **and**
the sentence carries the lab-confound clause; storage-transfer sentence only if H5 reaches N. A claim on ISU/SNL is "also holds / does not
hold", with its own n; never pooled with Samsung. Relative-reduction intervals use fixed margins from training data; the pp and relative
intervals are not corrected for the training-side margin being an estimate (stated). The judge's refutation sentence ("lower RMSE than every
model") is carried by tier S on H1a, H1b, H1c; N on H1c supports only the tie wording (title 3).

## 7. Power and precision (simulation; assumptions, not Samsung numbers)

Code `stats_power_sim.py`; results `power_sim_alpha0.025.json` (family level used here) and `power_sim_alpha0.05.json` (earlier run at 0.05;
same conclusions, MDEs 0.4-1.1 points lower); MDE table `mde_table_alpha0.025.csv`. 2,000 simulations per cell, bootstrap B = 1,000 inside
the simulation (10,000 in the real analysis), the exact decision rule of section 2.3, scale E = 1.

**Assumptions.** (A1) Condition-level paired differences are iid across conditions: `d_c = mu + sigma_d Z + seed noise`. (A2) `sigma_d`
from the correlation `rho` between the two arms' condition errors with SD(error)/E = 0.45 (measured: ISU 0.45, SNL 0.45 on training
sides): `sigma_d = 0.45 sqrt(2(1-rho)) E`, i.e. 0.14 E (`rho` 0.95, variants of one model: H1a, H1b), 0.20 E (0.90), 0.32 E (0.75, different
families: H1c). Measured anchor: ISU power law vs LightGBM have `rho` = 0.75 and SD(d)/E = 0.39 on training sides. The `rho` for PINN-KAN
pairs is **not measured**; it can be estimated from inner-CV condition scores before the freeze as a power report only (no rule changes).
(A3) Shape of Z: standardised ISU (power law - LightGBM) differences (skewness 1.06, excess kurtosis 2.7), plus normal and mirrored
(left-skewed) versions. (A4) Seed noise: per-seed SD 0.05 E, S = 5. (A5) Margins `delta_S` = 0.02 E, `delta_N` = 0.10 E.

**Minimum detectable relative improvement** (80% power, superiority at `delta_S`, skewed shape; power = P(tier S)):

| n (conditions) | arms' error correlation | Holm-3 step 1 (a = 0.025/3) | Holm-4 step 1 (0.025/4) | last step (0.025) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40 (Test B, ISU-like) | 0.95 (H1a, H1b) | **9.8%** | 10.3% | 9.1% |
| 40 | 0.90 | 13.7% | 13.8% | 12.5% |
| 40 | 0.75 (H1c) | **19.4%** | 19.8% | 17.5% |
| 27 (design effect 1.5) | 0.95 / 0.90 / 0.75 | 12.5% / 15.5% / > 20% | 12.8% / 16.2% / > 20% | 10.2% / 13.9% / 19.9% |
| 13 (Test C) | 0.95 / 0.90 / 0.75 | 16.8% / > 20% / > 20% | 17.4% / > 20% / > 20% | 14.3% / 19.0% / > 20% |

In pp: with E = 5 pp, n = 40 detects 0.49 / 0.69 / 0.97 pp at `rho` 0.95 / 0.90 / 0.75; with E = 3 pp, 0.29 / 0.41 / 0.58 pp. Samsung's E is
unknown, so quote the relative figure. Adding H6 to the family (Holm-4 instead of Holm-3) raises the MDE by about 0.1-0.5 relative points.
Power at selected true effects, n = 40, Holm-3 step 1: mu = 0.08: 0.47 / 0.20 / 0.07; 0.10: 0.83 / 0.43 / 0.13; 0.15: 1.00 / 0.94 / 0.47; 0.20:
1.00 / 1.00 / 0.84 (columns `rho` 0.95 / 0.90 / 0.75).

**Non-inferiority power at true equality** (`mu = 0`, `delta_N = 0.10 E`, P(tier S or N)), n = 40: Holm-3 step 1: 0.96 / 0.68 / 0.24; last
step 0.99 / 0.81 / 0.40. n = 27: 0.87 / 0.46 / 0.14. n = 13: 0.40 / 0.16 / 0.05. A tie with the best data-only learner (`rho` ~ 0.75) is
therefore **resolved as N only about a quarter to two fifths of the time** at 10%; with `delta_N = 0.15` the normal-approximation power at
Holm-3 step 1 is about 0.7 (not simulated). That is the price of a tight margin and the lead should know it: the tie outcome of the plan
is likely to read "unresolved" if the data-only learners are a different model family from PINN-KAN.

**Conjunction** (title 1 needs S on H1a, H1b, H1c), n = 40, equal true effect mu in all three, contrasts correlated 0.5, Holm, Wilcoxon only
(BCa omitted, so slightly optimistic): mu = 0.08: 0.45 (`rho` 0.95) / 0.18 (0.90); 0.10: 0.79 / 0.39; 0.15: 1.00 / 0.92.

**Plain reading.** The Samsung test-B design (40 conditions) can show that PINN-KAN beats the lambda = 0 network and the best alternative
stress map if the true gain is about 10% (same-model contrasts); it can show superiority over a different-family data-only learner only if
the true gain is about 19% (roughly 1 pp at E = 5 pp). The P3 pilots found the best data-only learner already at the replicate reference
on ISU (and on SNL by the judge's definition), so a 19% gain over it is unlikely; the plan should expect the N or "unresolved" tier on H1c
and title 3, and Shlok should be told so now rather than after the run. Test C (n = 13) cannot resolve anything below about 17% and is a
descriptive check. The power figures do not include fold-level dependence beyond the n = 27 scenario, and `rho` for PINN-KAN pairs is an
assumption.

## 8. Reported limits of this ruling
1. `rho`, seed SD and error shape for PINN-KAN pairs are assumptions; real values come from inner-CV condition scores, usable only for a power
   statement, not for changing rules.
2. The margins 2% and 10% are judgement; they are fixed now so that outcomes cannot move them. The sensitivity rows show the dependence.
3. SNL has 23 conditions, 20 with replicates; claims there are exploratory in strength (MDE > 20% for `rho` 0.75).
4. The `R_WARP` additive/multiplicative choice and the 0.25-4 clip are conventions; both forms are always reported.
5. The simulation uses B = 1,000 and 2,000 draws per cell (Monte Carlo SE of a power value about 0.01).
6. No Samsung number appears; the headroom quantities for Samsung (`E/R_LOO`, `E/R_WARP`) are to be computed on Samsung training sides after
   parsing, as descriptive entries in the PREREG section 11 slot; they decide nothing.

## 2. Why the current design produces F1 (diagnosis, for the judge)

- PINN role "integrate the law": removed by the closed form (D013 (a)); A2 is the exact solution of the PINN's own law.
- PINN role "regularise F where data are absent" (collocation, twins): F is a surrogate of the law; with the law fixed, collocation adds no information about the cell, only consistency with the law's own stress map. It cannot beat the law-integrator unless the law is wrong.
- KAN role "accuracy": a hidden-layer KAN [5,h,1] with h up to 8 on 32 training conditions is a tuned spline MLP; accuracy over GAM/MLP is E2-sized.
- KAN role "interpretability": the KAN literature's selling point; the frozen design uses it only as partial-dependence plots of a non-additive map (D015 F1).
- Comparators: H1a (A9) is a low bar (F5), H6 is a win over a model its authors call inadequate (F6), H1c is gated and probably unwinnable (E1).

So the redesign changes the regime (scarce data) and the KAN claim (readable law, tie accepted), and demotes everything that cannot be won to a qualifier with fixed wording.

## 3. Options

Common conventions: unit = held-out condition; primary test = exact one-sided sign test on L_c = ln(S_comp,c / S_A,c) with margins ln 1.02 (tier S) and -ln 1.10 (tier N), as D012. "Chance" is a qualitative judgement from E1-E8, not a probability; no pilot on Samsung exists.

### Option 1. Knowledge transfer across arms (storage <-> cycling)

- **Hypothesis H-T1.** Training jointly on 132 storage cells lowers the forecast error of held-out cycling conditions relative to cycling-only training (A1 vs A13), because the shared calendar term r_cal(T, SOC) is identified by storage cells. Dose-response sub-hypothesis: the gain grows as cycling conditions become scarce (m = 8 vs 32) and with the calendar share. Converse H-T2: cycling cells lower the forecast error of held-out storage conditions (A1 vs A14, S2, n = 19).
- **Endpoint.** Condition-level retention RMSE (pp), log ratio. Dose-response: Spearman correlation between L_c and a **label-free** design covariate (designed EFC per week, E7), n = 40.
- **Test.** Exact sign test A13 vs A1, tier S/N, n = 40, level 0.025. Dose-response: Fisher-z approximation, 80 % power for |rho_s| about 0.43 at n = 40 (textbook value, not simulated). H-T2: n = 19, sign test (15 positives needed at 0.025); no tabulated MDE below about 17 % at n = 13 and above 12.5 % at n = 27 suggests above 15 %.
- **Power.** Same-model contrast (rho about 0.95): MDE about 9-12 % at n = 40. Any stratified subset (for example the 20 lowest-throughput conditions) has n = 20, not tabulated; interpolating the n = 13 and n = 27 columns puts the 80 % MDE above 13 % even at rho 0.95 (to be simulated by the statistician).
- **Risk.** (i) The calendar share of fade is probably modest in every cycling condition (E7: throughput 23-112 EFC/week, factor 5 only), so the transfer gain is probably below 9-12 %. (ii) Storage cells also feed the backbone tournament; the gain may be a backbone effect, not a calendar-term effect. (iii) The carrier is the shared law, which A2 has too; the soft residual is not the carrier. (iv) Temperature is lab-aliased (35 C INT, 45 C HM); storage cells at 10 C are all SIE. (v) H-T2 is expected null: storage conditions are measured directly.
- **Chance of a demonstrable effect.** Low to moderate for the main effect at n = 40; moderate for the scarcity version (m = 8), where the cycling-only model has too few conditions to separate the calendar part.
- **Title and claim if it carries the paper.** "Calendar-ageing tests as informative priors for cycling forecasts: ..." Claim: "joint training lowered the typical held-out cycling error by X % (interval) and the gain was larger where cycling conditions were scarce."
- **Verdict.** Keep as a **qualifier** (Q-T) at two grid cells and as a figure panel; not strong enough alone.

### Option 2. Data-poor regimes (learning curves)

Two axes, both available from the data and the code base.

**2a. Early check-ups k (window length).** k in {0, 2, k_full}, k_full = k_cyc by the frozen rule. k = 0 is the window-information reference demanded by D015 F7 (condition-only forecast, no cell information); k = 1 descriptive.
**2b. Ageing tests m (number of training cycling conditions per fold).** m in {8, 16, 32}; the 8 held-out conditions of each fold are identical for all m, so contrasts are paired across the grid. Training subsets are a deterministic max-min (space-filling) selection among the fold's 32 training conditions in standardised stress space (identifier-only, label-free, reproducible; the logic of the dataset's own conditioned-Latin-hypercube and pi-OED designs); random subsets are a sensitivity row. All storage cells stay in training in the base grid.

- **Hypothesis H-P (the PINN as a prior).** In the most data-poor cell the physics-informed family (law + F + residual, stress map chosen per fold by inner CV among A1 KAN, A3 parametric, A4 additive GAM) has a lower median condition error than the best data-only learner (inner-CV best of A9, A11, A12 with physics-motivated features, see fairness below). Prediction: the advantage is largest at (m = 8, k = 2) and shrinks as m and k grow (E1 says it is about nil at (32, k_full)).
- **Endpoint.** Condition retention RMSE (pp) at each cell; primary quantity L_c per cell.
- **Test.** Fixed-sequence testing at 0.025 per step (no multiplicity cost): g1 = (8, 2), g2 = (16, 2), g3 = (32, 2), g4 = (32, k_full); stop at the first step that fails. Tier S one-sided; reverse tier S evaluated at g1 regardless (safety finding). Result = number of consecutive passes J (0 to 4) and the sign at g1.
- **Power.** Different model families, rho about 0.75: 80 %-power gain 17-22 % at n = 40 (E4). That is large for E1's regime but small for a regime where a learner with 8 training conditions in a 5-D stress space must extrapolate; whether the true gain at g1 reaches it is **not measured** (Samsung blinded). The pre-freeze pilot P6 (section 5.6) measures the direction on ISU training sides.
- **Risk.** (i) At m = 8 every model may collapse to the same near-constant forecast (ratios near 1). (ii) Inner CV with 8 conditions is noisy (2 inner folds; equal for all arms). (iii) Straw-man risk: a reviewer will call a data-only learner without Arrhenius-type features a weak baseline; fairness condition: A11/A12 get a tuned choice between raw stresses and physics-motivated features (1/T, log C-rate, log DoD, SOC and SOC squared, the A3 abscissae) and the data-only family includes A9. (iv) Subsampling conditions changes the hull; report lab and temperature composition per cell. (v) "Physics beats data-only at small n" is a known pattern; the novelty is the quantified crossover on a designed dataset with a semi-empirical reference, not the pattern.
- **Chance.** Moderate for g1 (textbook regime, unmeasured here); low for g4 (E1).
- **Title and claim.** P+: "Physics-informed neural networks ... forecast lithium-ion capacity fade from few ageing tests and early check-ups"; claim: "with m = 8 training conditions and two check-ups the typical held-out condition error was X % lower than the best data-driven learner (interval), and the advantage extended down to cell g_J."
- **Verdict.** Adopt as the PINN's primary role.

### Option 3. KAN as readable, physically checkable stress laws

- **Hypotheses.** H-K1 accuracy tie: the closed-form law extracted from the trained KAN (A15) is non-inferior to the unsimplified KAN (A1) within 10 %. H-K2 stability: the selected function family per stress input is the same across outer folds. H-K3 consistency: the direction of each extracted stress effect agrees with the sign of the corresponding exponent in the dataset authors' (verified) model A10, and the effective activation energy of the calendar term is reported with its fold spread against the authors' own fit and against **verified** literature only. Optional H-K4: the extracted law is more accurate than A10 (superiority), as a qualifier.
- **Procedure (A15, to be specified in full by method-architect after the opponent round).** Per outer fold, on training conditions only: tuned KAN with hidden width limited to {1, 2}; sparsify (existing L1 plus entropy penalty, sparsity level from the tuned grid); prune edges below a pre-set importance; fit each surviving first-layer edge by a **pre-registered library** (zero, linear, power, log, exponential/Arrhenius in 1/T, quadratic, hinge) with a fixed BIC rule and a complexity cap; refit the free coefficients end to end under the same loss. The extraction rule is frozen before any outer prediction.
- **Endpoints.** (K-acc) sign tests of A15 vs A1 at +-ln 1.10 (TOST) at g4. (K-stab) number of the 5 stress inputs whose family label (pruned counts as a label) is identical in at least 4 of 5 folds; context: the same count on the NC-2 permuted-stress fits. (K-con) number of inputs whose extracted direction matches the sign of A10's exponent; needs FB-2 closed. (Q-A10) A15 vs A10 tier. Thresholds "4 of 5 inputs" are the proposer's design choices, flagged for the statistician; they are not derived.
- **Power.** TOST at n = 40 with same-model rho about 0.95: 0.71-0.95 at Holm step 1 (E4), higher at 0.025. K-stab and K-con are counts over 5 inputs and 5 folds: no formal power; they are pass/fail descriptors with pre-stated rules. This is the honest limit of interpretability claims.
- **Risk.** (i) A15 loses accuracy through the width cap or the library (then the KAN role is "not shown"). (ii) With 3 storage temperatures (10 C all SIE, 45 C all HM) E_a is lab-confounded; the comparison is only to A10's fit on the same data, and a lab-indicator row is mandatory. (iii) The 35 C level exists in cycling only. (iv) The BIC library can pick different families fold to fold when the edge is nearly linear (stability fails for a benign reason; report "equivalent over the hull" as a second label, pre-defined as max difference below 5 % of the edge range, a proposer's choice). (v) Reviewers may call 5 inputs a thin test of "interpretability"; the claim is worded as "stable and consistent", not "discovered physics".
- **Chance.** Accuracy tie: moderate to high. Stability: moderate, unknown. Consistency with A10's signs: moderate (monotone effects expected, but non-monotone SOC-window effects may appear). Superiority over A10 (K-D): unknown.
- **Title and claim.** K+: "... with readable Kolmogorov-Arnold stress laws ..."; claim: "a closed-form law extracted from the KAN reached the accuracy of the full KAN within 10 % (interval), the same functional family was selected in k of 5 folds for n of 5 inputs, and the directions agreed with the dataset authors' model for n of 5 inputs."
- **Verdict.** Adopt as the KAN's primary role. It converts "KAN = spline MLP" into a deliverable (a formula plus its stability), with the tie pre-stated.

### Option 4. What the soft residual can do that the closed-form ODE cannot (and an honest statement if nothing)

Honest statement first. With a fixed law and a closed-form (or RK4) integral, the hard ODE uses every piece of information the law carries. The soft residual adds **no** information about the system; it can only (a) relax the law where the law is wrong (F can bend, lambda_res tunes how much), (b) interpolate between the law-driven limit (lambda_res large, A2-like) and the data-driven limit (lambda_res small, A9-like), (c) expose where the law is violated (residual map), and (d) accommodate laws without a closed form, which RK4 also handles. Anything beyond (a)-(c) would be a claim without a mechanism. If the data show a tie with the better of A2 and A9 everywhere, the PINN's contribution is robustness (not having to choose), and the paper must say so.

Testable forms:
- **4a. Regime adaptivity ("no regret").** Using the same (m, k) grid: A1 is non-inferior (tier N) to the inner-CV best of {A2 hard law, A9 data-only} at each cell, and strictly better (tier S) at any cell where the two limits cross. Test: sign tests per cell, N then S; no extra data. Power: A1 vs A2 and A1 vs A9 are near-same-model contrasts (rho 0.90-0.95): NI at equality 0.40-0.95 (E4); the S tier at a crossing cell needs 9-16 %. Chance: NI moderate; S at a crossing cell low (E3: 4-16 %). Wording: "tracked the better of ... within 10 %" or "was not shown necessary".
- **4b. ## 5. Recommended design ("Design R")

### 5.1 Question and claim
"How few ageing tests and early check-ups does a physics-informed neural network with learned stress laws need to forecast the capacity fade of unseen cycling conditions, and are the learned stress laws readable and consistent with the dataset authors' model?" The claim contract is the outcome table of section 6; no sentence of the paper is chosen after the data.

### 5.2 Grid, arms, tuning
- Grid cells (m, k): m in {8, 16, 32}, k in {0, 2, k_full}; k = 1 descriptive. Primary path (fixed sequence): g1 = (8, 2), g2 = (16, 2), g3 = (32, 2), g4 = (32, k_full). If k_full = 2 then g3 = g4 and the sequence has three steps; if k_full = 3, g4 = (32, 3).
- Held-out set: the unchanged Test B (5 folds of 8, 40 conditions, `samsung_splits_v2.json`, hash pinned); only the training side is thinned.
- Arms: physics-informed family A1, A3, A4 (stress map by inner CV); A2 hard ODE; A9 data-only F; A10 semi-empirical (with per-cell factor, and "as published" row); A11, A12 data-only learners with physics-motivated feature option; A13 cycling-only (Q-T); A15 symbolic (K role; g4 only, plus g1 descriptive); R1, R2 and the k = 0 population forecast (reference). Dropped from Test B: A5, A7 (their only contrast, H1b, is replaced; A7 may stay at g4 as the "PiDDM-style" comparator), H3, G2-based KAN "win".
- Tuning: equal for every arm; same N of TPE trials, inner folds 2 for m < 32 and 3 for m = 32 (a stated rule, equal across arms); training-side only; comparators chosen per fold and per cell by inner-CV score, never by test error. The k axis costs inference only for A1-A4, A9, A10 (window inversion at test time); A11/A12 retrain per k (cheap).

### 5.3 Statistics
- Primary tests: exact one-sided sign tests (D012), tiers S (ln 1.02) and N (-ln 1.10), median estimand, exact order-statistic interval.
- P role: fixed-sequence over g1-g4 at 0.025 each (tier S); reverse S at g1 tested regardless.
- K role: TOST at g4 (two sign tests at +-ln 1.10, 0.025 each); K-stab and K-con as pass rules; Q-A10 tier.
- Qualifiers (Q-A10, Q-H, Q-T) at g1 and g4, each labelled secondary, interval reported, tier word by the shared template of section 6; none changes a title.
- Holm is not needed inside the primary roles (fixed-sequence for P; K's two components are a conjunction of independently stated pass rules). The two roles are tested at their own 0.025 and are not combined into a family claim; the abstract says so. This replaces the unclear alpha accounting of D015 F4.
- Rules from D015 adopted here: (i) whenever the sign of the mean paired pp difference differs from the median log ratio at a primary cell, the abstract adds the mean difference with its BCa interval and the worst-condition error; (ii) a lab-stratified, lab-indicator (including A10) or retain-all sensitivity row that lowers a primary tier lowers the tier word in the abstract automatically (other sensitivity rows carry no consequence); (iii) Holm ordering is by the statistic actually tested (moot here); (iv) a type-I simulation with fold-level and lab-level shared effects is still required before the freeze.

### 5.4 Fairness conditions (to be checked by red team)
1. Data-only family sees the same training cells, equal tuning budget, and may use Arrhenius-type features.
2. A10 uses the verified form (FB-2) both as published and with the per-cell factor; the weaker of the two sets the wording.
3. Subset selection is label-free and deterministic; random subsets are a sensitivity row.
4. All storage cells stay in the base grid; storage-off is Q-T.
5. Lab and temperature composition of every thinned training set is tabulated; the lab-indicator row exists for Test B including A10.

### 5.5 What the retained freeze conditions still require
FB-1 to FB-6 and FB-8 to FB-10 are unchanged; the new code items are: the subset selector, the k = 0 population forecast and k = 2 window path, the symbolic extraction (A15), data-only learner feature option, the fixed-sequence assembly in `stats_step.py`, the sentence templates, and a unit test for each limiting case (m = 32 reproduces the unthinned run bit for bit; k = full reproduces Test B; A15 with an untruncated library reproduces A1 to float tolerance on a noiseless synthetic law; subsets are identical for identical identifiers). README, stats step and draft 5.5 must be aligned with the new document (D015 F12).

### 5.6 Pre-freeze pilot P6 (training sides only, ISU, non-binding)
Learning curves on ISU (63 conditions, cyclic term only): m in {8, 16, 32, all} and the weeks-window analogue of k, for A1, A2, A9, A11, A12, A10-analogue, inner CV on training conditions only (the P3 enforcement). Purpose: show whether the ordering "law-based better than data-only at small m" exists at all on a dataset that is already "not blind". Pre-set reading, fixed now: if on ISU the physics-informed family is not lower than the best data-only learner at the smallest m in a majority of the five outer training sides, the lead tells Shlok before the freeze that P0 is the likely outcome; the design is not changed on the basis of the pilot's size. The pilot uses no Samsung number.

### 5.7 Compute (arithmetic from PREREG section 16, not measured)
Baseline Test B core at N = 20 is about 9.4 CPU-h expected (35.8 ceiling) for 8 neural arms over 5 folds. Design R drops two arms (A5, A7) from Test B and adds m = 8 and m = 16 refits for A1, A2, A3, A4, A9 with smaller training sets and 2 inner folds; the k axis adds inference only. Order of magnitude: the extra work is comparable to the dropped work, so the core total stays near 10-20 CPU-h expected with a ceiling near 40-70 (three single-thread processes: about one day wall clock expected). A15 adds a few refits per fold. The timing run at the freeze commit fixes N; the Partial plan of D008 (a) applies to every arm equally if triggered.

### 5.8 Power summary for the recommended design
- P role per step: a gain of about 17-22 % (assumed rho 0.75, n = 40) is detectable at 80 %; smaller gains would end as "none" at that step. Whether the gain at g1 reaches it is the open risk.
- K-acc: TOST detectable at equality with power 0.71-0.95 or higher (rho 0.95).
- Qualifiers: Q-A10 and Q-H are same-model-like contrasts (MDE 9-16 %); Q-T as in option 1.
- No simulation was run for fixed sequences, for the thinned-training correlation structure, or for n = 20 strata; the statistician must supply them (a freeze condition).

## Opponent’s verdict: revise, not accept as written

Design R addresses F1 by moving the experiment into data-poor regimes and giving the KAN a deliverable beyond raw accuracy. But it does **not yet show that PINN or KAN is the reason for any gain**. The data-efficiency question is valuable to battery-test planners; “physics helps when data are scarce” by itself is a familiar result, and the proposal itself cites small-sample PINN/PIKAN work. The exact prior results and their comparability to this task are **unverified** here. A JES paper must show *where* the prior helps, against a fair comparator, and what the learned law adds.

### 1. PINN claim and baseline fairness

The proposed P+ comparison is a **physics-informed family**—law, solution network, residual, and whichever stress map wins inner CV—against a data-only family. That can establish a useful system-level result, but it cannot attribute the gain to the residual, KAN, or rate-law structure. A win over that family comparator should not be described as “the PINN helps” without matched component ablations.

The fairness clause allowing data-only models to use Arrhenius-type features is necessary but underspecified. Every learner needs the same condition data, time/exposure coordinates, early-window summaries, and access to per-cell adaptation. Give the data-only side the same transformed inputs—such as \(1/T\), log C-rate and DoD, SOC terms, calendar time, and throughput—and tune them inside training folds. Otherwise the PINN receives physically engineered features and the comparator is accused of being deliberately uninformed. Conversely, a raw-feature model should remain as a separate baseline to show what feature engineering buys.

The P3 headroom on ISU/Sandia warns that full-data gains over flexible learners may be absent. It does not establish Samsung’s small-\(m\), small-\(k\) effect. The proposed ISU P6 is useful for rejecting a hopeless idea, but it cannot validate Samsung behavior or its storage arm.

### 2. KAN role: promising, but current endpoints risk decoration

Readable and fold-stable equations could be a real contribution if an electrochemist can check them and they predict a held-out condition. The current criteria—same selected function family in four of five folds, agreement in direction with A10, and extracted-law error within 10% of the numeric KAN—are mostly **internal consistency checks**. The 4/5 threshold is arbitrary, and agreement with a comparator fitted to the same dataset is not independent physical validation.

The electrochemical case needs more. Check whether calendar temperature dependence implies plausible activation energies, but use literature matched to the cell chemistry, SOC, and protocol; do not assume NCA/graphite unless verified. Check SOC dependence against the dataset authors’ reported non-monotonic behavior above 80% SOC, but only where there are measurements and sufficient replication. That behavior concerns calendar cells; it does not automatically validate cycling SOC-window effects. The storage temperature/lab confound further limits any activation-energy claim. **Literature ranges and the cell chemistry are unverified here.**

KAN versus a prespecified GAM/polynomial need not be a race if the claim is readability, but compare them anyway. At minimum, show equally sparse, rounded functions for both, out-of-fold prediction from each, and stability of *predicted response curves over the supported stress region*. If only the KAN can be simplified under the chosen extraction library, the comparison is unfair.

### 3. Statistics: useful grid, fragile gate

At \(n=40\), the ruling estimates an 80%-power detectable improvement of roughly **17–22%** for a different-family comparison at \(\rho=0.75\). Smaller effects are likely to be unresolved. Those are simulation assumptions, not Samsung power measurements; correlation and effect size at \(m=8\) remain unknown. The fixed-sequence rule also means that if \((m=8,k=2)\) fails, confirmatory claims at \(m=16\) or \(m=32\) stop—even if the advantage appears there. That is a poor fit for the stated question, “how many tests are needed?” Report the entire learning curve with simultaneous intervals or a preregistered trend/interaction test; reserve the gated claim for “benefit holds down to \(m=8,k=2\).”

Also make the \(k_{\text{full}}\) rule fixed from cadence before outcome inspection. The four grid points are not four independent replications: they reuse the same outer conditions and fitted folds. The proposed sign tests need a type-I simulation that includes fold-shared and lab-shared effects, as the proposal itself acknowledges. Do not describe the power as established until that is done.

### 4. Better use of the same data

Keep the data-efficiency experiment, but make it a **matched factorial ablation**:

- **Physics axis:** structured rate-law prior active versus removed/relaxed, with the same solution model, latent dimension, inputs, and tuning budget.
- **Stress-map axis:** KAN versus the strongest tuned GAM/interaction-GAM, using the same prior and training subsets.
- **Data axes:** \(m=8,16,32\) and \(k=0,2,k_{\text{full}}\), with identical outer held-out conditions and label-free condition subsets.

The primary PINN question becomes whether the physics prior changes the learning curve—especially at low \(m\)—relative to a data-only model given the same physics-motivated features. The KAN question becomes whether its extracted, rounded response law is stable and predicts held-out conditions as well as the full KAN and the matched GAM. Treat the soft-residual versus hard-integration comparison as secondary: when both encode the same rate law, the residual may add little beyond the prior. If soft PINN wins only under controlled law misspecification, say that is the demonstrated regime.

### Required changes before freeze

1. **Replace the single fixed-sequence result with an inferential learning-curve analysis.** Keep \(g_1\) as the threshold claim, but don’t let its failure erase evidence about \(g_2\)–\(g_4\).
2. **Match the comparison and attribution.** Same information and adaptation for data-only models; separate physics-prior and KAN effects with the factorial ablations. Do not call a family-level win a residual-specific win.
3. **Make the KAN result externally checkable.** Freeze the symbolic library and simplification rules; report held-out curve accuracy and stability against GAM/polynomial simplifications; restrict electrochemical validation to supported, independently sourced expectations, with chemistry and lab-confounding limits stated.

Without these revisions, Design R is more defensible than the original full-data comparison, but it still risks reading as a learning-curve study with a PINN/KAN label attached.

That behavior concerns calendar cells; it does not automatically validate cycling SOC-window effects. The storage temperature/lab confound further limits any activation-energy claim. **Literature ranges and the cell chemistry are unverified here.**

KAN versus a prespecified GAM/polynomial need not be a race if the claim is readability, but compare them anyway. At minimum, show equally sparse, rounded functions for both, out-of-fold prediction from each, and stability of *predicted response curves over the supported stress region*. If only the KAN can be simplified under the chosen extraction library, the comparison is unfair.

### 3. Statistics: useful grid, fragile gate

At \(n=40\), the ruling estimates an 80%-power detectable improvement of roughly **17–22%** for a different-family comparison at \(\rho=0.75\). Smaller effects are likely to be unresolved. Those are simulation assumptions, not Samsung power measurements; correlation and effect size at \(m=8\) remain unknown. The fixed-sequence rule also means that if \((m=8,k=2)\) fails, confirmatory claims at \(m=16\) or \(m=32\) stop—even if the advantage appears there. That is a poor fit for the stated question, “how many tests are needed?” Report the entire learning curve with simultaneous intervals or a preregistered trend/interaction test; reserve the gated claim for “benefit holds down to \(m=8,k=2\).”

Also make the \(k_{\text{full}}\) rule fixed from cadence before outcome inspection. The four grid points are not four independent replications: they reuse the same outer conditions and fitted folds. The proposed sign tests need a type-I simulation that includes fold-shared and lab-shared effects, as the proposal itself acknowledges. Do not describe the power as established until that is done.

### 4. Better use of the same data

Keep the data-efficiency experiment, but make it a **matched factorial ablation**:

- **Physics axis:** structured rate-law prior active versus removed/relaxed, with the same solution model, latent dimension, inputs, and tuning budget.
- **Stress-map axis:** KAN versus the strongest tuned GAM/interaction-GAM, using the same prior and training subsets.
- **Data axes:** \(m=8,16,32\) and \(k=0,2,k_{\text{full}}\), with identical outer held-out conditions and label-free condition subsets.

The primary PINN question becomes whether the physics prior changes the learning curve—especially at low \(m\)—relative to a data-only model given the same physics-motivated features. The KAN question becomes whether its extracted, rounded response law is stable and predicts held-out conditions as well as the full KAN and the matched GAM. Treat the soft-residual versus hard-integration comparison as secondary: when both encode the same rate law, the residual may add little beyond the prior. If soft PINN wins only under controlled law misspecification, say that is the demonstrated regime.

### Required changes before freeze

1. **Replace the single fixed-sequence result with an inferential learning-curve analysis.** Keep \(g_1\) as the threshold claim, but don’t let its failure erase evidence about \(g_2\)–\(g_4\).
2. **Match the comparison and attribution.** Same information and adaptation for data-only models; separate physics-prior and KAN effects with the factorial ablations. Do not call a family-level win a residual-specific win.
3. **Make the KAN result externally checkable.** Freeze the symbolic library and simplification rules; report held-out curve accuracy and stability against GAM/polynomial simplifications; restrict electrochemical validation to supported, independently sourced expectations, with chemistry and lab-confounding limits stated.

Without these revisions, Design R is more defensible than the original full-data comparison, but it still risks reading as a learning-curve study with a PINN/KAN label attached.
