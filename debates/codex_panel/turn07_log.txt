- **Splits (deterministic, from identifiers, written to `runs/splits/` with a hash before any model sees data; CONTRACT 6).** PL: 8 folds, each holding out one condition (both cells); training = the other 7 conditions (14 cells). NASA: 5 folds, each holding out one group; training = the other 4 groups. No random element, hence no seed.
- **Hull status declared in advance** (not exclusions): PL folds c1 (only SOC_mid 30) and c7 (only SOC_mid 70) extrapolate in SOC_mid; the other six interpolate in every active input. NASA folds G3 (only 43 C) and G4 (only 1 A) extrapolate; G1, G2, G5 interpolate. A "interpolating folds only" row is reported beside the all-fold row.
- **No m axis.** The training side is the whole campaign minus the held-out unit (7 PL conditions; 4 NASA groups). A learning curve over the number of conditions is not specified (7 and 4 are the largest m; m of 2-6 on PL is "too few points for a trend claim", feasibility 4(b)); P3 has no analogue.
- **Window rule: the Samsung rule, applied per campaign from the schedule alone (PREREG 5, FB-6).** k_full = round(n_med / 3) with n_med the median number of intermediate check-ups per included cell, **counted from the operation tables / discharge records before any capacity is read**. Grid: PL k in {0, 2, k_full}; NASA k in {0, round(k_full/2), k_full} (check-ups come every cycle, so "2" has no meaning there; the half-window keeps the ratio of the scarce to the full window roughly as on Samsung; a design choice). Arithmetic from the feasibility counts for NASA: the 23 included cells have discharge-record counts with median 72, so k_full = 24 cycles and the half window 12 (the script recounts). For PL the count is unknown (only 2 of 16 cells parsed). If k_full = 2 on PL the grid is {0, 2}; if k_full < 2 PL is no-go (as PREREG J16 section 8).
- **Forecast set:** all later quality-passed check-ups of the cell, to the end of its record (no life-level cut: the sets do not all reach a common retention level); a cell is scored with at least one forecast point; the number of points per cell is reported; a sensitivity row restricts to cells with at least 3 (PREREG 5).
- **Headline row per campaign:** A1c against D\* at the scarce window (PL k = 2, which with 7 training conditions is the nearest analogue of g1 = (8, 2); NASA half window). All other rows are reported without ranking.

| Arm | On the extra campaigns | Change from PREREG 7.1 |
|---|---|---|
| A1c | PINN-KAN, cyclic KAN on the active inputs only, **no calendar KAN** (`has_calendar=False`, as ISU/SNL) | input mask; same tuned space, same `h` set, same F net (width 32, depth 3, z dim 2) |

| D\* | inner-CV best of {A9, A11, A12}; feature sets as in 7.4 restricted to the active inputs (PL: SOC_mid, DoD, log C_dch; NASA: 1/T, log C_dch) plus t and EFC; **storage-rows option removed** (no storage cells) | none else |
| R1, R2, k = 0 reference | as defined | none |

- **Tuning budget:** the same N, the same sampler rule, and the same inner-fold rule that the frozen Samsung `budget_rule.json` fixes for a training side with fewer than 32 conditions (2 inner folds, or 4 if pilot P6b adopted four); grouped by condition inside the training side. **No timing run of its own and no Partial-plan re-decision**: if the Samsung rule says N = 40 or 20, the extras use that. Tuned once per fold, scored in inner CV at the scarce window, applied at every k (7.1 rule for m < 32). Hard-ODE backbone tournament once per fold on that fold's training units, as in PREREG 7 / D20 6.
- **Endpoint:** cell RMSE (pp) of the five-seed ensemble forecast over the forecast set, mean over the unit's scored cells, then the paired log ratio L_u = ln(S_comparator,u / S_target,u) per held-out unit; positive favours A1c. Mean paired pp difference and the worst-unit error are printed beside every median (the 9.8 rule 1 reading: if the sign of the mean disagrees with the median, the Results sentence adds both).
- **Seeds:** final refits 0-4; tuning seed 1000 + fold index (NASA: fold index 0-4; PL: 0-7); TPE sampler seed = fold index; no Monte Carlo step is needed (counts and order statistics only), so the 20261105 root is unused.

### 1.7 Statistics: descriptive, with exact intervals (and why no gated family)

Machinery: the sign-test and order-statistic helpers of `graph_test.py` (RES section 1), used only in their *interval* form.

| Quantity | Rule | Numbers (arithmetic, n fixed by the design) |
|---|---|---|
| Per unit | condition score, log ratio | PL n = 8; NASA n = 5 |
| Count | positives = units with L_u > 0 (L_u = 0 is not positive) | reported as "k of n" |
| Interval for the median | the full range [L(1), L(n)] of the sorted unit log ratios | coverage at independent units 1 - 2^(1-n): **0.9922 (n = 8)**, 0.9688 (n = 6), **0.9375 (n = 5)**; the next inner interval has only 0.9297 (n = 8) and 0.625 (n = 5) |
| Smallest exact one-sided p of a unanimous result | 2^-n | 0.0039 (n = 8), 0.0313 (n = 5); 7 of 8 gives 0.0352 (above 0.025) |
| Plain exact p | **not reported as a result**; it exists in the files for audit | no tier is derived |

Why no test and no tier: (i) with n = 5 even 5 of 5 gives 0.031 > 0.025 and with n = 8 only 8 of 8 passes 0.025, before any allowance for dependence; (ii) the PREREG confirmatory rule (fixed design effect, rho_s0 = 0.03) was simulated for n = 40 only (RES 4.4; PREREG 9.3 "the simulation covered n = 40 only") and its counts do not exist for n = 5 or 8; (iii) units are not independent: the 8 PL (or 5 NASA) training sets overlap in 6 of 7 (3 of 4) conditions, so the independence coverage above is an optimistic upper figure and is printed as such; (iv) adding nodes to the graph or the secondary family would change the Holm levels (0.005, ...) and the critical counts of the approved analysis (PREREG 9.4, 9.5, 9.11), which is a change to the closed design. **Alpha accounting row (to be added to 9.11 by the designer): "EXT-PL, EXT-NA: none; counts and ranges only".**

**Direction labels (selection of a Results sentence, not inference).** For each contrast: "A1c lower" iff positives >= 6 of 8 (PL) or >= 4 of 5 (NASA); "comparator lower" iff negatives >= 6 of 8 or >= 4 of 5; otherwise "mixed". Under a fair coin per unit these have probability 0.1445 each way for n = 8 (37/256) and 0.1875 each way for n = 5 (6/32), so "mixed" has probability 0.711 and 0.625 (arithmetic). The labels exist to remove discretion in wording, are printed with the null probabilities, carry no error control, and are never called significant or a tier. A fixed sentence template per label (section 1.13) is the only text permitted.

**Always printed, in the Appendix:** the full per-unit table (all 8 or 5 log ratios and condition scores, per arm, per k), the per-unit hull status, the number of forecast points per cell, and the interpolating-folds-only row.

### 1.8 Fade go/no-go and schedule go/no-go (the lead's request; computed only after the addendum card is frozen)

Order of events: the card (splits, stress coordinates, active inputs, QC rules, window rule, this go/no-go, templates) is committed and hashed; **then** `fade_gate.py` (written and committed before it is run) reads capacities once and writes `fade_gate.json`; then models. The script uses capacity values only, no model, and its verdict is a printed rule, not a choice.

- **Definitions.** F_c = 100 x (1 - retention at the cell's last quality-passed check-up) in pp; F_u = median of F_c over the unit's cells. A cell is *scheduled* if it has at least 3 post-window check-ups at k_full.
- **S-gate (schedule), per campaign:** go iff at least 75 % of included cells are scheduled (PREREG JA8-3 analogue, "fewer than 3 post-window check-ups for most cells").
- **F-gate (fade), per campaign:** go iff at least 75 % of the units have F_u >= 5 pp (PL: >= 6 of 8; NASA: >= 4 of 5). 5 pp is a design choice, ten times the 0.5 pp noise constant of the synthetic design (PREREG 13) and the order of the replicate spread, not a measured quantity.
- **No unit and no cell is dropped by the gate** (3.3(8)). A unit with small fade stays in and persistence will usually win there; that is reported.
- **No-go consequence:** the campaign is reported in one sentence plus its fade table (F_c per cell, which is not a model result), and no forecast table. The no-go cannot change any Samsung item.
- Expected, from the README stop rules only (not from data): NASA cells in G3 (10 days, 40 cycles) and part of G5 (B0049-52: 25 cycles, software crash) may fail the F-gate or the S-gate; whether NASA survives is unknown and is allowed to fail.

### 1.12 CPU cost (arithmetic, not a measurement; CPU only, three single-thread processes)

Basis (PREREG 16, from V3/J16): one neural arm over 5 outer folds at full Samsung data, N = 20, costs about 1.2 CPU-h expected and 4.5 at the ceiling, with 3N + 5 = 65 fits per fold, i.e. 325 fits: **13.3 s expected, 49.8 s ceiling per full-data fit**; the m = 8 and m = 16 refits are about half of that (6.6 s, 24.9 s). I take the extra-campaign fit at **0.25 of the Samsung m = 8 expected cost (1.7 s)** and at **1.0 of the m = 8 ceiling (24.9 s)**; the extra campaigns have 14 or about 19 training cells and no storage cells, but step counts and collocation do not shrink with cell count, hence the generous factors. Fits per arm and fold with 2 inner folds: 2N + 5 = 45 (N = 20) or 85 (N = 40). Arm-units per campaign: A1c, A9, A4c = 3, A2 about 0.3, A11/A12 retrained per k about 0.5; total 3.8.

|---|---|---|---|
| N = 20, expected / ceiling (CPU-h) | 0.63 / 9.5 | 0.39 / 5.9 | 1.0 / 15.4 |

## 3. Is this the best result possible, or is there room for improvement?

- The best *realistic* outcome is modest: an internally consistent descriptive subsection, and, if PL goes, a direction (a or c) on 8 units. It cannot become a confirmatory replication; the honest label is "does not contradict / is consistent with", only in the descriptive sense.
- **PL is the strongest asset** (not seen by the author; design varies SOC mid-point, depth and rate; check-ups at a fixed reference cycle, like Samsung). It is also the weakest on size: n = 8 and two folds are extrapolations.
- **NASA adds the name, little evidence.** It is the first item cut under any CPU pressure. A reasonable alternative for the lead to weigh: keep NASA only as the "datasets considered" row plus the fade table (zero CPU) if the professor accepts CALCE PL as the second dataset. I propose to run it if the F-gate and S-gate pass, because the professor named NASA.
- **Room for improvement:** (i) read Saxena et al. (temperature, check-up cadence, window control) before the card freezes, which could add a fourth active input or a temperature note; (ii) if a designed, temperature-controlled campaign with fixed reference check-ups were available it would be a better second set than NASA (ISU and SNL, already in PREREG 3.4, are not blind; the Oxford, SNU and TBSI folders in `data/raw` were not examined and are not proposed here); (iii) a pooled test across ISU, SNL, PL and NASA would be a meta-analysis of tiny n and is not proposed.
- Where this extension could lift the paper: showing that the same pipeline runs, unchanged, on a SOC-window design is the sort of robustness material a JES reviewer expects; it does not lift the title.

## 4. What are we lacking that a JES reviewer would hit?

1. **CALCE PL is not on disk.** One of 8 zips is almost complete (SOC_20-80_2C, 73.4 of 74.6 MB), one is at 40 of 67.5 MB, six have not started; only 2 of 16 cells have been parsed (feasibility 4, 6). Cycle counts, check-up counts and the characterisation capacity position are unverified. The 6-of-8 completeness rule exists for this.
2. **Provider facts unverified:** PL temperature, check-up cadence and rest handling (page digest only); NASA chemistry and format ("Li-ion", 2 Ah per README; 18650 LiCoO2 is secondary knowledge, not to be printed as a provider fact).
3. **Citations not yet verified:** Saxena et al. 2016 (J. Power Sources 327, 394-400), Saha and Goebel 2007 (repository entry), the earlier study. Each needs a Crossref-verified DOI and a page/table location via the citation validator before use; the paper was not read.
4. **Code that does not exist:** the two loaders, two dataset specs (input masks), the splits, `fade_gate.py`, the A10c separability test, PC-1 on the new designs. The pinned-file rule (below) must be respected.
5. **Selection of the datasets** is by the professor's request and by what could carry the law; a reviewer may ask "why these two"; the "datasets considered" table with reasons is the answer.
6. **No comparison with the PL authors' model or with published NASA numbers**; a reviewer may want one. Stated limitation; the tasks differ.
7. **No temperature/batch separation on NASA** and **no calendar term** (rest-heavy PL: calendar time is a large share of elapsed time; D\* may use t, A1c cannot; this favours D\*, conservative for A1c).
8. **SOC-window drift** (Coulomb-counted windows) means the stress coordinate is nominal.

## 5. What would make a reviewer reject it

- A tier word, "significant", "validated", "generalises" or "robust across datasets" attached to n = 8 or n = 5. Prevented by the vocabulary rule, the templates, and the absence of a test.
- Selective reporting: showing the extra campaigns only where A1c wins. Prevented by the fixed Conclusions sentence in every direction, the per-unit tables and the "campaign void / no-go" sentence.
- Hidden analyst freedom on NASA (labels seen before). Prevented by hashing every rule before fitting, by deriving the rules from schedule counts, and by the previously-analysed label; not removable, so disclosed.
- Reading the section as a cross-dataset study (the retired topic), or as an evaluation-protocol comparison: prevented by section 1.3.
- Changing a gated item after the fact: prevented by isolation (1.9) and by the freeze order (1.14).
- Different treatment of A1c and the comparators on these sets (e.g. a longer budget for A1c): prevented by identical budget rules, equal trials, the same ensemble rule and `n_train_used` reporting.

## 1. What the author is asking for, and where the D021 findings leave us

# D021: per-edge cap on free coefficients of the frozen symbolic library ([ARCH: cap])

Author: method-architect, 2026-11-05. Status: proposal for red-team and judge; the designer fills PREREG_v4 7.3 item 1 and 17.3 from this file.
Reading: full text of `research/CONTRACT.md`, `code/pinnkan/symbolic.py`, `symbolic_library.json`, `code/tests/test_pk_v2_symbolic.py`, `code/pinnkan/synth.py`, `arms.SymbolicArm`, `runs/build_v2/{RESULT,CHOICES}.md`; PREREG_v4 7.3, 17.3 and the K-chain rows (grep plus the paragraphs read); D016 judge section 3 (full). `runs/prereg/subsets.json`: training identifiers only (design values of the 32 training conditions per fold). No Samsung label, outer-test datum or score was opened. CPU only (2 processes, 50 min). All numbers below are from `runs/D021_cap_synth/` (synthetic) or are counts of design identifiers.

## 1. Decision

**`max_coeffs_per_edge` = 2**, the same for A15 and G15, for the cyclic map and the calendar KAN, at every grid cell. Set in `code/pinnkan/symbolic_library.json`. Library hash (rule, cap-independent) `2bb5532ec941ae62542b894a671b7c56eb3d643a750386328047c4f50934abc4` (unchanged); sha256 of the library file with the cap, `45d171654e60e511b85e395b92d40e6eb1a3bde97c7faf171cc92c115a054efb` (the pin of PREREG 18 step 2b; recompute after any edit).

Consequence stated plainly: with the code's coefficient counts (zero 0; linear 2; log 2; quadratic, power, exponential, Arrhenius, hinge 3, each counting its additive constant), **cap 2 leaves {zero, linear, log} eligible on every edge.** The other five forms stay in the hashed library file but can never be selected. The extracted law is then a sum of effects that are each linear in the stress (rate exponential in the stress) or linear in its logarithm (rate a power law of the stress), with composition through the outer edge when h = 2. That is the standard semi-empirical form of a calendar plus cycle ageing law, and the sign of every effect is read off a slope.

**This decision has two costs that the designer and judge must accept or reverse** (section 6): (i) D016 judge 3.5, the check whether the calendar SOC slope changes sign above SOC 0.8, cannot be answered by the extracted law (no non-monotone form is eligible); (ii) the seven-family recovery unit test of PREREG 7.3 item 4 still runs, but at cap 3 as a test of the library functions, not at the pinned cap.

## 2. What the cap is (read from the code, not assumed)

`choose_family` drops a family iff `N_COEFS[f] > cap`. `N_COEFS` tops out at 3. So the cap is a three-way switch, not a dial:

| cap | eligible | note |
|---|---|---|
| 0 or 1 | zero only | every edge pruned; the law is a constant (verified by test and `edge_study.json`) |
| 2 | zero, linear, log | |
| 3, 4, 5, ... | the whole library | identical extraction (verified: family and theta equal in 120 of 120 pipeline fits and in every edge-study case; unit test) |

So "one neighbour on each side" of cap 2 is cap 1 (degenerate) and cap 3; of cap 3 it is cap 2 and cap 4 (identical to 3 by construction). A cap of 3 or more is "no cap": the preregistered safeguard would then rest on BIC and the ridge alone. That is the honest comparison, and it is why the study below compares cap 2 with cap 3.

## 3. Design arguments (no model fitted)

1. **The training design has three temperature levels.** In all five folds, at m = 32, 16 and 8, the training conditions have exactly 3 distinct temperatures (23, 35, 45 C; from `subsets.json`, identifiers only), with at least 2 conditions per level at m = 8. Other inputs: m = 32 has 14-18 distinct SOC_max, 10-11 DoD, 9-10 C_ch, 17-20 C_dch values; m = 8 has 4-5, 5-6, 4-6, 7-8. A temperature edge with k coefficients carries k - 1 identifiable shape parameters (the additive constant is confounded with the law's level). Three levels give two identifiable contrasts. A 3-coefficient form then interpolates the three level means with no residual degree of freedom, and the data cannot tell quadratic, exponential, hinge, Arrhenius and power apart. A numerical check (`edge_study.json`, `identifiability_3_levels`): for 400 arbitrary sets of three level means, the quadratic fits exactly in 400, exponential and Arrhenius in 137, hinge in 65; in 137 of 400 sets at least three different 3-coefficient forms fit exactly. Cap 2 leaves a slope and one residual degree of freedom on the temperature edge.
2. **BIC runs on the KAN's grid, not on the data.** The extraction scores families against the KAN edge on G = 101 points of the training hull; the number of conditions never enters. Between the data points the KAN edge is the spline's smoothness prior. Any form with enough shape freedom can follow that interpolation, and the 1 % floor `rms_tol_rel` is the only barrier. With 8 to 32 conditions the spline artefacts routinely exceed 1 % of an edge's range, so the floor does not stop the choice of a 3-coefficient form (edge study: a truly linear edge with a smooth 2 %-of-range perturbation is recovered as linear in 25-45 % of draws at cap 3 and in 100 % at 0.5 %; section 5 shows the same on the full pipeline).
3. **Parameter count against conditions.** At cap 3 the median extracted cyclic law has 18 coefficients for A15 h = 1 and 35 for h = 2 (G15: 15) from 32 training conditions; at m = 8 it has 17, 34 and 14.5 from 8. At cap 2 the counts are 12, 24 and 10. Both exceed 8 at g1; at m = 32 cap 2 stays below the 32 conditions for all three arms, cap 3 does not for h = 2 (35). (Coefficients are not all identifiable, which is argument 1; this is a count, not a degrees-of-freedom claim.)
4. **What cap 2 can and cannot represent on the 23-45 C hull.** The textbook Arrhenius effect on a log-rate (power with q = -1 in 1/T) is approximated by the log or linear form with a relative RMS error of 0.27 % of the edge range, below the 1 % floor; at cap 3 BIC itself already prefers the linear form for it (it selects linear at 0.54 % error). So for temperature the cap costs nothing the extraction can resolve. For genuinely curved edges the cost is real: noiseless quadratic with curvature ratio 0.3 loses 4.6 % of range, exponential with b = 1.5 loses 9.7 %, a hinge at mid-range 14.6 %. Those are the shapes that cap 2 cannot represent.
5. **Matched-pair symmetry.** The cap is per edge and the same for both arms, and the code path is one class; the cap cannot by itself favour A15 or G15. A15 with h = 2 has twice the edges of G15, so the total coefficient count differs by structure (printed beside K2, PREREG 7.3 item 2), which is unrelated to the cap.

K2 compares A15 with G15, so a baseline handicap of A15 on additive truth is the conservative direction for the K+ claim.

## 5. Alternatives and why they lost

1. **Cap 3 (equal to no cap).** Keeps D016 3.5 and the full library. Loses because on synthetic data it is never better in extracted-law error at m = 32 (worse in 6 of 6 arm-by-kind means), picks a 3-coefficient form on 92 % of truly linear edges, cannot be identified on the 3-level temperature edge, had the only non-finite refits, and adds nothing the 23-45 C hull can resolve for temperature. Its one advantage is representational reach for genuinely curved edges (section 3 item 4), which the synthetic truths hardly contain (G0 is linear except a mild sin; G2 has one hinge whose threshold moves with temperature, so no single-edge form fits it).
2. **Cap 1 or 0.** Every edge pruned; the law is a constant. Not a candidate (tested).
3. **A cap that differs by layer or by input (for example 3 on the calendar SOC edge, 2 elsewhere).** Would keep D016 3.5 and respect the 3-level temperature edge. Not chosen because the frozen rule has one number and the brief fixes one cap; a per-layer cap changes the interface and the hashed rule and has not been tested here. It is the first thing to try if the judge refuses cost (i) of section 1 (section 6).
4. **A level-aware eligibility rule (a family needs more distinct training levels than coefficients).** Principled for the 3-level temperature edge and compatible with cap 3. Not chosen: it changes the frozen rule and the library hash, needs red-team review and a new test, and was not part of the brief.
5. **Keep cap 3 and rely on BIC and the ridge.** The study shows the BIC floor does not prevent false structure (92 %), so the safeguard is not effective at this sample size.

## 6. What a JES reviewer, the judge and the red team should know

- **Cost (i), D016 3.5.** With cap 2 the calendar SOC slope cannot change sign, so the statement "the slope changes sign above 0.8" can only be checked on the learned KAN partial dependence (A1 or the parent), as a descriptive observation, not on the closed-form law. The designer must either strike or reword D016 3.5, or reopen the cap (alternative 3).
- **Cost (ii), library reach.** PREREG 7.3 item 1 lists eight forms; at cap 2 five are unreachable. The paper must say that the library is the frozen superset and the cap restricts it to {zero, linear, log}; the designer should state the cap in 7.3 and not leave a reader to find it in the code.
- **Where the synthetic result may not transfer.** G0 and G2 are mild by construction; real ageing edges (a saturating DoD or C-rate effect, an SOC maximum) may be curved enough that cap 2 loses more than the synthetic study shows (edge study: 1.5 to 19 % of edge range for noiseless curved forms). K1 (A15 within 10 % of A1) is the claim at risk; its power depends on this. Nothing here is a Samsung result.
- **Calendar edges** were not scored in E_law (the metric is the cyclic surface); the cap applies to them too.
- **Test C** (35 C held out) leaves 2 temperature levels in training; any A15 or G15 on Test C has a temperature edge with a single contrast, which cap 2 fits exactly and cap 3 would not identify at all. Not scored here.
- **Seeds and hyperparameters.** One parent seed per design seed, hyperparameters at the grid middles, 10 replicates; the intervals are over design seeds. The tuned configuration of the real run may differ.
- **The run configuration does not yet pin the cap.** `run.py` and `guard.py` do not read `symbolic_library.json`; PREREG 18 step 2b requires the cap value and the file hash in the config hash. That wiring is outside this brief (RESULT.md already lists `guard.build_freeze_payload` additions as open).

## 7. Re-open if
(a) the judge refuses cost (i) (then alternative 3, a calendar-only cap of 3, needs a code change, a new hash and a rerun of this study on the calendar surface); (b) a replicate shows cap 4 != cap 3; (c) the library, the 1 % floor or the grid G changes; (d) the real training sets have fewer than 3 temperature levels for a scored arm; (e) a pilot on Samsung training sides shows that cap 2 leaves a training-side E_law-equivalent gap above 10 % of the KAN's range (a check that uses no outer-test datum).

Cell-to-condition structure.
- **27 cells have one fixed protocol**, giving **21 distinct nominal (T, discharge, cut-off) tuples**: 24 C/2 A (cut-offs 2.7,
  2.5, 2.2: 5 cells), 24 C/4 A square wave (4), 24 C/4 A CC (2), 43 C/4 A (4), 4 C/1 A (4), 4 C/2 A (4 tuples, 2 cells
  each: B0049-52 and B0053-56). **Replicates exist only for 5 tuples** (B0005 and B0036; B0006 and B0018; the four 4 C/2 A

  stress space.
- **Stresses that vary:** ambient temperature (4, 24, 43 C), discharge current (1, 2, 4 A, i.e. 0.5C, 1C, 2C of the rated
  2 Ah), waveform (constant versus square wave), cut-off voltage (2.0 to 2.7 V, a weak depth-of-discharge lever).

  the law cannot be tested; the SOC window is nearly the same in every cell.

  batch and date; each temperature group has a different current set.
- **Depth of data.** Discharge cycles per cell: 25 to 197; **15 of 34 cells have 47 or fewer** (B0025-32, B0038-40,
  B0049-52), and 13 have at least 100 (B0005-07, B0018, B0033, B0034, B0036, B0042-44, B0054-56). The calendar span is 6 to 67 days. How far each cell actually faded is **not computed here**; a

  reasons not analyzed"; B0049-52: capacity and voltage "very low" in several runs, stopped by a software crash.
  (ii) README says capacity is for discharge "till 2.7V" although cut-offs differ. (iii) B0025-28 appear in two inner
  zips (set 2 and set 3) with identical CRCs: **duplicates, count once**. (iv) B0028: README says 4 A square wave for all
  four cells, but the discharge samples have a median absolute current of about 2.0 A (B0025-27: about 4 A); the README and
  the data disagree. (v) B0050 and B0052 contain discharge records with all-zero current or sub-1 V minimum voltage (parse
  and earlier inventory: empty field needing tolerant reading). B0033 (minimum about 1.0 V against a 2.0 V cut-off) and B0040 (about 1.75 V against 2.7 V) also contain discharge records
  whose minimum voltage is far below the stated cut-off. (vi) B0053 has 55 charge but 56 discharge records. (vii) Mixed-condition cells
  listed above. (viii) Impedance is always measured at 24 C even in the 4 C groups.
- **Calendar and storage.** Continuous cycling, no storage arm and no rest design; time and cycle count are almost the
  same coordinate (a cycling-only law cannot separate calendar from cycle effects here and does not need to).

**What it can support.**
- (a) Leave-condition-out forecast, cycling-only law: **mechanically possible** on the 27 fixed-protocol cells (21 tuples;
  5-fold on tuples gives about 4 held out per fold), forecasting from an early prefix of each held-out cell's own record
  (capacity is available every cycle, so any "k check-ups" is a choice, not a limit). Two informative stresses
  (T, discharge current) plus cut-off. Not testable: charge rate, SOC mid-point, DoD (beyond cut-off), the KAN stress
  functions for those inputs.
- (b) Learning curve: only m of about 4, 8, 16 tuples; with 21 tuples and heavy grouping the curve would be coarse.
- (c) Within-condition early-life forecasting: yes for the 13 cells with at least 100 discharge cycles (how far they faded is not computed here).
- Role: **descriptive generalisation** in a short results subsection or supplement, with the stop-rule and short-record
  caveats. It must not enter any gated confirmatory test of the Samsung design.

# D021: per-edge cap on free coefficients of the frozen symbolic library ([ARCH: cap])

Author: method-architect, 2026-11-05. Status: proposal for red-team and judge; the designer fills PREREG_v4 7.3 item 1 and 17.3 from this file.
Reading: full text of `research/CONTRACT.md`, `code/pinnkan/symbolic.py`, `symbolic_library.json`, `code/tests/test_pk_v2_symbolic.py`, `code/pinnkan/synth.py`, `arms.SymbolicArm`, `runs/build_v2/{RESULT,CHOICES}.md`; PREREG_v4 7.3, 17.3 and the K-chain rows (grep plus the paragraphs read); D016 judge section 3 (full). `runs/prereg/subsets.json`: training identifiers only (design values of the 32 training conditions per fold). ## 1. Decision

**`max_coeffs_per_edge` = 2**, the same for A15 and G15, for the cyclic map and the calendar KAN, at every grid cell. Set in `code/pinnkan/symbolic_library.json`. Library hash (rule, cap-independent) `2bb5532ec941ae62542b894a671b7c56eb3d643a750386328047c4f50934abc4` (unchanged); sha256 of the library file with the cap, `45d171654e60e511b85e395b92d40e6eb1a3bde97c7faf171cc92c115a054efb` (the pin of PREREG 18 step 2b; recompute after any edit).

Consequence stated plainly: with the code's coefficient counts (zero 0; linear 2; log 2; quadratic, power, exponential, Arrhenius, hinge 3, each counting its additive constant), **cap 2 leaves {zero, linear, log} eligible on every edge.** The other five forms stay in the hashed library file but can never be selected. The extracted law is then a sum of effects that are each linear in the stress (rate exponential in the stress) or linear in its logarithm (rate a power law of the stress), with composition through the outer edge when h = 2. That is the standard semi-empirical form of a calendar plus cycle ageing law, and the sign of every effect is read off a slope.

**This decision has two costs that the designer and judge must accept or reverse** (section 6): (i) D016 judge 3.5, the check whether the calendar SOC slope changes sign above SOC 0.8, cannot be answered by the extracted law (no non-monotone form is eligible); (ii) the seven-family recovery unit test of PREREG 7.3 item 4 still runs, but at cap 3 as a test of the library functions, not at the pinned cap.

## 2. What the cap is (read from the code, not assumed)

`choose_family` drops a family iff `N_COEFS[f] > cap`. `N_COEFS` tops out at 3. So the cap is a three-way switch, not a dial:

| cap | eligible | note |
|---|---|---|
| 0 or 1 | zero only | every edge pruned; the law is a constant (verified by test and `edge_study.json`) |
| 2 | zero, linear, log | |
| 3, 4, 5, ... | the whole library | identical extraction (verified: family and theta equal in 120 of 120 pipeline fits and in every edge-study case; unit test) |

So "one neighbour on each side" of cap 2 is cap 1 (degenerate) and cap 3; of cap 3 it is cap 2 and cap 4 (identical to 3 by construction). A cap of 3 or more is "no cap": the preregistered safeguard would then rest on BIC and the ridge alone. That is the honest comparison, and it is why the study below compares cap 2 with cap 3.

## 3. Design arguments (no model fitted)

1. **The training design has three temperature levels.** In all five folds, at m = 32, 16 and 8, the training conditions have exactly 3 distinct temperatures (23, 35, 45 C; from `subsets.json`, identifiers only), with at least 2 conditions per level at m = 8. Other inputs: m = 32 has 14-18 distinct SOC_max, 10-11 DoD, 9-10 C_ch, 17-20 C_dch values; m = 8 has 4-5, 5-6, 4-6, 7-8. A temperature edge with k coefficients carries k - 1 identifiable shape parameters (the additive constant is confounded with the law's level). Three levels give two identifiable contrasts. A 3-coefficient form then interpolates the three level means with no residual degree of freedom, and the data cannot tell quadratic, exponential, hinge, Arrhenius and power apart. A numerical check (`edge_study.json`, `identifiability_3_levels`): for 400 arbitrary sets of three level means, the quadratic fits exactly in 400, exponential and Arrhenius in 137, hinge in 65; in 137 of 400 sets at least three different 3-coefficient forms fit exactly. Cap 2 leaves a slope and one residual degree of freedom on the temperature edge.
2. **BIC runs on the KAN's grid, not on the data.** The extraction scores families against the KAN edge on G = 101 points of the training hull; the number of conditions never enters. Between the data points the KAN edge is the spline's smoothness prior. Any form with enough shape freedom can follow that interpolation, and the 1 % floor `rms_tol_rel` is the only barrier. With 8 to 32 conditions the spline artefacts routinely exceed 1 % of an edge's range, so the floor does not stop the choice of a 3-coefficient form (edge study: a truly linear edge with a smooth 2 %-of-range perturbation is recovered as linear in 25-45 % of draws at cap 3 and in 100 % at 0.5 %; section 5 shows the same on the full pipeline).
3. **Parameter count against conditions.** At cap 3 the median extracted cyclic law has 18 coefficients for A15 h = 1 and 35 for h = 2 (G15: 15) from 32 training conditions; at m = 8 it has 17, 34 and 14.5 from 8. At cap 2 the counts are 12, 24 and 10. Both exceed 8 at g1; at m = 32 cap 2 stays below the 32 conditions for all three arms, cap 3 does not for h = 2 (35). (Coefficients are not all identifiable, which is argument 1; this is a count, not a degrees-of-freedom claim.)
4. **What cap 2 can and cannot represent on the 23-45 C hull.** The textbook Arrhenius effect on a log-rate (power with q = -1 in 1/T) is approximated by the log or linear form with a relative RMS error of 0.27 % of the edge range, below the 1 % floor; at cap 3 BIC itself already prefers the linear form for it (it selects linear at 0.54 % error). So for temperature the cap costs nothing the extraction can resolve. For genuinely curved edges the cost is real: noiseless quadratic with curvature ratio 0.3 loses 4.6 % of range, exponential with b = 1.5 loses 9.7 %, a hinge at mid-range 14.6 %. Those are the shapes that cap 2 cannot represent.
5. **Matched-pair symmetry.** The cap is per edge and the same for both arms, and the code path is one class; the cap cannot by itself favour A15 or G15. A15 with h = 2 has twice the edges of G15, so the total coefficient count differs by structure (printed beside K2, PREREG 7.3 item 2), which is unrelated to the cap.

## 4. Study (rule fixed and committed before it was run: `study_rule.md`, commit c0a9793)

Design: `synth.make_synthetic` kinds G0 (additive, linear in T, DoD, C_ch, C_dch plus a sin in SOC) and G2 (non-additive, with a hinge in C_ch and a DoD x SOC product); 40 cycling conditions with 3 temperature levels, 8 held out, 32 train (m = 32), m = 8 by the project's max-min selector; arms A15 h = 1, A15 h = 2, G15; one parent fit per arm and cell, then extraction and the end-to-end refit at cap 2 and cap 3, extraction only at cap 4; hyperparameters fixed at `default_hp`. Outputs: `runs/D021_cap_synth/pipeline_p0.jsonl`, `pipeline_p1.jsonl`, `decision.json`, `edge_study.json`; code `code/prereg/d021_{edge_study,cap_study,cap_decide}.py`.

**Two things the rule did not anticipate, reported as found.**
- *The D2 margin is thin.* The verdict rests on an upper bound of 0.0210 against a threshold of 0.02. The point estimate (0.0168) and the false-structure rate (92 % against 0 %) point the same way and cap 3 is worse in 9 of 10 and 8 of 10 seeds, but a different bootstrap draw could flip the formal verdict. The exponential and Arrhenius forms are the likely cause (the code clamps the exponent at 20 and the refit can still blow up); I have not diagnosed it. The gap already exists in the parent networks (parent E_law A15 h = 1 minus G15: +0.0173 at m = 32, +0.0170 at m = 8, post hoc computation), so it is a property of the extra composition layer in a 26-condition fit, not of the cap: cap 2 adds 0.005, cap 3 removes 0.003. K2 compares A15 with G15, so a baseline handicap of A15 on additive truth is the conservative direction for the K+ claim.

## 5. Alternatives and why they lost

1. **Cap 3 (equal to no cap).** Keeps D016 3.5 and the full library. Loses because on synthetic data it is never better in extracted-law error at m = 32 (worse in 6 of 6 arm-by-kind means), picks a 3-coefficient form on 92 % of truly linear edges, cannot be identified on the 3-level temperature edge, had the only non-finite refits, and adds nothing the 23-45 C hull can resolve for temperature. Its one advantage is representational reach for genuinely curved edges (section 3 item 4), which the synthetic truths hardly contain (G0 is linear except a mild sin; G2 has one hinge whose threshold moves with temperature, so no single-edge form fits it).
2. **Cap 1 or 0.** Every edge pruned; the law is a constant. Not a candidate (tested).
3. **A cap that differs by layer or by input (for example 3 on the calendar SOC edge, 2 elsewhere).** Would keep D016 3.5 and respect the 3-level temperature edge. Not chosen because the frozen rule has one number and the brief fixes one cap; a per-layer cap changes the interface and the hashed rule and has not been tested here. It is the first thing to try if the judge refuses cost (i) of section 1 (section 6).
4. **A level-aware eligibility rule (a family needs more distinct training levels than coefficients).** Principled for the 3-level temperature edge and compatible with cap 3. Not chosen: it changes the frozen rule and the library hash, needs red-team review and a new test, and was not part of the brief.
5. **Keep cap 3 and rely on BIC and the ridge.** The study shows the BIC floor does not prevent false structure (92 %), so the safeguard is not effective at this sample size.

## 6. What a JES reviewer, the judge and the red team should know

- **Cost (i), D016 3.5.** With cap 2 the calendar SOC slope cannot change sign, so the statement "the slope changes sign above 0.8" can only be checked on the learned KAN partial dependence (A1 or the parent), as a descriptive observation, not on the closed-form law. The designer must either strike or reword D016 3.5, or reopen the cap (alternative 3).
- **Cost (ii), library reach.** PREREG 7.3 item 1 lists eight forms; at cap 2 five are unreachable. The paper must say that the library is the frozen superset and the cap restricts it to {zero, linear, log}; the designer should state the cap in 7.3 and not leave a reader to find it in the code.
- **Where the synthetic result may not transfer.** G0 and G2 are mild by construction; real ageing edges (a saturating DoD or C-rate effect, an SOC maximum) may be curved enough that cap 2 loses more than the synthetic study shows (edge study: 1.5 to 19 % of edge range for noiseless curved forms). K1 (A15 within 10 % of A1) is the claim at risk; its power depends on this. Nothing here is a Samsung result.
- **Calendar edges** were not scored in E_law (the metric is the cyclic surface); the cap applies to them too.
- **Test C** (35 C held out) leaves 2 temperature levels in training; any A15 or G15 on Test C has a temperature edge with a single contrast, which cap 2 fits exactly and cap 3 would not identify at all. Not scored here.
- **Seeds and hyperparameters.** One parent seed per design seed, hyperparameters at the grid middles, 10 replicates; the intervals are over design seeds. The tuned configuration of the real run may differ.
- **The run configuration does not yet pin the cap.** `run.py` and `guard.py` do not read `symbolic_library.json`; PREREG 18 step 2b requires the cap value and the file hash in the config hash. That wiring is outside this brief (RESULT.md already lists `guard.build_freeze_payload` additions as open).

## 0. Recommendation (single)

**Do not change A1, A15, G15 or the cap before the freeze. Put the KAN-side methodological contribution into an identifiability-audited extraction protocol (candidate E below), which changes no scored arm.** The pre-fixed synthetic rule adopted none of the architectural candidates: shape-constrained edges (M) lost in my implementation, library-aware training (L) improved accuracy slightly at m = 32 but missed its false-structure criterion and is not better at m = 8. ## 1. What the author is asking for, and where the D021 findings leave us

The KAN today is a spline map from stress to log-rate, used as a black box and then read by a post hoc symbolic step. D021 showed that the post hoc step is the weak link: BIC on the KAN's grid picks a 3-coefficient form on 92 % of truly linear edges (cap 3), the temperature edge has only 3 training levels, and the cap that stops the false structure (2) leaves {zero, linear, log} and makes D016 3.5 unanswerable. A methodological novelty "inside the KAN" would have to attack that: make the edges simpler during training (L), or constrain them by physics (M), or type them (B-candidate b). Each is tested or argued below.

## 2. Candidates

| id | idea |
|---|---|
| (a) M | monotone spline edges by construction for inputs with a physics sign (T +, DoD +, C_ch +; SOC_mid and C_dch free), outer edges increasing; unconstrained KAN as the ablation |
| (b) | physics-typed bases: T entered as the Arrhenius abscissa, power laws on throughput, so the "extracted" law is exact |
| (c) L | library-aware training: add to the parent's loss lambda x the squared distance of every edge (on the extraction grid) from its best {linear, log} fit, so that extraction loses nothing |
| (d) ML | M and L together |
| (e) E (mine) | identifiability-audited extraction: per edge report the number of training levels, the identifiable shape parameters, the BIC margin, the false-structure rate from a synthetic calibration, and label a form "readable" only where the data can identify it; no change to any scored arm |
| (f) (mine) | raise the BIC floor (rms_tol_rel 1 % to about 3 %) and keep cap 3: untested; changes the frozen rule and hash |

## 3. Per candidate: novelty, effect, fairness, cap, cost, tests, title

**Prior work to check (all unverified, from memory).** Monotone KANs: "MonoKAN" (certified monotonic KAN with cubic Hermite splines, 2024-2025 preprint). Shape-constrained additive models: Pya and Wood, SCAM, Statistics and Computing 2015. Monotone networks and lattices: Sill 1997; Gupta et al., JMLR 2016. KAN and its symbolic snapping: Liu et al., arXiv 2404.19756 and KAN 2.0, arXiv 2408.10205. Library-based equation learners: Martius and Lampert, EQL 2016; Brunton et al., SINDy, PNAS 2016; relaxed discrete choice as in DARTS. Identifiability: Raue et al., Bioinformatics 2009 (profile likelihood); stability selection, Meinshausen and Buhlmann 2010. In our target journal the precedent for KAN inside an Arrhenius law is Koenig and Deng, J. Energy Storage 158 (in `notes/PRIOR_ART_SCAN_lead.md`). Whether any of these (or a 2026 battery paper) already does what is proposed is for the novelty auditor.

**(a) M, shape-constrained edges.** Novelty: the method (monotone spline coefficients, monotone KAN) is prior art, MonoKAN and SCAM at least; what remains is the application with physics signs to a joint calendar-plus-cycle law, and the argument below. One real argument for it: cycling temperature is lab-confounded (35 C all INT, 45 C all HM), so a free T edge can absorb a laboratory offset as a non-monotone temperature effect; a sign constraint forbids that. Against: the physics fixes the sign for T and DoD, probably for C_ch (not verified from the dataset paper), not for SOC_mid (the dataset authors report non-monotone behaviour above 80 % SOC) nor C_dch. Effect at g1: uncertain. In the committed check M was far worse (G0 m = 8: E_par 0.169 against 0.121; section 4) but the check's M was under-initialised; a post hoc diagnostic (2 seeds, GAM, G0, m = 32) gives 0.060 and 0.052 for M with a better start, against 0.079 and 0.061 for B, i.e. about 0.014 of range better. So M could help where the sign prior is right and the data are scarce, and could hurt where it is wrong (G3 was meant to test that, and could not, because M was under-trained). K1/K2: no direct effect. Fairness: every law-based arm (A1, A2, A4, A5, A13, A15, G15) would need the same signs, or K2 stops isolating composition; the data-only comparators cannot be given them (LightGBM accepts monotone constraints, the GP does not), so P1 and P2 would then mix the law with a sign prior and the attribution of the gain to the physics law (explanation E1) is muddied. Two checks become tautologies: PC-2 (calendar rate monotone in T) if E(SOC) were constrained, which it must not be. Cap: monotone edges are simpler: false structure at cap 3 was 37.5 % (M) and 29 % (ML) against 94 % (B), still far from 0, so M does not replace the cap. Cost: about 2 days (parametrisation with a reliable initialisation, a certified-monotone test, wiring into all seven arms, a new tuned choice or a fixed one) and A1 is the primary arm: the pilots P4/P5 that support the design used the unconstrained KAN. Title: "Kolmogorov-Arnold stress functions" stays true; a new word ("physics-constrained") would be needed to claim it and the freeze has no sentence for it. Test plan: monotonicity certificate on a dense grid for every fit; G0/G2/G3 recovery with a corrected init and 10 seeds; lab-aliasing synthetic (a laboratory offset on the middle temperature level) as the case where M should win.

**(b) typed bases.** Novelty: none (the calendar term already uses the Arrhenius abscissa; KA-CRNN uses Arrhenius coefficients). Effect: the edge study (D021) shows 1/T and linear-in-T differ by 0.5 % of range on the 23-45 C hull, below the 1 % floor, so exactness buys nothing numerically; it changes the library and its hash and the seven-family unit test. What it does give, at no cost, is an activation-energy readout (the slope in the Arrhenius abscissa) at report time, with the 3-level caveat. Not a contribution; do as a readout only.

**(c) L, library-aware training.** Novelty: modest. The idea is quantisation-aware training transplanted to symbolic libraries; EQL, SINDy and KAN's own snapping are the nearest. What may be new is applying a projection-residual penalty on the very grid that the frozen BIC rule reads, with one library for a KAN and its matched GAM. Effect (committed check, 6 seeds, section 4): at m = 32 the parent's error is lower by 0.0055 of the truth range (upper bound -0.0035, better in 6 of 6 seeds), the cap-3 extracted law of L is better than the cap-2 extracted law of B by 0.0055 on G0 and G3 and by 0.0088 on G3 alone (the U-shaped C_ch edge that cap 2 cannot hold), but false structure at cap 3 only falls from 94 % to 79 % (criterion: 25 %), and at m = 8 L is slightly worse than B (+0.003 to +0.008). So L helps where there is data, does nothing for the scarce cell, and does not fix the BIC problem. K1/K2: applied to the parents of A15 and G15 only and with the same lambda and code path, K2 still isolates composition; it narrows the A15-minus-G15 gap on additive truth from 0.0250 to 0.0196. A1 would be untouched, so K1 would compare an L-trained closed form with a plain A1; that favours K1 slightly and must be disclosed. Cap: L1 failed, so D021 stands; the replacement rule I had specified (cap 3 plus lambda_lib) is not justified by the data. Cost: about 60 lines plus tests (1 day), +15 to 40 % per parent fit (measured 20 s to 23 s KAN and 15 s to 21 s GAM at m = 8), and lambda would be one more number (it replaces the unused sparsity choice for A15/G15). Title: unchanged.

**(d) ML.** Tracks M (E_par at G0, m = 32: 0.149 against 0.152 for M and 0.072 for L) and is worse than L alone in every cell of the check. Not a candidate.

**(e) E, identifiability-audited extraction.** Novelty: moderate, in the analysis of KAN laws rather than the network: KAN papers read symbolic forms off trained edges; D021 shows with ground truth that this over-reads edges (92 % false structure at cap 3, 0 % at cap 2), that the design fixes how many shape parameters an edge can carry (3 temperature levels give 2 contrasts; 137 of 400 random 3-level data sets are fitted exactly by at least three different 3-coefficient forms), and what the cap costs on curved edges (up to 19 % of edge range). A protocol that reports, per edge, levels, identifiable parameters, BIC margin and the calibrated false-structure rate, and withholds the word "readable" where the data cannot support it, is a methodological contribution a JES reviewer can use. Prior work: identifiability and profile-likelihood analysis (Raue et al.), stability selection; neither is for KAN edges to my knowledge (unverified). Effect on scored results: none (it adds a table and a figure panel to the Fig. 5 / Table 2 material and reuses `runs/D021_cap_synth`). Fairness: A15 and G15 get the same audit. Cost: 0.5 to 1 day, no change to arms or to the hash. Test plan: the audit reproduces the D021 false-structure rate on the same synthetic seeds; a unit test that an edge with L levels never gets a family with more than L - 1 coefficients flagged "identifiable"; per-edge label stability across the five seeds and the five folds (already planned in 11a item 6) is added as the stability-selection column. Title: "readable Kolmogorov-Arnold stress laws" is then supported rather than assumed, and the K+ sentence's "readable" can cite the audit.

**(f) larger BIC floor with cap 3.** Untested; touches the frozen rule and the library hash; no novelty. Mentioned because it is the cheapest route to cap 3 if D016 3.5 must be answered (together with a calendar-only cap, D021 alternative 3).

## 5. Why this and why not each alternative

1. **Why not M now.** Primary arm change two steps before the freeze, an unmeasured optimiser sensitivity, sign validity partly unverified, fairness to the data-only comparators not achievable, PC-2 must be protected, and the method is prior art (MonoKAN, SCAM). If the diagnostic's 0.014 survives a proper round it is a real g1 candidate.
2. **Why not L now.** All accuracy criteria passed, but the gain is 0.0055 of range at m = 32 and nothing at m = 8, and it does not fix the problem it was designed for (false structure 79 %). It is also a change to the K-chain parents that favours K1 slightly.
3. **Why not (b).** No numerical effect on this hull; changes the hash.
4. **Why not ML.** No better than M, and worse than L alone in every cell.
5. **Why E.** It is the only candidate that adds a defensible methodological result without touching a scored arm, and it uses evidence already on disk. Its weakness is that it is a protocol result, not an architecture; I do not claim more.

## 6. Is this the best result possible? What are we lacking? What would a reviewer reject?

Room for improvement: yes. (i) M with a robust parametrisation (I-splines or Hermite as in MonoKAN, with a start that does not depend on a magic constant) and a lab-aliasing synthetic where it should win; (ii) L with lambda in {1e3, 1e4} and a larger floor (f); (iii) both on Samsung training sides with inner CV only. Each synthetic round is about one hour on two processes; the Samsung-side step needs the lead.
Lacking: any real-data evidence; a verified physics sign for C_ch; the novelty audit of every name above; a tuned lambda; refit results (the check stops before the end-to-end refit); more than six seeds.
A reviewer would reject: "monotone KAN is MonoKAN" for (a); "this is EQL/KAN snapping with a penalty" for (c); "synthetic only, 0.5 % of range" for (c); "the protocol is bookkeeping, not a method" for (e) unless the calibration table and the identifiable-parameter rule are shown clearly and the label discipline changes what the paper claims.

## Datasets proposal — revise

A separate descriptive section can satisfy the professor’s request to include both datasets, but it will be **supporting evidence, not a JES advance**. With eight CALCE conditions and five NASA groups, the results will be fragile; NASA’s conditions are also confounded with batch, and its measured capacity mixes ageing with cycling-condition effects. Keep these results out of the paper’s main evidence for the method.

The proposed within-campaign training and testing avoids directly testing cross-dataset transfer, and the “previously analysed” disclosure is necessary. It does not make the NASA analysis independent: prior exposure could still shape choices. The retired-topic risk is reduced, not erased, by prohibiting pooled training and cross-campaign error comparisons. Keep the language strictly descriptive, avoid “external validation” or transfer claims, and disclose the prior NASA analysis prominently.

A separate sign rule would imply more than these small, overlapping holdout designs can reliably establish. In particular, five NASA groups cannot reach a one-sided \(p<0.025\) sign-test threshold even if all five favor the model. For CALCE, finish the download, resolve missing blocks, and confirm the check-up and fade criteria before committing to results. If either dataset fails its identifier-based go/no-go, report that fact rather than substitute another set.

**Required changes:**

1. Freeze an addendum after Samsung’s freeze, with all splits, windows, exclusions, and go/no-go criteria fixed before fitting either dataset.
2. Report per-condition/group outcomes and uncertainty descriptively, separately by campaign; no inferential tier or cross-campaign comparison.
3. Disclose prior NASA label exposure and its batch/capacity-measurement confounds; describe CALCE’s small condition count and incomplete-download risk.

## KAN proposal — revise

An “identifiability-audited extraction protocol” could help reviewers interpret the KAN outputs, but calling it a novel methodological contribution is a stretch. D021’s synthetic study shows why audits matter; it does not establish that the proposed audit is distinct from existing model-selection or identifiability practice. Relevant prior work is **unverified**.

The synthetic rejection of monotone KAN (M) is not conclusive evidence that the architecture is inferior: the proposal reports strong sensitivity to initialization, only six seeds, no end-to-end refit, and no held-out RMSE decision metric. That is a fair reason to avoid a last-minute architectural change, not to claim M has been fairly ruled out. Do not add M to the primary analysis without a new, prespecified test that addresses those issues and applies sign constraints fairly to all relevant comparators.

Cap 2 permits only zero, linear, or log edge forms. It is compatible with “readable Kolmogorov-Arnold stress laws” if that phrase means compact extracted expressions that retain predictive accuracy; it does **not** support claims that the method identifies Arrhenius, power-law, or hinge physics. Cap 2 also cannot represent the proposed non-monotonic SOC behavior. The title may remain, but any physical interpretation must be narrower than the model’s label.

**Strongest legitimate KAN contribution within three days:** present **selective, design-aware extraction**. For each edge, report the number of distinct training stress levels, the number of shape parameters the design can identify, the eligible families under cap 2, and the fold/seed stability of the extracted form. Where the data cannot distinguish expressions, report that ambiguity rather than naming a discovered law. Test it by applying the same extraction and reporting rules to A15 and G15, the existing design-matched synthetic cases, and held-out Samsung conditions. The risk is that a reviewer sees this as careful reporting rather than a new KAN method; frame it as an audit protocol unless a targeted novelty check finds otherwise.

**Required changes:**

1. Use cap 2 as a complexity safeguard, but remove claims of recovered Arrhenius, power-law, hinge, or non-monotonic SOC mechanisms.
2. Add the design-level identifiability and fold/seed stability audit for both A15 and G15, with abstention when the form is not identifiable.
3. Treat the M result as inconclusive under the tested optimization setup; do not claim it was fairly rejected or introduce it into the confirmatory model.
