# Gaps left by PIKAN + PINN4SOH that a JES paper could close

Basis: `notes/code/PIKAN.md` and `notes/code/PINN4SOH.md` (full code reading; PIKAN journal paper NOT read, PINN4SOH Supplement NOT read). Retired topics are not proposed: nothing below is about how the evaluation split changes accuracy, or about conformal / uncertainty calibration. Leakage-free cell-level evaluation (all hyper-parameters tuned on training cells only, 5+ seeds, paired comparisons) is our standard practice; where a gap needs a control from the evidence (e.g. a t-only baseline), that control is part of the method study, not a claimed contribution. Each gap states a test whose outcome could be negative. Numbers cited are from the repo files named; none comes from a run of ours.

1. **Residual along the trajectory instead of at fixed features.** Both codes penalise u_t = dF/dt at fixed x (`PIKAN/Model/PIKAN_opt.py:152-170`; `PINN4SOH/Model/Model.py:216-235`), but SOH moves with k through both t and the drifting features, so the physical rate is the total derivative u_t + u_x . dx/dk. The consecutive pairs needed for a finite-difference total derivative are already built (`PIKAN/dataloader/dataloader.py:77-83`) and unused. Test: replace L_PDE by a total-derivative residual (and variants with a physics-informed G form), compare with the original residual at identical training cells, seeds and budget; endpoint: test RMSE/MAPE and whether the learned G now matches the finite-difference dSOH/dk of the data. Could fail if G stays unconstrained.

2. **Cycle-unit time and a causal formulation (forecast, RUL).** Both repos scale time per cell by whole-life min-max (`dataloader.py:58-65`; PIKAN notebook `XJTU_Dataloader` cell 5 prints t running -1 to +1 over 371 cycles), so the method needs each test cell's end of life and gives rates per unit life fraction. Neither can forecast (PIKAN.md section 7). Test: train with t in physical cycles and features from a causal window only, integrate G forward from the last observed cycles, report error at fixed horizons and RUL error in cycles against PIKAN-style estimation re-implemented under the same inputs. Needs a dataset with long trajectories (see 8).

3. **Mechanistic G with a KAN correction, with identifiable parameters.** PIKAN's G is a free KAN, and the prior-guided symbolic G differs wildly by dataset: XJTU 0.3067(u+0.6144)^2-0.7876, HUST 0.5289(0.0146-u)^2-0.6422, MIT 9.9945(u-0.9476)^2-0.0092, TJU 0.4035(0.2592u-1)^2-0.3959; Verhulst C = -2.217 for XJTU (`Notebooks/PIKAN_*_Var2_PDEAutoDiscovery.ipynb` cells 28-30). Test: constrain G to a known family (power-law / sqrt-time SEI growth with Arrhenius factor, logistic, knee model) in physical cycle units and let a small KAN learn only the residual; endpoint: are the fitted physical parameters stable across cells of one protocol (variance across cells and seeds, interval overlap), and does accuracy match the free G? Fails if parameters are not identifiable.

4. **Symbolic extraction that survives rounding.** In the PIKAN notebooks the extracted formula is a dense sum of sinh/cosh/bessel/sinc terms with coefficients 1e-4..5e-3; rounding to 4 digits moves MAPE from 0.0042 to 0.1458 (XJTU direct), 0.0058 to 0.1013 (XJTU prior-guided), 0.0089 to 0.2152 (HUST), 0.0083 to 0.0725 (TJU) (`PIKAN_XJTU_Var2.ipynb` cell 29; `*_PDEAutoDiscovery.ipynb` cell 33). No l1/entropy regulariser is used in training (PIKAN.md section 2). Test: train with sparsity regularisation and a restricted symbolic library (low-degree polynomial, exp, power) and report symbolic-to-network MAPE ratio after rounding to 3 significant digits, together with formula length; success criterion fixed in advance (e.g. ratio below 1.1).

5. **KAN basis ablation at matched parameters and tuning.** PIKAN uses one KAN family (efficient-kan B-spline, grid 5, order 3, ~10 parameters per edge) and 75,960 + 57,000 parameters against 7,781 + 5,821 for the PINN (`PIKAN/README.md` table 7.3); its own medium model (6,120 + 8,840) is roughly as accurate (README 7.1). kan-review lists ready implementations: Chebyshev (ChebyKAN), Gaussian RBF (fast-kan), Fourier, wavelet (Wav-KAN), Jacobi, rational (`kan-review/README.md` section 6). Test: same data, same inner-loop tuning on training cells, parameter-matched MLP vs 4-5 KAN variants in the PINN framework; endpoint: paired RMSE differences with confidence intervals across seeds. May show the KAN advantage is a capacity or smoothness effect.

6. **Does the physics help beyond a KAN? (attribution with a pre-specified endpoint).** In the released group means, PIKAN with alpha = beta = 1e-3 is close to the data-only KAN: XJTU b1-b6 MAPE 0.0037, 0.0126, 0.0077, 0.0068, 0.0100, 0.0062 vs KAN 0.0041, 0.0126, 0.0081, 0.0064, 0.0157, 0.0062 (`processed_results/.../PIKAN_opt_XJTU_results_group_mean.xlsx`, README 7.1), and the single-run 2-input comparison is 0.003580 vs 0.003724 MAPE (`PIKAN_XJTU_Var2.ipynb` cell 8 vs `KAN_XJTU_Var2.ipynb` cell 8). The repo has no controlled ablation. Test: factorial ablation (no physics / residual only / monotone-type term only / both), alpha and beta chosen by inner CV on training cells, multiple cell-level folds so the number of test cells is not 2-3.

7. **Loss-weight selection without a test-based grid.** PIKAN trains 49 (alpha, beta) pairs per seed and keeps the best on test MAPE; the gap between best and median group is 0.0031 vs 0.0042 (XJTU b1) and 0.0057 vs 0.0083 (HUST) for PIKAN, but 0.0060 vs 0.0242 (XJTU b1) and 0.0078 vs 0.0202 (HUST) for the PINN, so the method's practical value depends on sensitivity to the weights (`processed_results/results_soh-estimation/*group_mean.xlsx`, my summary). Test: adaptive weighting (self-adaptive residual weights, gradient-norm balancing, uncertainty weighting; kan-review 7.1 lists ssRBA) with weights fixed from training data only; endpoint: median-over-seeds performance and spread without any search, compared with the best-of-grid inner-CV choice.

8. **A dataset outside the four PINN4SOH tables.** Everything in both repos uses the same 16 charge-window features on XJTU, TJU, HUST, MIT (`PIKAN/README.md` section 4). Shlok's list names NASA PCoE and CALCE (raw curves, capacity regeneration in NASA, different protocols). Test: extract features from raw curves under a documented rule, run the best method from 5-7; endpoint: does the gain over a data-only KAN and over the PINN survive, and are the regeneration spikes captured? Required for the cycle-unit forecasting in gap 2 as well. Licence and cell counts to be checked by the data steward.

9. **Label-free handling of capacity regeneration.** PIKAN's physics term multiplies the predicted step by the true label step (`PIKAN_opt.py:190`), so it needs labels and rewards matching regeneration noise; PINN4SOH's paper states a different (pure monotone) form (paper Eq. 8). Test: compare (a) none, (b) pure non-increasing penalty with a learned slack for regeneration events, (c) the PIKAN term, on a dataset with real regeneration (NASA/CALCE), with endpoint error on the cycles around regeneration events and monotonicity violation rate on test cells.

10. **Identifiability of G from synthetic ground truth.** The PIKAN paper (abstract in README) claims that the discovered equation has "structural similarity to existing electrochemical models", yet the repo contains no synthetic experiment where the generating law is known (not found in the code I read; the paper itself may). Test: simulate cells from known laws (Verhulst, power-law, knee) with realistic noise, feed the PINN4SOH/PIKAN/our method, and measure recovery error of the law's parameters and of the functional form (e.g. after symbolic extraction). Cheap on CPU. Also tests the PINN4SOH degeneracy (G fed u_t, `Model.py:232`) by an ablation with and without u_t in G's input. A negative result (G not identifiable from SOH data of this kind) would itself be reportable.

Notes for the lead
- Items 1, 3, 4, 10 give the method novelty; 5-7 give the strong-baseline and attribution evidence a JES reviewer will ask for; 2, 8, 9 widen the scope (forecasting, new data, regeneration).
- PIKAN is in the target journal (JES 153, 120860, 2026-04) and its paper is unread; before committing to 1-4 the novelty auditor should confirm the paper does not already do them (the repo says it does not, but the paper may contain extra experiments not in the repo).
