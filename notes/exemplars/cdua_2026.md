## Problem, claims
Forecast future capacity of EV batteries from real-vehicle charging data, with 95 % intervals. **Three numbered contributions** at the end of the Introduction (CDUA model; a framework for small-sample real-vehicle data, 20 vehicles; hybrid feature engineering), then a roadmap paragraph. Abstract carries the numbers (relative MAE 0.94 %, relative RMSE 1.14 %, 95 % interval relative width 3.74 %). Sections: 1 Introduction, 2 Data preparation and feature engineering (2.1 labelled capacity, 2.2 median filter, 2.3 hybrid feature selection), 3 Methodology (3.1 diffusion theory, 3.2 architecture, 3.3 modules, 3.4 training and inference), 4 Results and discussion (4.1 baselines, 4.2 **feature ablation**, 4.3 **model ablation**), 5 Conclusion. The Results section opens with a three-point plan ("first ... second ... third") that maps to 4.1-4.3.

## Evaluation (as stated)
20 vehicles, 5-fold cross-validation with **4 vehicles per test fold** (Fig. 8 text), input windows of 8, 16, 24 and 32 weeks (Table 2 rows), baselines LSTM and Seq2Seq. Metrics RMSE, MAE, interval width. No seeds statement found in the text I grepped; no significance test.

## Figures and tables (form from captions/text)
Main text: 9 figures, 5 tables.
- Fig. 1 (a)-(f): data pre-processing panels (current profile, capacity extraction, median-filter before/after).
- Fig. 2: two-panel feature importance, (a) Pearson correlation bars, (b) XGBoost importance ranking. Message: why those features.

- Fig. 7: **2x2 forecast panels** on one vehicle: (a) point forecast with 8-week input, (b) interval forecast with 8-week input, (c) point 16-week, (d) interval 16-week. Message: horizon/window effect with intervals.
- Fig. 8: per-fold results with four marker styles for the four test vehicles in a fold. Fig. 9: **box plots of absolute prediction errors** for the model ablation.
- Table 1 hyper-parameters; Table 2 point accuracy (RMSE, MAE per window length, per model); Table 3 95 % interval widths; Table 4 feature ablation; Table 5 model ablation.

## What a JES reviewer will have liked / what we should do better
Liked: explicit feature-ablation and model-ablation sections with their own tables; a hyper-parameter table; interval metric table. Weak: 20 units, 4-unit test folds, no paired test, no seeds in sight, no physical model. Our design (condition-level units, paired Wilcoxon, 5 seeds) is stronger and should be said in one sentence.

## Use for us
Result-section skeleton: baselines, then ablation by component, then ablation by input set, each with a table; a closing box plot of errors per ablation (form for our Table 2 companion). Forecast figure with two window lengths side by side (window length = our k check-ups primary vs k+2 secondary).
