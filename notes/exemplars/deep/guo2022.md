## (A) Contribution as stated; contribution-list pattern
- Stated aim: review how physics-based lifetime models and data-driven methods are combined; classify the combinations; show publication trends; compare the approaches; give future directions (Abstract; Sec. 1, p. 3).
- Pattern: **four-bullet contribution list in Sec. 1** (p. 3) followed by a roadmap paragraph: (1) three physics-based model families with flowcharts and an application-oriented requirements comparison; (2) combination approaches split into two main categories, each with flowcharts; (3) publication trends as line and pie charts plus a pros/cons comparison to help readers pick a method; (4) a future direction, with electrochemical-model PINN named as promising. The prior-work gap is stated first: five earlier reviews (Table 1: Andersson, Krewer, Liao, Finegan, Aykol) lean on electrochemical models and machine learning and give little attention to semi-empirical models and to the other data-driven families (Sec. 1, p. 2). This gap is useful to us.
- Framing move (Sec. 3 opening, p. 8): white-box physics models are expensive and hard to parametrise, black-box data-driven models depend on large training sets and only return what they were trained to predict, so grey-box hybrids are needed. One short phrase quoted: "Why we need 'grey box' lifetime modeling?" (Sec. 3 heading text).
- Abstract carries one number (machine learning is 78 % of data-driven methods) and one temporal claim (physics-guided data-driven started to emerge as a lifetime method after 2020). No Highlights block in the PDF I read (cannot say whether the web page has one).

## (E) Evaluation (a review has none of its own)
- No quantitative cross-method comparison; Table 5 is qualitative. No accuracy numbers are collated.
- No search protocol: databases, queries, date window (the past 20 years, Sec. 4.1), inclusion and exclusion criteria are not stated; the phrase selected papers in contribution 3 is undefined. Publication counts are for papers the authors chose. 2022 counts stop at 1 June (stated).
- The review does state, as a recurring caveat on reviewed hybrids, that they still need validation across more conditions, battery types, loads and temperatures (Sec. 3.2.2, p. 14; Sec. 3.2.3, p. 15). It calls for standardised test procedures that give a minimum test matrix and test cost (Sec. 4.3, p. 17) and for open-source multi-conditional data (Sec. 5). Those three statements are the field's own admission of the gap our paper tests (E2: the authors report them).

## (F) Novelty framing moves
1. Coin and define "grey box" for lifetime modelling and organise the field around it; the label became the citation hook (120 citations by 2026-11).
2. Two-level taxonomy with one flowchart per leaf, a uniform visual grammar, and a comparison table (Table 5).
3. Numbers in the Abstract that look like findings (78 %, after 2020).
4. A forward-looking proposal (electrochemical PINN) so the review reads as agenda-setting, not just summary.

## (G) Limitations (stated and unstated)
- Stated: online applicability and validation under more conditions are open; no separate limitations section, only a future-perspectives section (Sec. 4.3).
- Unstated: (i) no review protocol, so selection bias is uncontrolled; (ii) trend counts are tiny and read from a figure; (iii) category boundaries overlap (Eq. 6 UODE is filed under residual learning but is also an embedding); (iv) the Abstract says pie charts show different physical-model applications but Fig 10 has one pie, for data-driven methods; the Conclusions claim electrochemical models take more than 50 % of the physical part with no figure or table showing that share; (v) the Abstract places the surge after 2020 while the plot already shows 10 papers in 2020; (vi) the 78 % is the sum of the two machine-learning slices (54 % non-probabilistic + 24 % probabilistic), while Sec. 4.1 stresses non-probabilistic ML; (vii) data availability statement is the platform default (the authors say they are unable or chose not to specify the data); (viii) the unresolved cross-reference noted in (B).

## Taxonomy of grey-box approaches (Guo Sec. 3) and where our model sits
- **A. Data-driven assisted physical models** (physics leads): A1 parameter identification (Fig 4); A2 reduced-order physical model, including ML-identified ROMs from a descriptor library (Fig 5b; Gasper et al. [128] use symbolic regression and LASSO over Arrhenius and Tafel descriptors for calendar fade, with long-term extrapolation checks on unused data, Sec. 3.1.2, p. 10); A3 uncertainty quantification (Fig 6).
- **B. Physics-guided data-driven** (data leads): B1 physics-based data generation (Fig 7); B2 physics-based residual learning, y = physics + NN correction, including the universal-ODE form dY/dt = mechanism + NN(mu, X, theta) (Eq. 6, Sec. 3.2.2); B3 physics-based embedding, either (a) physics output fed to ML or (b) ML replaces part of the physics model with the physics acting as constraint (Fig 9; Sec. 3.2.3 cites parameter-as-network-output examples such as a physics-constrained DNN whose network supplies model parameters as functions of operating conditions).
- **Our model**: a semi-empirical calendar-plus-cycle ODE (power-of-time and Arrhenius structure, Guo Fig 3) with KAN-parametrised stress functions and a physics-residual training loss. This is **B3(b) physics-based embedding**, with B2 (Eq. 6 UODE) as the nearest neighbour if the KAN acts as an additive correction. The extracted closed-form laws are an **A2-type symbolic reduction** (Gasper et al. is the nearest prior). The comparison to a GAM is not in Guo's taxonomy.
- Guo's own PINN outlook is electrochemical-PDE-centred (Sec. 4.3); the semi-empirical ODE route is the part the review says was under-covered (Sec. 1). We can claim that gap with a Guo citation, provided we say it as "Guo et al. note that semi-empirical models received little attention in earlier hybrid reviews" and not as a statement that no work exists.

## (I) Hostile-reviewer attacks on this paper (so we avoid them)
1. No review protocol, no selection criteria: any trend figure can be an artefact of selection.
2. Percentages with no n; the denominator for 78 % and the above 50 % is never defined.
3. Abstract claims not matched by displayed figures (pie physical-model applications; post-2020 timing).
4. Categories overlap and no decision rule is given for assigning a paper to a category.
5. No accuracy comparison, so high predictive accuracy for residual learning and embedding is repeated from the sources.
6. Emphasis on electrochemical models underplays semi-empirical ageing models used in industry.
7. Typesetting error (unresolved reference).

7. **[TEXT]** Include a limitations paragraph; Guo has no limitations section and reviewers can say so. Our BLUEPRINT already plans one.
8. **[FIG]** For any trend or coverage plot: print n, use a 2-D chart, never a 3-D pie.
