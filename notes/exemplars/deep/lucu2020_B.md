## (A) Contribution as the authors state it
A generic Gaussian-process cycle-ageing model for capacity loss across a broad range of cycling conditions (temperature, depth of discharge, middle-SOC, charge and discharge C-rate), showing that it learns from new conditions and from dynamic profiles, and quantifying the minimal number of laboratory tests (Abstract; Sect. 1).
Wording pattern, Sect. 1: three roman-numeral main contributions (the generic cycle model; the extension and validation of the first paper's claims including learning from new data and a compositional covariance; implicitly the dynamic-profile validation) followed by "this study also extends the secondary contributions presented in the first paper" with three items (minimal number of laboratory tests; validation on 122 static cells plus 2 dynamic; sensitivity analysis of capacity loss to stress factors). Same two-tier pattern as Part A; the series frame ("In a series of two papers", abstract) is the novelty device. Quote (once): "learning from real operation data" (title).

## (B) Structure
Section list (same skeleton as Part A, deliberately): 1 Introduction; 2 Experimental cycle ageing data (2.1 static, 2.2 dynamic); 3 Data preprocessing and evaluation of the resulting data; 4 Gaussian process theory; 5 Development of the cycle ageing model (5.1 assumptions and input selection, 5.2 kernel construction, 5.2.1 individual components, 5.2.2 whole kernel); 6 Learning from static operating conditions (6.1 metrics, 6.2 training case studies, 6.3 prediction results: accuracy, confidence, sensitivity); 7 Learning from dynamic operating conditions; 8 Discussion, limitation of the study and further works; 9 Conclusions; Appendices A (cycle test matrix), B (raw data, variability), C (results per training case).

## (E) Evaluation protocol
- Data (Sect. 2): 20 Ah NMC (4:4:2)/graphite pouch cell; 124 cells; 122 at 34 static cycling conditions, at least 3 cells per condition; check-ups every 4,000 Ah (about 100 full equivalent cycles); 2 dynamic cells (#124, #125). Ranges (Sect. 9): 25-45 C, 20-100 % DoD, 20-80 % middle-SOC, C/3-2C charge and discharge. No data below 25 C or above 2C (the authors say so in Sects. 3 and 8).
- Task: iterative prediction of dQ increments (Ah-throughput step) accumulated to Q.
- Splits: 16 nested training cases built in a fixed rule-based order: temperature (Cases 1-2), DoD (3-7), middle-SOC (8-11), charge C-rate (12-14), discharge C-rate (15-16). Four error groups per case: training cells, validation cells (not in the case), "targeted validation cells" (validation cells that differ from training only in the factor being learned, other factors seen), all cells. This targeted subset is the cleanest definition of "held-out factor level" in the literature we hold.
- Headline numbers: Case 4 (26 cells at 9 cycling conditions) is the adequate trade-off; its validation MAE_Q is 1.02 % (MAE_dQ 0.55 %), training 1.12 % (0.68 %); CS_dQ / CS_Q 94.02 / 82.01 % (validation), 90.65 / 77.75 % (training); at convergence the pooled CS is about 75 % for Q and about 90 % for dQ (Sect. 6.3.2, 8). Conclusions: 0.58 % MAE_dQ and 1.04 % MAE_Q over 122 cells. Static 100 % DoD cells age faster than the same DoD inside a dynamic profile (Sect. 8, with a literature pointer): a path-dependence flag.
- Metrics: six metrics (MAE and RMSE of dQ and of Q, two CS values); acceptance threshold 2 % MAE_Q.
- Baselines: none. Statistics: none (no seeds, no repeated orderings, no CI, no test).
- Editorial slip: Sect. 6.1 still says "the two ageing models", carried over from a version that compared two models.

## (F) Novelty framing
Series framing ("in a series of two papers") plus a gap statement taken from the authors' earlier review: underuse of stress-factor inputs and insufficient validation. The Part B-specific claim: the findings in Part A "corroborate" in cycling (Conclusions): nonparametric models learn progressively; isotropic kernel components suit stress factors within the recommended window. Calendar-cycle integration is explicitly deferred ("subject of ongoing research", Sect. 1, 8, 9): that open door is exactly the space our paper steps into, with a physics-based law class and held-out conditions.

## (G) Limitations and negative results
Stated: no cold or high-C-rate data; Case 4 model insensitive to middle-SOC, charge and discharge C-rate, so extra tests would be needed for fast-charging applications (Sect. 8); GP complexity O(n^3) time and O(n^2) memory and the local-vs-cloud deployment trade-off; need for algorithms that convert logged time series into stress-factor profiles and for a state-of-health estimator to supply targets (Sect. 8); static vs dynamic DoD discrepancy "should be verified in further work"; Phase 3 (knee) excluded. Negative or awkward results shown: validation CS_Q is about 25-35 % for Cases 1-2 and about 45 % at Case 12 (Fig. 4b, image); the Case 12 error spike and its explanation (the 2C condition appears alone and the model over-estimates intermediate rates, fixed at Case 13); overestimated fade of cell #124. Not stated: fixed ladder order; unbalanced design; raw replicate variance up to 74 %^2 behind a defective cell.

## (I) What a hostile reviewer would attack
1. One fixed ordering of 16 cases and hand-selected "targeted validation" subsets; the "26 cells is enough" claim is not shown to be stable.
2. The accuracy figures are means over heterogeneous cells with no interval, test or baseline; GP vs nothing.
3. Calibration is poor (CS_Q about 75 %) while the paper sells growing confidence.
4. Cell counts shift between abstract (124), Sect. 2.1 (122 static, 34 conditions) and Sect. 9 (122 cells after one was discarded).
5. High-C-rate and cold conditions absent, yet the title and abstract speak of "a broad range" of cycling conditions.
6. Static-to-dynamic transfer shown on 2 cells with updates chosen after seeing the profile.
7. Preprocessing drops the initial rise and the knee, using whole-trajectory knowledge, so deployment claims overreach.

## (J) Lessons for our paper
1. [FIG] Use Fig. 4's grammar for our main learning-curve figure and fix its weak points: show a band over random ladder orderings, put m on the x axis and k as line colour or as panel columns, keep a separate row for "targeted held-out conditions" (a held-out level of one factor with the others seen), and draw the replicate noise floor as a horizontal line. A second figure can be a heat map over (m, k) of held-out MAE with the contour at our pre-declared threshold.
2. [FIG] Reuse Fig. 5's three-colour role code (blue train, red validate, green demonstration cell) as small multiples; show the same held-out condition at four rungs of m, and add the extracted closed-form law as a thin dashed overlay beside the network forecast.
3. [FIG] Reuse Fig. 2's "factor dependency" banners for the data overview: one panel per factor, replicate curves visible, colour = level. Use a colour-blind-safe palette; mark which level is held out for the test.
4. [FIG] Extend the idea of Fig. 6: draw the learned stress laws beyond the training range, with the data-supported range shaded, to show where the law is trusted. Their sigma goes to about 2.6 % at 5 C charging although data stop at 2 C; ours should show that visibly.
5. [TAB] Put a ladder table (conditions added, cells, share of data, check-ups) next to the replicate-variance table with footnotes naming the cause of each large variance (Table B1). Both defeat "what did you remove" questions.
6. [TEXT] Write the adequacy rule before the results: the rung at which held-out error first falls below a threshold stated relative to the replicate noise floor. Report it in the abstract as "N cells and M conditions", as they do (26 cells at 9 conditions).
7. [TEXT] Cite both parts for the learning-curve concept so that our novelty is not "learning curve" but the closed-form physics-structured law, joint calendar and cycling, the second axis (k check-ups), and paired condition-level tests with multiplicity control. Say that no baseline or statistical test is reported in either part.
8. [TEXT] Say plainly which stress axes our held-out conditions do not cover (their Sect. 8 does this for cold and fast-charge data) in one limitations paragraph.
9. [ANALYSIS-EXPLORATORY] Their discrepancy between static and dynamic exposure suggests a path-dependence probe: if any cells change condition between check-ups, test whether the additive calendar + cycle law mispredicts them; if none do, state that path dependence is outside the matrix. Mark exploratory.
10. [ANALYSIS-EXPLORATORY] A "which factor entered at which rung" error decomposition (their targeted validation, Fig. 4c) on our ladder, reported per factor with paired contrasts; exploratory unless the rung order is pre-registered.
