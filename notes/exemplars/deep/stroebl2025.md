## (A) Contribution as the authors state it
The study uses data from a published large multi-stage ageing campaign [27] to test, for the first time on real cells, whether a parameter-individual optimal experimental design (pi-OED) lowers parameter uncertainty of a semi-empirical calendar and cycle ageing model compared with non-model-based designs (full factorial, conditioned Latin hypercube) at equal experimental effort (Sect. 1, last paragraph; Sect. 2.4).
Contribution-list wording pattern (Sect. 1, five bullets): each bullet is a bold-style noun-phrase label followed by a colon and one sentence: "Quantification of OED gain", "Multi-Stage Experimental Framework", "Parameter-Individual OED (pi-OED)", "Validation with Real-World Data", "Critical Assessment of OED Limitations". Two bullets carry the words "for the first time". The last bullet is a limitations bullet that promises "a balanced assessment" (my paraphrase of Sect. 1). Quote (once): the framing sentence "A balanced assessment ... highlights admissibility and limits of the OED approach" (Sect. 1, bullet 5, ellipsis mine).

## (E) Evaluation protocol
- Task: parameter-uncertainty reduction in a fixed parametric model, not forecasting. No held-out conditions, no predictions, no accuracy comparison across model families. Primary metric: bootstrap standard deviation of each parameter estimator, compared across Case 2 and Case 3 (Eq. 16); secondary: Fisher-information (Cramer-Rao) standard deviation.
- Data (Sect. 3, Sect. 3.1, 3.2): calendar 1,876 points, 48 excluded (2.56 %); S1 calendar 11 of 15 T-SoC combinations with 3 repetitions (33 channels), T = 10, 23, 45 C, SoC = 5, 20, 50, 80, 100 %, final SoC range 5-80 %; cycle S1 25 cLH points x 3 replicates (75 channels), 959 points with 44 outliers (4.59 %), S2a 467 points with 1 outlier, S2b 450 points with 3 outliers; S2b uses 12 pi-OED test points; factor ranges T 23-45 C, SOC_max 0.2-0.8, C_ch 0.05-1.0, C_dch 0.05-2.0, DoD 0.2-0.6.
- Units of analysis: one realisation of S2a and one of S2b per model (n = 1 study replicate per arm). Bootstrap: blockwise, n_BS = 10,000 (Sect. 3.1); block length not stated in the capture.
- Baselines: FF (calendar) and cLH (cycle) at equal number of trials; no random-design baseline.
- Statistics: Shapiro-Wilk at alpha = 0.05 per check-up (Figs. E.12, F.13); Mann-Whitney U and Spearman for the LOCO residual test (Table 1). No multiplicity correction; no effect-size interval on the 28 %.
- Validity rule: Eq. 16, sigma_BS(Case 3) < sigma_BS(Case 2), a strict inequality on point estimates.

## (F) Novelty framing
Moves: (1) names a gap in two steps, no prior real-data validation of OED for ageing "on a comprehensive level" and no study of both calendar and cycle (Sect. 1, para 4-5); (2) cites simulation studies as existing evidence and says experimental validation is "pending"; (3) recycles their own earlier multi-stage framework [26] and the published dataset [27] as the base, so novelty is the evaluation, not the data; (4) three "first" claims (pi-OED; real-data validation; calendar and cycle together); (5) pre-empts scope critiques in one sentence (constant-current profiles, predefined semi-heuristic model, path dependence and pack inhomogeneity left out "in favour of clarity and stringency"). The paper never claims a better model; it claims a better experimental practice and a diagnostic.

## (G) Limitations and negative results
Stated: scope (item 5 above); SoC range cut after seeing non-monotone behaviour (Sect. 3.1: "a deliberate decision not to change the model"); non-converged estimators and 4-sigma non-overlap between S1 and the later cases (Sect. 4.1); cycle model inadequate and errors non-normal (Sect. 4.2); thresholds for applicability "remain the subject of future research" (Sect. 5).
How the negative result is handled: it is in the highlights, gets its own results subsection, has the same figure size as the positive result (Fig. 7 vs Fig. 5), is explained by three testable hypotheses (R3 start values too far, R1 model validity via R2_adj and noise floor, low sensitivity of the target parameters via leave-one-covariate-out and Mann-Whitney), and ends as a two-part recommendation (check validity after the first stage; optimise only parameters that are both uncertain and significant). Unstated: single study replicate; no quantification of how often a 28 % gain would arise by chance (the virtual experiment suggests large variability of sigma_BS across designs).

## (I) What a hostile reviewer would attack
1. n = 1 per arm: the 28 % has no interval, and Case 1 vs Case 2 distributions differ by more than 4 sigma while sharing data, so sigma_BS is not a trustworthy yardstick (their own Fig. 8).
2. Confounding of design and information: S2a fills a factorial grid, S2b repeats a single condition (Fig. B.9, Fig. 3c), so "equal effort" is equal run count, not equal coverage; an equally cheap replicate-only or space-filling alternative is absent.
3. Post hoc data exclusion: SoC 100 % removed to satisfy the monotone-SoC assumption R1 after seeing the data.
4. LOCO test logic: p > 0.05 and rho_s > 0.995 between two nested models' residuals is expected when the dropped terms are weak; no equivalence test, no paired or grouped test (residuals within a cell are dependent).
5. Single chemistry, single model structure; the conclusion "model inadequacy" rests on R2_adj < 0.95 from a rule quoted from another paper.
6. Internal slips: "approximately 17-fold" (38.03 / 2.099 is about 18.1); "S1 + S2b non-OED" in Sect. 4.2 should be S2a; duplicated conclusion paragraphs; Fig. B.9 legend lists an unused "Manual" sampling.
7. Short horizons: calendar curves end near 340 days and cycle curves near 4,600 FEC (Fig. 3, read from image), far from end of life.

## (J) Lessons for our paper
1. [FIG] Build the design-map figure as a scatter matrix with shaded physical-constraint regions (their Fig. B.10), but put it in the body, mark the held-out stress conditions with a box or open marker, and show replicate multiplicity. They hid theirs in an appendix.
2. [TEXT] Mirror the method flowchart (their Fig. 2) in run-in headings so every case study reads the same way. Ours: Define the stress-law class, Design the ladder over (m, k), Fit, Predict held-out, Test. One box per pre-registered step.
3. [TEXT] Write the contribution list as 4-5 labelled bullets including one "Critical assessment / limits" bullet, and make 2 of 5 highlights falsifiable boundary statements (when it fails, what rule transfers). Use the last highlight for a transferable principle.
4. [TAB] Include a model-adequacy table next to the noise floor: R2_adj, RMSE, MAE of the full and reduced model with measurement noise sigma_exp stated (their Table 1). Ours: forecast MAE/RMSE per arm beside the replicate scatter from the triplicate cells, so reviewers see when errors sit at the noise floor.
5. [ANALYSIS-EXPLORATORY] This paper reports that dropping C_ch and C_dch does not change the fit on this same cell, so a hostile reviewer will expect us to show that our learned stress laws capture C-rate dependence that the heuristic form could not, or to say that they do not. Run a leave-one-stress-factor-out arm (T, SoC window, DoD, C_ch, C_dch), scored on held-out conditions with the same paired condition-level test; label it exploratory unless it is pre-registered. Never call a C-rate effect real without the paired contrast.
6. [FIG] Add a known-truth synthetic recovery panel (their Fig. 8 idea: histograms with the true value as a dashed line) and report the fraction of repeats in which the estimate misses, not only the worst case. This answers the identifiability attack on extracted closed-form laws.
7. [FIG] Do not clip bars silently: if an off-scale value must be shown, use their arrow-with-value device or a log axis, and add intervals. Forest plot of paired differences remains our primary-endpoint figure; theirs is the form to beat.
8. [TEXT] State assumptions as numbered remarks (R1 correct model, R2 homoscedastic normal noise, R3 start values near truth) and test each in the results (Shapiro-Wilk per check-up, appendix figures). Ours: A1-A3 for the stress-law extraction, with a diagnostic per assumption in an appendix figure.
9. [TEXT] Keep the body near 6,000 words and 8-10 display items; move per-check-up diagnostics, parameter tables and normality checks to appendix items (their appendices A-G hold 5 figures and 2 tables).
10. [ANALYSIS-EXPLORATORY] Report data removed (their 48/1,876, 44/959, 1/467, 3/450 outliers with percentages) in a single table for our own cells and check-ups, with the rule that decided each exclusion made before the test run.
