## Problem and stated contribution
Proposes KANs as an alternative to MLPs on small-scale "AI + Science" tasks: learnable univariate functions (B-splines) on edges, sums on nodes, generalised from the 2-layer width-(2n+1) Kolmogorov-Arnold form to arbitrary width and depth (Eq. 2.1-2.8, p.4-6). Claims: (1) accuracy: smaller KANs match or beat larger MLPs; faster neural scaling (alpha = k+1 = 4 for cubic splines) (p.8, Fig 3.1); (2) interpretability: sparsification, pruning, symbolic snapping, with two worked scientific examples (knot invariants, Anderson localisation) (Section 4); (3) continual learning without catastrophic forgetting (toy, Section 3.5). They also state that KANs are about 10x slower than MLPs (p.33).

## Data
This is a methods paper; data are synthetic or from earlier work.
- Five toy functions with known KA representation (Section 3.1, p.14): Bessel J0(20x); exp(sin(pi x) + y^2); xy; a 100-dimensional exp of mean of sin^2; a 4-dimensional composite.
- 15 special functions from scipy.special (Table 1, p.16).
- Feynman dataset, the no-units variants with at least two variables (Table 2, p.16-17); inputs drawn uniformly, noise-free.
- Poisson PDE on [-1,1]^2 with known solution sin(pi x) sin(pi y^2) (Section 3.4, p.18); n_i = 10000 interior and n_b = 800 boundary points.
- Knot data of Davies et al. (ref 45): 17 invariants, signature as 14-class label (Section 4.3); Anderson localisation models (MM, GAAM, MAAM), eigenstates from numerics, fractal dimension as label (Section 4.4).
- No real measured noisy data and no noise level study anywhere in the paper.

## Model
- KAN layer = matrix of 1-D functions phi_(l,j,i); network = composition (Eq. 2.2-2.7, p.5). Shape written [n0, n1, ..., nL].
- Each edge: phi(x) = w_b silu(x) + w_s spline(x), spline = sum_i c_i B_i(x), cubic (k = 3) by default, grid with G intervals (Eq. 2.10-2.12, p.6). Initialisation: w_s = 1, spline approx 0 via c_i ~ N(0, sigma^2) with sigma = 0.1 (footnote 2, p.6); w_b Xavier. Grids updated on the fly according to the activation range (footnote 3, p.6) because splines live on bounded intervals.
- Parameters O(N^2 L (G + k)) versus O(N^2 L) for an MLP (p.7).
- Grid extension (Section 2.4, p.9-10): fit a finer spline to the coarse one by least squares over the input distribution (Eq. 2.16); in the experiments G = 3, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200 (up to 1000 in the toy figure), increased every 200 LBFGS steps, 1800 steps total (p.9, p.14). Test loss is U-shaped in G; conjectured optimum at the interpolation threshold (parameters of a [2,5,1] KAN = 15 G vs 1000 samples, G about 67, observed about 50) (p.10).
- Approximation theory (Theorem 2.1, p.7): if f has a representation with (k+1)-times differentiable univariate functions, there are B-splines with ||f - KAN_G||_(C^m) <= C G^(-k-1+m); C depends on f and on the representation, and the dependence on dimension is left as future work (p.8). Empirically [2,1,1] KAN scales as G^-3 on RMSE, G^-4 only when taking the median squared error (p.10).
- Physics-informed variant used only in Section 3.4 (Poisson PINN with KAN in place of the MLP).
- Simplification (Section 2.5.1, p.11-12): sparsification loss L = L_pred + lambda (mu1 sum_l |Phi_l|_1 + mu2 sum_l S(Phi_l)), mu1 = mu2 = 1 (Eq. 2.17-2.20), where |phi|_1 is the mean absolute activation over the Np inputs and S is the entropy of the normalised edge magnitudes; visualisation with transparency tanh(beta A) (beta = 3); node-level pruning with incoming/outgoing scores and threshold theta = 1e-2 (Eq. 2.21); symbolification fix_symbolic(l,i,j,f) fits y approx c f(a x + b) + d by an iterative grid search over (a, b) and linear regression for (c, d) (p.12); suggest_symbolic and auto_symbolic for libraries (Appendix A, Table 6).
- Training: LBFGS (vanilla); the authors note they did not use Adam-LBFGS switching or boosting (p.14).

## Tuning
Architecture shapes and sparsity strengths were swept (MLP width 5 or 100 with depth 2-6; KAN width 5, depth 2-6, pruned with lambda = 1e-2 or 1e-3; 3 seeds per setting, p.15, p.17). Reported numbers for Tables 1 and 2 are the "best model over random seeds and depths" with the "lowest test RMSE" (p.17), i.e. selected on the test loss; the Pareto frontiers (Fig 3.2, D.1) are drawn on training and test curves of the same runs. There is no validation set in these experiments. Appendix C (p.44-45) lists dependence on hyperparameters for interpretability: entropy penalty is needed (without it duplicates), results depend on seeds ("with some unlucky seed, the pruned network could be larger than needed"), lambda controls sparsity, a small G encourages "ensembling" that hides the structure.

## Evaluation
- Splits: random train/test on noise-free synthetic data; knot data as in ref 45 (train/test accuracy). No cell-wise or group-wise splits (not applicable).
- Metrics: test RMSE versus parameter count (Pareto frontier); classification accuracy for knots and mobility edges.
- Seeds: 3 per hyperparameter setting for Tables 1-2 (p.15, p.17); 100 seeds x 2 lambdas for the unsupervised knot clusters (p.25: 200 networks grouped into three clusters).
- Headline numbers (with locations): Fig 3.1 (p.14) KANs close to alpha = 4 while MLPs plateau; Table 1 (p.16) lists minimal and best KAN test RMSE next to the MLP value for the 15 special functions (the columns are partly garbled in my text extraction, so I did not count wins; the text states KANs are consistently better, p.15); on Feynman equations "MLPs and KANs behave comparably on average" (p.17); Poisson PDE: width-10 2-layer KAN reaches 1e-7 MSE against 1e-5 for a 4-layer width-100 MLP with about 1e2 vs 1e4 parameters (p.18), but the authors add that a symbolic ground truth is an unfair test for MLPs and that their KAN implementation is "typically 10x slower" (p.18). Knot signature (Table 3 p.23): KAN [17,1,14] 81.6 % accuracy with about 200 parameters against 78.0 % for the 4-layer width-300 MLP of ref 45 with about 3e5 parameters; the caption adds that an MLP with 60 parameters reached about 80 % (public, unpublished result, Table 3 caption). Symbolic formulas (Table 4 p.24): formula A (human, DeepMind) 83.1 %; auto-discovered B 62.6 %, C 71.9 %, D 84.0 %, E 82.8 %, F 77.8 %. Mobility edges (Table 5 p.28): GAAM theory 99.2 %, KAN auto 99.0 %; MAAM theory 98.6 %, KAN auto 97.1 %, with manual steps 97.7 %, 97.7 %, 96.6 % (step 4A) and 95.4 % (step 4B).
- Statistical tests: none. Spread over seeds: not shown in the tables.

## Ablations
- Learnable activations without edge placement (LAN) versus KAN (Appendix B, p.42-44): LANs are less interpretable; weight matrices leave too many degrees of freedom; LAN improves SIREN image fitting by PSNR (single image setting).
- Effects of entropy penalty, seed, lambda, G and spline order k on interpretability on the exp(sin(pi x) + y^2) toy (Appendix C, Fig C.1).
- Grid size versus accuracy (Fig 2.3), KAN depth 2 vs 3 on the 4-D example (p.14), human-constructed vs pruned KAN shapes (Table 2).
- Not done: noise, small-data regimes with real measurements, KAN versus MLP at equal training time, comparison with symbolic regression tools on identical problems (only discussed in prose, p.13), tuning of MLP training (Adam, schedules).

## Hidden assumptions
1. A smooth Kolmogorov-Arnold representation of the target exists (p.7-8, p.14); the theory gives a rate only then, with a constant that depends on the representation.
2. Noise-free targets; the interpretability workflow (prune, snap, retrain affine parameters until "machine precision", p.13) is demonstrated where the loss can drop to machine precision, i.e. a noise-free symbolic target.
3. A human or a library (suggest_symbolic) can identify the right symbolic form; the symbolification step needs the activation to be (nearly) a known function after affine rescaling.
4. Inputs lie in the grid range; the grid is updated on the fly and boundary effects slow the scaling (p.10).
5. Selection by lowest test loss over seeds and depths for the reported numbers.
6. Sparsity (lambda) and pruning threshold encode what "simple" means; different scientists may differ (p.32).

## Weaknesses a reviewer would hit
- All accuracy claims are on noise-free synthetic functions or small curated problems; Feynman results are "comparable" with MLPs (p.17); special-function advantage is clearer; the authors concede the symbolic-ground-truth PDE comparison favours KANs (p.18).
- MLP baselines are trained with vanilla LBFGS and fixed width 5 or 100 (p.14-15), without Adam, schedules or tuning; the authors say so.
- Reporting is the best of seeds and depths on the test loss (p.17), no validation split, no spread; the Pareto frontier is an upper envelope of many runs.
- Interpretability is not robust to seeds and settings (Appendix C; unsupervised discovery needs different seeds to find different relations, p.22; 200 networks fall into three clusters, p.25). Different symbolic formulas B-F attain different accuracies for the same signature problem (Table 4), i.e. the formula is not unique.
- The knot comparison: the KAN found a different dominant variable (mu_r) from DeepMind's (mu_i), and an MLP with 60 parameters reached about 80 % (Table 3 caption p.23), weakening the parameter-efficiency claim.
- Scaling alpha = 4 is not met (G^-3 measured, G^-4 only on the median, p.10); at G = 1000 optimisation fails; and a sudden loss increase at large grid size on uniform training grids is unexplained (Appendix E).
- Training is "10x slower" than MLPs (p.18, p.33).
- Continual-learning claim rests on one 1-D Gaussian-peak toy (p.19) and the authors say it may not generalise.
- Nothing about identifiability: the paper never tests whether a learned univariate function equals the generating function when data are noisy or when several decompositions fit.

## Gaps and hooks for our paper
Procedure and limits that matter for a physics-informed KAN that learns stress-dependent degradation laws:
- Parametrisation to adopt and report: cubic B-spline (k = 3), grid G, SiLU residual branch, grid update, grid extension schedule G = 3 to 20-50 (my inference from the interpolation-threshold argument on p.10: with a few hundred cycles per cell the number of spline coefficients per edge must stay far below the number of samples, so G must stay small).
- Compositional laws are KA-friendly: x/y = exp(log x - log y) is learned with log and exp edges (p.20, Fig 4.1b). An Arrhenius-power-law rate, rate = A exp(-Ea/(R T)) I^n f(SOH), is a sum in log space, i.e. a [stress, 1, 1] KAN with exp on the output edge. Test: does a [T, I, DOD, SOH] KAN with sparsity recover exactly this structure on simulated cells?
- Symbolic step needs a noise-free target and a library. Our data are noisy; test: how large an SOH noise level breaks the pruning / snapping (recovered structure and parameters versus noise), using the Appendix C factors: lambda, entropy, seeds, G.
- Seed- and lambda-stability of the pruned architecture (p.22, Appendix C): report how often the same edges survive across seeds and across cell folds as an identifiability measure; this is not reported by Liu et al., and for them "unlucky seed" is just a remark.
- Non-uniqueness: Table 4 shows several formulas with different accuracy; our paper should report the Pareto frontier of formulas (length vs error) and a confidence band across seeds, not a single formula.
- "When KAN beats MLP" per the paper: low-dimensional smooth or oscillatory functions, compositional structure, small data, interpretability needed (Fig 6.1); when it does not: Feynman-type simple monotone dependencies ("comparable", p.17), speed-critical or large-scale (Fig 6.1; p.33). Battery degradation rate laws are low-dimensional, smooth, monotone in stress; that places us in the Feynman regime where accuracy gains are not expected, so the contribution must be interpretability and identifiability, not RMSE.
- Equal-parameter and equal-tuning MLP baseline with Adam and validation-based early stopping (they use none).
- PINN usage: the KAN-PINN example (Poisson, Section 3.4) uses weights lambda = 0.01 between interior and boundary loss and a known solution; our residual loss is on a learned law, so no analytic reference; hence the need for synthetic-law recovery tests (GAPS.md item 10).
