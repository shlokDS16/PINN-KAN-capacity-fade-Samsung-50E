## Problem and stated contribution
SOH estimation that works across chemistries and charge/discharge protocols. Claims: (1) a PINN of "B2" type (Aykol et al. classification) where the degradation dynamics du/dt = g(t, x, u, ...) is carried by a neural network G, constrained through a PDE-residual loss; (2) a generic feature extraction from the last 0.2 V of the CC charge and the CV tail; (3) a new XJTU dataset of 55 NCM cells; (4) validation on 387 cells and 310,705 samples, MAPE 0.87 % (abstract p.1); (5) better than MLP and CNN in regular, small-sample (1-4 training cells) and transfer experiments (Tables 2-4).

## Data
- Four sets (Table 1, p.4): XJTU (new; 55 NCM LiNi0.5Co0.2Mn0.3O2, 2000 mAh, room temperature, six protocols, batches 1-6, 8 cells per batch except batch 2 with 15); TJU (Zhu et al.: NCA 3500 mAh, NCM 3500 mAh, NCM+NCA 2500 mAh; 25/35/45 C; the table text lists 66, 55 and 9 cells per batch so 130 cells); MIT (Severson et al.: LFP 1100 mAh, 30 C, 125 cells); HUST (Ye et al.: LFP 1100 mAh, 30 C, 77 cells).
- Inputs: 16 features plus cycle number t (Methods "SOH estimation", p.5): mean, std, kurtosis, skewness, charge time, accumulated charge, slope, entropy of the voltage (window V_end-0.2 V to V_end) and of the current (CV phase, 0.5 A to 0.1 A) (p.5, Fig 3a). Min-max scaling to [-1, 1] (p.5). Per the code note (section 3) the scaling is per cell with whole-life min and max, including t, and a 3-sigma filter is applied; the paper does not say so.
- Label: SOH_k = Q_k / Q_0 with Q_0 nominal capacity (Eq. 5, p.10).
- Cycles: all cycles of each cell are used as samples (310,705 samples).
- Split: training / validation / test batteries about 6:2:2, test batteries "evenly distributed" (p.7); numbers in Tables S2-S6 (not read). The code note reports that the validation set is a random 20 % of consecutive-cycle pairs of the training cells, not separate batteries.
- Leakage stance (Discussion p.9): they cite Geslin et al. and state that training on cell A and testing on cell B avoids leakage, "instead of ... early data from battery A and testing it with later data".

## Model
- Two networks (Fig 1c p.3, Fig 6 p.8): solution network F(t, x; theta) giving u = SOH, and a dynamics network G(t, x, u, u_t, u_x; theta') giving the SOH decay rate. Residual H := F_t - G(...) (Eq. 4). Sizes: in the code F is a 17-60-60-32 sine-activated MLP plus a 32-32-1 head with dropout 0.2, G a 3-hidden-layer width-60 MLP with 35 inputs (code note section 1); the paper text says "small fully connected networks" and refers to Supplementary Note 3 (not read).
- Physics prior (how enforced): loss terms. L = L_data + alpha L_PDE + beta L_mono (Eq. 6-9, p.10): L_PDE = sum of squared residuals H; L_mono = sum of ReLU(u_hat_(k+1) - u_hat_k), "unless capacity regeneration occurs" (p.10). Weights alpha, beta are "trade-off parameters"; values in the Supplement. No architectural constraint; no parametric degradation law; the old empirical laws (linear, exponential, power-law, failure forecast model) are named as special cases of Eq. 2 when only time enters (p.9) but none is used. Higher-order derivatives dropped (p.10).
- Per the code note (section 1 and 6.1): G receives u_t as an input, so L_PDE can be zero by copying u_t; code monotonicity term differs from Eq. 8 (uses true label step and a sum).
- KAN: none (see `PIKAN.md` for the KAN fork).
- Training: Adam, warm-up then cosine, up to 200 epochs, batch 256/512, early stopping patience 20, no seed set (code note section 4); not in the paper main text.

## Tuning
Not described in the main text (Supplement). Code note: alpha, beta and learning rates are set by hand per dataset (XJTU 0.7/0.2, HUST 0.6/0.1, MIT 0.5/0.01, TJU 0.1/0.1; small-sample XJTU 0.5/10); a `grid_search()` function exists with the criterion not visible; a post-publication `fix.md` says an earlier code version had bugs and the supplementary alpha, beta no longer match the code. Test metrics are computed at each validation improvement (logging only). Treat tuning as not independent of the test cells.

## Ablations
None in the main text: no run without L_PDE, without L_mono, with different alpha / beta, with the other network sizes, or with G unfrozen in the transfer. All of these may be in the Supplement (not read). Only comparison: same-size MLP and CNN (no physics) and the transfer variants (fine-tune F only, source-only, train with target).

## Hidden assumptions
1. Cycle-level samples of different cells are the unit; the test cells are cell-disjoint but shared across the scaling statistics (per-cell whole-life min-max, code note section 3), so end-of-life information of the test cell is in its scaled t.
2. The 16 summary features of the last 0.2 V of the CC charge and the CV tail exist for every cycle (full charge to V_end and CV to 0.1 A).
3. G is "independent of protocols and datasets" (Methods p.10); it is frozen in transfer. This is an assertion that the frozen G transfers because it is dynamics; not tested against a frozen generic F or unfrozen G.
4. The PDE residual is informative; with u_t among G's inputs it need not be (code note 6.1).
5. A near-monotone SOH penalty is correct; capacity regeneration is excluded or tolerated.
6. 10 repeats of the same split are enough to describe stability.

## Weaknesses a reviewer would hit
- "Physics" is not a law: G is a black-box MLP, no parameter has a physical unit, and the claim that G "learns degradation dynamics" is not demonstrated by any inspection of G or any recovery experiment. In the code, L_PDE can be satisfied by identity on u_t (code note 6.1).
- No ablation of the physics terms in the main text; improvements over MLP and CNN could stem from the monotonic term, the t input, the architecture sine activations, or the tuning of alpha, beta per dataset (hand-set; MLP, CNN have one fixed configuration).
- Gains are in the low-data regime: with many training cells the MLP or CNN is comparable (p.8; Table 2 for HUST and MIT).
- Text vs Table 2 MLP means disagree (2.60 vs 2.22 % for XJTU; 1.72 vs 1.68 % for TJU). The abstract MAPE 0.87 % is an unweighted mean over four datasets of very different size (55 to 130 cells), not a per-cell or per-sample figure.
- Small test sets (2-3 cells per batch), a single fixed split repeated 10 times, no cell-fold CV, no significance test.
- Code issues documented in the code note: validation not cell-level, no seeds, bug-fix post-publication changed the hyper-parameters, metric argument order bug (MAPE denominator).
- Title says "prognosis"; the paper estimates SOH from the features of the current cycle; there is no forecast of future SOH or RUL (code note 7).
- Features carry little of the operating stress (no temperature, rate, DOD as inputs), although Eq. 1 lists them; so a stress-dependent law cannot be learned.
- Transfer table has no MLP/CNN pre-train-then-fine-tune control; the benefit of "G frozen" is not separated from the benefit of any pre-training.
- Table 4 TJU row source-only: two entries both 0.1266; possible typo, unverified.

## Gaps and hooks for our paper
- Replace the black-box G by a KAN in which the stress variables (temperature, current/C-rate, DOD, SOC, throughput) are explicit inputs, and the physics term is a rate law d(SOH)/dN = g(stress, SOH); the paper's own Eq. 1-2 list those variables as intended x, but x is the 16 charge-window summaries only. Test: does the learned g depend on the stress inputs in the way Arrhenius / power laws predict, on cells with different stress levels (TJU has 25/35/45 C and 0.25-4C; XJTU has six protocols).
- A check of the PDE term: ablate L_PDE and L_mono separately (factorial), with and without u_t in G's input; this is the control the original lacks (our control, not a contribution).
- Identifiability test on synthetic cells with a known law (see GAPS.md item 10): the paper claims that G contains "general information reflecting the nature of battery degradation" (p.8-9) with no recovery test.
- Their transfer experiment (freeze G) is a ready template for a test of law transfer: freeze the stress-dependent law and re-fit only the cell-specific scale; compare against freezing a generic MLP.
- Benchmark protocol to match or surpass: 10 repeats and 6:2:2 by cell, but with the validation cells independent and seeds fixed (our standard). Report cell-fold spread rather than run spread.
