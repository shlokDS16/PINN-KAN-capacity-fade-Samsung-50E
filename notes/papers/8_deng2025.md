## Problem and stated contribution
Predict RUL ("remaining discharging cycles") of fast-charge cells from a short window of raw signals. Claims (Abstract, Sec. 5.3, Sec. 7): (i) a generic two-branch PIML architecture, (ii) a three-step training scheme (data-driven pre-training, physics alignment with frozen extractor, joint fine-tuning) whose result is "lower-bounded" by the pure data-driven model, (iii) an SEI-informed dilated CNN (SEI-DCN) with MAE 15 cycles from 4 cycles of data versus 65 for the same DCNN, and MAE 81 vs 311 on a later batch, (iv) the physics can be swapped (second SEI law) with similar outcome.

## Data
- Severson/MIT-Stanford-TRI fast-charge LFP dataset (Sec. 5.1): batches 2017-05 and 2017-06 for training/testing, 2018-04 as second "unseen" test set. Cycle-life distribution in Table 6 (2017-05: 36 cells at 500-1000, 10 at 1000-1500; 2017-06: 35 at 0-500, 12 at 500-1000, 1 at 1000-1500; 2018-04: 21, 18, 5 in the three upper bins).
- Inputs (Sec. 3.1-3.2, Table 2): voltage, current, temperature; sliding window 2500 s (about 3-4 cycles) with 2250 s overlap, shape [batch, 1, 2500] per channel; window may start and end mid-cycle.
- Label: remaining discharging cycles at the end of the window (Sec. 3.1). So each window from anywhere in the life gets a RUL label; the task is "RUL from a 3-4 cycle snapshot taken at any life stage", not prediction from the first cycles.
- Split of the main batches: "random data splitting equally divides the dataset into training and testing" with shuffled indices (Sec. 5.1). Test cells are numbered 1-35 in Fig. 8 text, so the split appears to be by cell, but the paper never says whether the shuffle acts on cells or windows; stated as uncertain. 20% of training windows form a validation set (Table 2).

## Model
- Data-driven branch: DCNN, 3 Conv1D+BN layers, kernel 3, 64 filters, dilation [1,2,4,8,16], causal padding, flatten (160,000), dense [128,64,1]; about 20.56 M parameters (Table 4). Adam lr 1e-3, batch 256, MSE.
- Physics branch (Sec. 3.3, Eqs. 1-2): Arrhenius SEI-rate D = D0 exp(-Ea / kB T) as the activation function of a custom layer, then p_out = C/D + b, i.e. life inversely proportional to the SEI growth rate (the paper calls this a Paris-law analogue). Only temperature enters the PI branch. D0 = 1.0 s^-1, Ea = 0.122 eV (Table 3); 64 units in the layer preceding the PI layer; the PI branch adds about 190,000 parameters (20,749,976 vs 20,559,681).
- Enforcement: architectural (custom activation functions and a fusion layer), not a loss residual. There is no PDE loss; p (64-dim) is concatenated with the 64-dim data-driven feature h_l and p_out is added to the final output (Eq. 5).
- Three-step training (Sec. 4): (1) train data-driven branch, PI branch frozen with zero output; (2) freeze extractor, train PI branch and final FCNN; (3) unfreeze everything, joint fine-tuning.
- KAN: none.

## Tuning
DCNN architecture "optimised" beyond Hong et al.'s published one (Sec. 5.3, from MAE 65 to 55), by procedures not described in this paper; PI-branch hyperparameters in Table 3. No search protocol, no mention of which data were used to choose the 2500 s window, epochs or the 3-step schedule. Because the test cells were fixed random halves and the best step count was judged on test (Table 7), test leakage into design cannot be excluded.

## Evaluation
- Metric: MAE of RUL in cycles, MAPE in the knowledge-swap test. No RMSE, R2 or per-cell spread in tables; per-cell MAE appears in Fig. 8 insets.
- Table 5 (predicted cycle error MAE, required cycles): shallow MLP 150 (4), MLP 174 (4), Full model [Severson] 99 (100), Variance 112 (100), SVR 245 (100), CNN 82 (4), CNN+LSTM 72 (4), DCNN of Hong et al. 65 (4), DCNN in our research 55 (4), proposed PIML 15 (4). The MLP/CNN/DCNN rows are quoted from Hong et al.; Severson rows are cycle-life prediction from the first 100 cycles, a different task (total life from early cycles), so rows are not comparable.
- Internal inconsistencies: the same DCNN is 55 (Table 5) and 65 (Sec. 7, Conclusion: "conventional DCNN... MAE of 65"); the headline 15 equals the result after the two-step stage in Table 7 (15 after alignment, 11 after joint training), while the paper calls the three-step model its proposal.
- New batch without retraining (Sec. 5.3.3, Table 6, Fig. 9): SEI-DCN MAE 81 vs DCNN 311.
- Knowledge swap (Sec. 6.2, Table 7): SEI-rate law MAPE 10.9 -> 8.4 %, cycle error 15 -> 11 after joint training; SEI-thickness sqrt-time law (Eq. 6) MAPE 12.7 -> 8.3 %, cycle error 17 -> 9. After joint training the "wrong-or-different" physics is as good or slightly better (9 vs 11 cycles).
- Seeds: not mentioned (no grep hit for seed/repeat/standard deviation). Statistical test: none. Hardware: one T4 GPU for inference (3.5e-5 s per sample).
- Per-cell: SEI-DCN worse than DCNN in test cell 5 (MAE 28 vs 22) and above 15 in cells 6-9 and 17 (Sec. 5.3.2).

## Ablations
- Two-step vs three-step (Table 7), two physics laws (Table 7), data-driven branch alone vs with PI branch (Fig. 7-8, MAE 65 or 55 vs 15/11), second-batch generalisation (Table 6). No ablation of Ea, D0 or C (fixed or learned unclear), of removing the temperature-only restriction, of the window length, or of a placebo branch (random-initialised same-size dense branch with no physics), which is the control that decides whether physics matters.

## Hidden assumptions
1. The gain from the PI branch is from physics rather than from extra capacity plus extra training stages; the placebo control is absent, and Table 7 plus Fig. 10 suggest the PI weights shrink after joint training.
2. Test cells and training cells share protocol families (batches 2017-05/06 share charging-policy families), so the main-batch MAE of 15 describes interpolation within protocol families; only batch 2018-04 tests a shift.
3. Window position in life is irrelevant to the reported MAE (an average over windows from 0% to 100% of life).
4. Temperature measurements (thermocouple on the can, with dropouts, Sec. 5.3.2) are reliable enough to carry the physics; the authors note they are not.

## Weaknesses a reviewer would hit
- No physics placebo and no seeds: the 15-cycle result cannot be attributed to the SEI law; Table 7 shows two different laws end up equal after joint training, which is evidence against identifiability of the embedded law.
- Task framing: "using only four lifecycle data" suggests early-life prediction, but the model is trained and tested on windows from all life stages; Table 5 mixes tasks.
- Inconsistent baselines (DCNN 55 vs 65) and headline (15 vs 11).
- Test-based model selection (stage count) and undescribed DCNN optimisation.
- Split unit (cell vs window) not stated, no variability across cells reported beyond per-cell MAE, no interval.
- Data "available on request"; code appears in a GitHub project named pimlphm/PIML-benchmark-architecture that surfaced in a search result (not inspected here).
- Physics is a single temperature-only Arrhenius factor; no current, voltage or time dependence, even though dominant stress in this dataset is the charging current profile.

## Gaps and hooks for our paper
1. Knowledge-swap test as an identifiability protocol: replicate Deng's Table 7 idea with a proper design. For our stress-dependent laws, fit candidate forms (Arrhenius-rate, power law, sqrt-t, Verhulst) as alternatives and report which the data can distinguish via predictive distance and parameter profile; Deng shows the answer can be "indistinguishable" and does not follow up.
2. Placebo control: same architecture, physics layers replaced by an unconstrained same-size layer, plus a version with shuffled temperature; this decides whether the physics matters (absent here).
3. Staged training with a frozen extractor and a "no worse than data-driven" guarantee by construction is attractive: adapt to PI-KAN (train a KAN on stress-free curves first, then fit the stress-dependent rate laws) and test the lower-bound claim empirically across 5 seeds.
4. Use cell-level splits with inter-batch test (2018-04 style) as the generalisation test and report per-cell errors, as Deng's Fig. 8 does, but with spread across seeds.
5. Physics via loss residual on a rate law (dQ/dN = f(stress)) rather than an activation function gives interpretable parameters that Deng lacks.
