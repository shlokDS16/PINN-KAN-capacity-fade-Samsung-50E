# 1. Introduction (contribution list)

## 1.1 Question and scope

How few cycling ageing tests and early check-ups does a physics-informed neural network with Kolmogorov-Arnold (KAN) stress functions need to forecast the capacity fade of held-out cycling conditions of the Samsung INR21700-50E campaign [@stroebl2024]? How much of any advantage comes from the physics law and how much from the KAN? Does the KAN yield a closed-form stress law that predicts held-out conditions at least as well as an equally simplified additive law? The word "few" applies to cycling conditions only: the 132 storage cells stay in training throughout. The scope is held-out cycling conditions of one cell type in three laboratories, and the paper does not claim generalisation to other laboratories. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 1 (question, scope words) -->

The dataset authors fitted a parametric calendar-plus-cycle model to these data and report that its cycle part is inadequate [@stroebl2026]. The present study replaces the stress dependence of such a law by a KAN, enforces the law through a soft physics residual, and tests the result against tuned data-driven learners, the dataset authors' model and a matched additive law, on conditions that no fitting step saw. The related work is in Section 2.

## 1.2 Contributions, stated as design commitments

The list states what is tested and how. It claims no outcome; every outcome, including a negative one, is reported (Section 5.4). The order follows decision D029. <!-- C3: D18 6 / A8 (contribution list, order of D029) -->

1. **A held-out test of a closed-form KAN law against an equally simplified additive law.** A closed-form law is extracted from the trained KAN (A15) and the same extraction is applied to an additive spline model (G15), with one frozen library, one coefficient cap, one selection criterion and one end-to-end refit. Chain K tests whether A15 is non-inferior to the network it came from (K1) and whether it is more accurate than G15 (K2), both on the 40 Test B conditions that the extraction never saw, at full data (Sections 4.6 and 5.4). We name no form or direction for individual extracted effects, because the training design aliases laboratory with temperature.
2. **A matched ablation ladder with tuned comparators.** The network with the physics law (A1) is compared with the same network without the law (A9), with an additive-map law (AG), with the hard-integrated law (A2), with the dataset authors' model (A10) and with the best tuned data-driven learner chosen by inner cross-validation (D\*). Every tuned arm gets the same budget of 40 trials, and the shares of the law and of the KAN are estimated with simultaneous intervals and are never read as a decomposition of the median (Eq. 11). This extends the matched comparisons of Jia et al. and Navidi et al. [AUTHOR: add verified references]; those studies concern lifetime from early cycles with random cell splits and short-term data, respectively [AUTHOR: confirm wording against the full texts].
3. **Learning curves over the number of cycling conditions and of check-ups.** For a joint calendar-plus-cycle law, forecast error on held-out conditions is estimated on a grid of $m\in\{8,16,32\}$ training conditions and $k\in\{0,2,k_{\mathrm{full}}\}$ window check-ups (Section 4.1), and chain P tests at the scarce cell $g_1=(8,2)$ whether A1 beats the best tuned data-driven learner (P1), whether removing the law hurts (P2), and whether the advantage is larger there than at $(32,2)$ (P3). We present this as an extension of the nested learning curve over the number of ageing conditions of Lucu et al. (J. Energy Storage 30, 101409 and 101410, 2020) [AUTHOR: add verified references], of the parameter-uncertainty study of the dataset authors on the same data [@stroebl2026] and of early-trajectory forecasting [@guo2026]. It differs from Lucu et al. in using a joint physics-informed law with KAN stress functions and a matched ablation, and from Stroebl et al. in forecasting held-out conditions with learned laws rather than reducing the parameter variance of a fixed one [AUTHOR: confirm against the full texts].
