# 4. Method

## 4.1 Problem statement and causal window

Every cell $c$ has a stress vector $\mathbf{s}_c$: $(T, \mathrm{SOC}_{\mathrm{mid}}, \mathrm{DoD}, C_{\mathrm{ch}}, C_{\mathrm{dch}})$ for a cycling cell, with $\mathrm{SOC}_{\mathrm{mid}} = \mathrm{SOC}_{\max} - \mathrm{DoD}/2$, and $(T, \mathrm{SOC})$ for a storage cell. With retention $r_c$, the state is the capacity loss in units of ten percentage points,

$$ y_{c}(t) = 10\,\bigl(1 - r_c(t)\bigr), \qquad t_n = t/500\ \mathrm{d}, \qquad E_n = \mathrm{EFC}/500. \tag{1} $$

For a held-out cell the model receives $\mathbf{s}_c$, the reference capacity, the first $k$ check-ups after the reference (the window) and the throughput rate, and it returns $r_c$ at every later check-up. Throughput is the logged one inside the window and the cell's own mean window rate afterwards, so no quantity after the window enters a forecast. Time is global (days since the reference), not a fraction of life. The window length is fixed from the test schedule alone: $k_{\mathrm{full}}=k_{\mathrm{cyc}}=\operatorname{round}(n_{\mathrm{med}}/3)$, where $n_{\mathrm{med}}$ is the median number of intermediate check-ups (regular and extended) of a cycling cell, counted from the member listings before any label is read. The count is $n_{\mathrm{med}}=11$, so $k_{\mathrm{cyc}}=k_{\mathrm{full}}=4$, and $k_{\mathrm{sto}}=\operatorname{round}(6/3)=2$ for storage cells 01 to 03. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 5 --> A cell whose number $n$ of intermediate check-ups is not greater than the window $k$ has its window capped at $n-1$, so that at least one later check-up is forecast; the cap is the same for every arm and every grid cell. It binds for six cycling cells at $k=4$ and for two at $k=2$, and two further cells whose later check-ups the incident rule removes stay unscored for every arm; their identifiers and record lengths after quality control are in Appendix A [RESULT: record lengths]. The nominal window of two check-ups is therefore shorter for two cells at $g_1$, $k_{\mathrm{full}}$ is not four for the six capped cells at $g_4$, and the cap makes a window depend on a cell's record length, which can track ageing speed. The number of held-out conditions stays 40. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 5 (cap disclosure, D033 M4) -->

*Grid of training conditions and window.* We vary two quantities: the number $m\in\{8,16,32\}$ of cycling conditions in the training side of each outer fold and the window $k\in\{0,2,k_{\mathrm{full}}\}$ of a held-out cell. A grid cell is $g=(m,k)$ and there are nine. Three are named: $g_1=(8,2)$, the scarce headline cell; $g_4=(32,k_{\mathrm{full}})$, full data; and $(32,2)$, which has the window of $g_1$ and four times its cycling conditions. The held-out conditions are always the 40 conditions of Test B (Section 5.1), and all storage cells stay in training. Training subsets are nested and chosen from identifiers alone. For each outer fold, a greedy max-min order over the fold's 32 training conditions, in the five stress coordinates standardised over those conditions, starts at the condition nearest their centroid; every later condition maximises its minimum Euclidean distance to those already chosen, ties go to the lower condition identifier, and at $m=8$ each cycling temperature keeps at least two conditions. The subset at $m$ is the first $m$ conditions of the order, and its laboratory and temperature composition is tabulated. At $k=0$ the window holds only the reference check-up: the per-cell latent and offset stay at their prior means, A10 uses its population parameters and the data-only learners use stress and horizon without window features. The case $k=0$ measures what the window carries and is not a tested cell. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 4.1 -->

## 4.2 The physics-informed network

*Law.* Fade follows one rate equation with a calendar term and a throughput term,

$$ \frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t_n} = \bigl[\,a_{\mathrm{cal}} + a_{\mathrm{cyc}}\,\nu(t)\,\bigr]\,h(y), \qquad y(0)=0, \tag{2} $$

where $\nu = \mathrm{d}E_n/\mathrm{d}t_n$ is the throughput rate (zero for storage cells) and $h$ is a single state-dependent modulation shared by both terms. The amplitudes are

$$ a_{\mathrm{cal}} = e^{\delta_c\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{sto}}}\, \exp\!\bigl[\ell_{\mathrm{cal}} + a(\mathrm{SOC}) + E(\mathrm{SOC})\,x(T)\bigr], \tag{3} $$

$$ a_{\mathrm{cyc}} = e^{\delta_c\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{cyc}}}\, \exp\!\bigl[\ell_{\mathrm{cyc}} + f(\mathbf{s})\bigr], \tag{4} $$

with the normalised Arrhenius abscissa $x(T) = (1/T_0 - 1/T)/(1/T_0 - 1/T_1)$, $T_0 = 298.15$ K and $T_1 = 308.15$ K. The pair $(a, E)$ is the output of a KAN on state of charge, so $E(\mathrm{SOC})\,R/(1/T_0 - 1/T_1)$, with $R$ the gas constant, is an effective activation energy that may vary with charge state. The stress map $f$ is a KAN on $\mathbf{s}$, and a cycling cell evaluates the calendar term at $(T, \mathrm{SOC}_{\mathrm{mid}})$. The levels $\ell_{\mathrm{cal}}$ and $\ell_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ are free. The per-cell log-offset $\delta_c$ has prior $\mathcal{N}(0,\tau^2)$ with $\tau = 0.15$ and scales the cycling term of a cycling cell and the calendar term of a storage cell.

The modulation is constant, self-limiting, $h = 1/(1+y/Y_0)$ with $Y_0 = 1$, or accelerating, $h = \exp(\gamma y)$ with $\gamma = 0.3$. One form is chosen per outer fold and per training-set size $m$, on that cell's training conditions only: a tournament of the hard-ODE variant (Section 4.4) at default settings keeps the simplest form within one standard error of the best, and a result from a larger $m$ is never reused at a smaller one [RESULT: form per fold and $m$]. With one $h$ for both terms, Eq. (2) integrates in closed form. Let $\Psi(y) = \int_0^y \mathrm{d}y'/h(y')$ and $\Phi(t) = a_{\mathrm{cal}}t_n + a_{\mathrm{cyc}}E_n(t)$. For piecewise-constant $\nu$,

$$ y(t) = \Psi^{-1}\bigl(\Phi(t)\bigr), \tag{5} $$

where $\Psi^{-1}(\Phi)$ equals $\Phi$, $Y_0\bigl(\sqrt{1+2\Phi/Y_0}-1\bigr)$ and $-\ln(1-\gamma\Phi)/\gamma$ for the three forms. The terms interact only through $h$, so the law is not an independent superposition.

*Solution network and residual.* A tanh network $g$ with three hidden layers of width 32 gives $F(t_n,\mathbf{s},\mathbf{z}_c) = t_n\,g(t_n,\mathbf{s},\mathbf{z}_c)$, which satisfies $F(0)=0$ by construction; $\mathbf{z}_c\in\mathbb{R}^2$ is a per-cell latent code. The physics residual comes from automatic differentiation, as in physics-informed neural networks [@raissi2019pinn],

$$ \mathcal{R} = \frac{\partial F}{\partial t_n} - \bigl[a_{\mathrm{cal}} + a_{\mathrm{cyc}}\,\nu(t)\bigr]\,h(F). \tag{6} $$

It is evaluated on three label-free collocation sets. Set (a) holds times along each training cell's axis, from the end of its record (or from zero; the placement is tuned) to 1.1 times the longest record. Set (b) holds Latin-hypercube points [@mckay1979lhs] inside the convex hull of the training cycling design, with $\nu$ from the design rates. Set (c) holds the calendar twins $(T,\mathrm{SOC}_{\mathrm{mid}})$ of the cycling conditions, with $\nu=0$.

*Training and inference.* The loss is

$$ \mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\Bigl[\overline{(F-y)^2} + \lambda\,\overline{\mathcal{R}^2}\Bigr] + \frac{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|^2}{\tau^2 N} + \frac{\|\mathbf{z}\|^2}{N} + \Omega, \tag{7} $$

where overbars are means over observations or collocation points, $N$ is the number of observations, $\sigma = 0.05$ (0.5 points) is a fixed nominal noise, and $\Omega$ combines a spline ridge penalty with a sparsity penalty (L1 and entropy of edge magnitudes). The weight $\lambda$ ramps from 0 to its tuned value over the first 40 % of the steps. The optimiser is Adam [@kingma2015adam] (rate $5\times10^{-3}$, cosine decay to 10 %, 2000 steps) with a global gradient-norm clip of 20, applied to every gradient-trained model in the study so that the comparison with $\lambda=0$ has no optimiser confound. The optimiser recipe (gradient clip, residual warm-up) was fixed after a divergence on a synthetic design; the SNL interaction pairs were fixed from the training-design structure; neither used Samsung data. <!-- C17: PREREG_v4 3.3(10)(b) --> For an unseen cell, with network and law frozen, $\mathbf{z}_c$ and $\delta_c$ are obtained from the window data plus the residual over the forecast horizon by one routine for every arm that infers them: a per-cell Levenberg-Marquardt Newton iteration in double precision, started at the prior mode, accepting only steps that do not raise the cell's objective, and stopped at a gradient norm of $10^{-6}$ (relative) or after 100 iterations. A cell that does not converge keeps its last accepted iterate and is counted. This is a computational convention adopted because fixed-step Adam loops had not converged and had unequal budgets across arms; a development rule on secondary-data training sides did not pass, and we do not claim that the convention passed it. No label after the window is used. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 7.5 --> <!-- src: code/pinnkan/law.py, pinn.py (constants as of the draft commit) -->

## 4.3 KAN parametrisation

A KAN layer maps $\mathbf{x}$ to $y_j = \sum_i [\,w^{b}_{ji}\,\mathrm{silu}(x_i) + \sum_m c_{jim}B_m(x_i)\,]$ with cubic B-splines $B_m$ on a uniform grid of $G$ intervals [@liu2024kan]. The implementation is our own. Stress inputs are scaled to $[-1,1]$ by fixed physical ranges (5 to 50 C; state of charge 0 to 1; DoD 0 to 0.8; charge rate 0 to 1.2C; discharge rate 0 to 2.2C). The cycling map is a KAN $[5, h, 1]$ with hidden width $h\in\{1,2,3,4,6,8\}$ and $G\in\{2,3,5,8,12\}$ (the width $h=1$ lets the map collapse towards a near-additive form when only eight conditions are available); the calendar KAN is $[1,2]$ with $G=5$. Without a hidden layer, KAN $[5,1]$ is an additive spline model (arm A4).

## 4.5 Tuning and seeds

Each tuned arm receives the same number $N=40$ of Tree-structured Parzen Estimator trials [@bergstra2011tpe; @akiba2019optuna], with the sampler seed set to the outer-fold index, at every pair of outer fold and $m$; failed trials are redrawn until $N$ valid ones exist. Inner folds are grouped by condition, stratified by temperature and drawn inside the outer-training conditions only: two for $m\in\{8,16\}$ and three for $m=32$. An arm is tuned once per outer fold and $m$, scored in inner CV at $k=2$ for $m<32$ and at $k_{\mathrm{full}}$ for $m=32$, and the tuned configuration is then applied at every $k$ of that $m$; the data-only learners retrain at each $k$. The objective is the primary endpoint computed out of fold, with the one-fit score of Section 5.2. A timing run at the freeze commit tests a trigger: if the median hard-ODE fit exceeds 30 s or the PINN fit exceeds 90 s, every arm uses 10 trials and two inner folds at $m=32$ [RESULT: whether the trigger fired]. A1 searches six dimensions: $h$, $G$, the spline ridge weight, the sparsity weight, $\lambda\in\{10^{-3},\dots,10^{2}\}$ and the collocation placement (other spaces: Appendix B). The test fold is never read by tuning, selection or a margin. Final refits use seeds 0 to 4, the deployed forecast of a seeded arm is the mean of its five refit forecasts (Section 5.2), and seeds are never units of analysis. Each outer fold and $m$ has one search, so search-to-search variation is not in the intervals, which we state as a limitation.

All computations ran on CPU, float32, one thread, torch 2.11.0+cu130 (CUDA build executed on CPU), numpy 2.4.2, Python 3.14.3; bitwise reproducibility is claimed on the same machine and library versions only. <!-- C17: PREREG_v4 3.3(10)(d) --> [AUTHOR: processor and memory]. ## 4.6 The closed-form law (A15) and its matched additive law (G15)

A15 extracts a closed form from a trained KAN and G15 does the same for the additive terms of A4. A frozen symbolic library, committed with a hash before any outer prediction and identical for both, offers per edge: zero (pruned), linear, quadratic, power, logarithm, exponential, Arrhenius (temperature only) and hinge. Each surviving edge is fitted on a fixed grid over the fold's training hull and chosen by the Bayesian information criterion, with at most two free coefficients per edge. With the coefficient counts of the code, this cap leaves zero, linear and logarithmic forms eligible on every edge, so the extracted law is monotone in each input by construction; the other forms stay in the hashed library but can never be selected. The free coefficients are then refitted end to end under the same loss. A15 starts from a KAN with $h\in\{1,2\}$ tuned like A1; G15 starts from A4. Calling the two laws "equally simplified" means that G15 is simplified by the same procedure (library, cap, criterion and refit), not that the two laws have equal size or extraction loss; A15 with $h=2$ has more edges. The calendar KAN is the same in both arms and is extracted identically, so the contrast K2 isolates the cyclic map. Coefficient counts and the training-side criterion value of both laws are printed beside K2. K1 and K2 are scored on Test B conditions the extraction never saw. The extracted cyclic laws are printed in full as closed forms, with a descriptive stability table, and we do not name forms or directions of individual extracted effects, because the training design aliases laboratory with temperature and the extraction has no laboratory term. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 7.3 items 1, 2, 3, 6(a) --> The tests of this step are that a noiseless synthetic law is recovered, that A15 with an untruncated library reproduces A1, and that A15 and G15 coincide when $h=1$ and the outer edge is linear.
