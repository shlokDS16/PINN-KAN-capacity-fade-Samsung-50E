# 5. Evaluation protocol

## 5.1 Splits

Splits are by cell and, for cycling tests, by condition: no cycle of a test cell and no cell of a test condition is seen in training or tuning. They are generated from identifiers only and hashed before scoring. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 4 -->

*Test B (primary).* The 40 cycling conditions form five folds of eight, stratified over temperature and stress. The held-out conditions are the same in every cell of the grid of Section 4.1, so every contrast is paired by condition across the grid. Only the training side is thinned, to the first $m\in\{8,16,32\}$ conditions of a nested label-free order, and all 132 storage cells are always in training. The setting $m=32$ is the unthinned training side. The laboratories are fixed design strata: each condition is assigned to its majority laboratory (HM 17, INT 19, SIE 4 of 40 conditions), a map fixed from identifiers before any score. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 4, 4.1, 9.1 -->

*Test C (secondary, descriptive): a held-out temperature-laboratory domain.* All 13 conditions at 35 C (39 cells, all from laboratory INT) are held out; training uses cycling at 23 and 45 C (27 conditions, 108 cells, not thinned) and all storage cells, with the window $k_{\mathrm{full}}$. Laboratory and temperature are aliased, so Test C is never called temperature extrapolation, the title omits 35 C, and any abstract sentence on it adds that the two cannot be separated in this design. INT cells at 23 C stay in training, which separates them only if there is no laboratory-by-temperature interaction. The grid does not apply to Test C. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 4, 11 (Test C wording) -->

*S2 (secondary, descriptive): storage transfer.* The 19 storage conditions form five folds of consecutive state-of-charge blocks within each temperature, with all cycling cells in training; only cells 01 to 03 are scored, and results are also given for state of charge up to 0.8. S2 has no tier word and no test. Two exploratory analyses, held-out replicates (Test A) and leave-23-C-storage-out (S3), make no claim. ISU and SNL are not part of the confirmatory run list. An optional descriptive replication on ISU (the Test B analogue at two cells, scarce and full data) is reported as "also holds" or "does not hold" with its own number of conditions and is never pooled with Samsung. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 3.4, 4 -->

## 5.2 Endpoint

A grid cell is $g=(m,k)$ with $m$ the number of training cycling conditions per outer fold and $k$ the number of check-ups in the window of a held-out cell (Section 4.1). Three cells are named: $g_1=(8,2)$, the scarce headline cell; $g_4=(32,k_{\mathrm{full}})$, full data; and $(32,2)$, which has four times as many training conditions as $g_1$ and the same window. A cell's forecast set is every later regular or extended check-up that survives quality control, with at least one per scored cell.

The primary endpoint is the cell-level retention RMSE in percentage points over the forecast set, averaged within condition and then over conditions. The scored forecast of an arm with seed-dependent refits (A1, A2, A4, A5, A9, A12, A13) is the mean of its five refit forecasts at each check-up, in retention space; arms without a seed-dependent step (A10, R1, R2 and the $k=0$ references) are unchanged. A non-finite forecast is replaced seed by seed, before averaging, with the persistence forecast and counted. The chain K contrasts are the exception: they score A1, A15 and G15 by the per-seed condition RMSE, because the claim concerns one closed-form law. The per-seed score is a descriptive row beside every score. The ensemble lowers each seeded arm's score by its own seed-variance share, so arms without seed variance gain nothing from it, a property of the model class. A1, A9, AG and A12 pay five fits at deployment and A11 and A10 pay one, and selection uses one-fit scores while deployment uses the ensemble; time and parameter count are reported for both. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 6 -->

The unit of analysis is the held-out condition, with equal weight. Secondary endpoints are error by horizon, remaining-useful-life error for cells that cross 80 %, MAE, MAPE, $R^2$, run time, parameter count, the number of training cells each arm used and the share of non-finite forecasts. The window-information gain is estimated for every arm as the change from $k=0$ to $k=2$ and to $k_{\mathrm{full}}$. The windows $k_{\mathrm{full}}-1$ and $k_{\mathrm{full}}+2$ are sensitivity rows at $g_4$ only. The replicate spread is computed on training sides as a descriptive reference and is never a margin or gate.

## 5.3 Contrasts and test

For a contrast with target arm $T$ and comparator arm $C$ at grid cell $g$, and held-out condition $c$, let $S$ be the condition score and

$$ L_{c} = \ln\bigl(S_{C,c}/S_{T,c}\bigr), \tag{8} $$

so that $L_c>0$ favours the target. Every contrast of Section 5.4 is a test on these $n=40$ log ratios. *Tier N* (non-inferior) requires the median of $L_c$ to exceed $-\ln 1.10$, so the target is at most 10 % worse; *tier S* (superior) requires it to exceed $\ln 1.02$, so the comparator is at least 2 % worse; *reverse tier S* is the mirrored test. The label is S, N, none or reverse S. Beside every tier we report the mean paired difference in points with a bias-corrected and accelerated bootstrap interval [@efron1987bca], the worst-condition error and the share of conditions won. A point-scale margin of $0.02E$ and $0.10E$, with $E$ the comparator's inner-CV score on the training conditions, is written to `margins.json` before any final refit meets a test condition and is descriptive. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 9.2 -->

*Test.* For a margin $\mu$ let $x$ be the number of conditions with $L_c>\mu$ (ties are not positive and $n$ is not reduced). The p-value is the upper tail of a beta-binomial distribution,

$$ p(x)=\sum_{j=x}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\,\frac{B(j+a,\;n-j+a)}{B(a,a)},\qquad a=\frac{1/\rho_{0}-1}{2}, \tag{9} $$

where $B$ is the beta function and $\rho_0=0.03$ is the assumed average pairwise correlation of the sign indicators, so $a=16.17$ and the design effect is 2.17. A node is rejected at local level $\alpha$ if $p(x)\le\alpha$. At $n=40$ this needs 30 positive conditions at $\alpha=0.025$ and at 0.0195, 31 at 0.0125 and 32 at 0.0055. We call it a sign test with a fixed design effect (assumed average pairwise sign correlation at most 0.03) and never call it exact or distribution-free. The reason is a simulation, not a result: with shared fold and laboratory effects the plain exact one-sided sign test [@dixon1946sign] exceeds 1.2 times its nominal size once the intra-fold correlation of the latent log ratio reaches 0.03 to 0.05, and is 3 to 4 times nominal at an intra-fold correlation of 0.10 with an intra-laboratory correlation of 0.05, while the design-effect rule stays below nominal over the region simulated. The plain exact test is reported beside every gated and secondary contrast as an independent-conditions row; it never sets a state or a tier and cannot raise one. The estimand is the median held-out condition, with an order-statistic interval [@thompson1936median]: the unadjusted 95 % interval is $[L_{(11)},L_{(30)}]$ and the interval simultaneous over the nine grid cells is $[L_{(8)},L_{(33)}]$. A relative reduction in error is $X=100\bigl(1-e^{-\mathrm{med}\,L}\bigr)$ %, and an excess is $Y=100\bigl(e^{\mathrm{med}\,L}-1\bigr)$ %, each with its interval transformed the same way; the abstract speaks of the typical held-out condition. Blinding exclusions can reduce $n$ below 40, in which case the counts are recomputed from $n$ by the same rule; the simulation covered $n=40$ only. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 9.3, 9.7, 3.3(11), section 1 (estimand) -->

*Power.* At $n=40$, with 30 positive conditions required, 80 % power needs a relative reduction of the median condition error of about 0.24, 0.20 and 0.14 when the logarithms of the two arms' condition errors correlate at 0.6, 0.75 and 0.9 (simulation with right-skewed errors; at 0.75 the figure is 0.20 to 0.23 for right-skewed and normal errors and 0.26 for left-skewed errors). The correlations are assumed, not measured. Thirteen conditions are too few for such differences, so Test C is descriptive. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 section 1 (Power) -->

## 5.4 Claims, multiplicity and the outcome table

There are two confirmatory chains, tested in one sequentially rejective graph at family-wise level 0.025, one-sided. The graph is Bonferroni-based and valid under any dependence of its tests; every node uses the test of Section 5.3. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 9.4 -->

*Chain P (the physics law).* **P1** asks whether A1 has a lower median condition error than D\*, the best tuned data-driven learner, at $g_1$ (tier N, then S). D\* is the learner among A9, A11 and A12 with the lowest inner-CV score on the training conditions of the outer fold at that $m$, never the one that is best on the held-out conditions. **P2** asks whether the same network without the physics law (A9) is worse at $g_1$, and is tested only after P1 reaches tier S. **P3** asks whether the advantage over D\* is larger at $g_1$ than at $(32,2)$:

$$ D_c = L_c(g_1) - L_c(32,2), \qquad L=\ln\bigl(S_{\mathrm{D}^*}/S_{\mathrm{A1}}\bigr), \tag{10} $$

with margin 0, tested after P2. Equation (10) is paired by condition and uses the same window, forecast set and caps at both cells; the tuning protocol follows $m$ (two inner folds at $m=8$, three at $m=32$), so a P3 pass has two readings the design does not separate: fewer cycling conditions, and noisier two-fold selection at $m=8$.

*Chain K (the KAN).* **K1** asks whether the closed-form law extracted from the KAN (A15) is non-inferior to A1 at $g_4$ (tier N). **K2** asks whether A15 is more accurate than the equally simplified additive law G15 at $g_4$ (tier N, then S), and is tested only after K1.

*Levels.* The initial local level is 0.0195 on P1 (tier N) and 0.0055 on K1, a split of 0.78 and 0.22 chosen by a rule fixed in advance and applied by simulation, so P1 needs 30 and K1 32 of 40 positive conditions. A chain passes its level along its own path and to the other chain only when its last node is rejected; once the P chain is complete, K1 is tested at 0.025 and needs 30 of 40. The choice of weights trades the chains: in simulation the P chain through P3 gains 0.12 to 0.16 in probability of completing and K1 loses 0.04 to 0.05. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 9.4 -->

*Safety findings.* The reverse tests of P1 and of K2 are run regardless of the graph, one-sided at 0.025 each (30 of 40), and are labelled safety findings. A further sign test, of A1 against carrying the last window check-up forward (R1) at $g_1$, sits outside the graph, adds no alpha and enters the outcome table as a modifier (M3). A sign test of the median of $d_c=L_{\mathrm{law},c}-0.5\,L_{\mathrm{total},c}$, with $L_{\mathrm{law}}$ the A9 contrast and $L_{\mathrm{total}}$ the D\* contrast of A1 at $g_1$, decides whether the extent of the advantage is described as "most" physics; it also sits outside the graph. We state that a positive median of $d_c$ is not the same statement as $\operatorname{med}L_{\mathrm{law}}\ge 0.5\operatorname{med}L_{\mathrm{total}}$, and both medians are printed. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 9.4, 9.8 rules 4 and 5 -->

*Secondary family.* Five qualifiers form a second graph at 0.025: A1 against the hard ODE A2 (Q-H) at $g_1$ and $g_4$; A1 against the dataset authors' model (Q-A10) at $g_1$ and $g_4$, for both forms of A10, with the weaker comparison setting the tier; and training with and without the storage cells (Q-T, A1 against A13) at $g_1$. With one node per test the graph is Holm's procedure [@holm1979], with 33, 32, 32, 31 and 30 positive conditions at the levels 0.005, 0.00625, 0.00833, 0.0125 and 0.025. A test whose S node is not rejected never releases its level, so a family that ends mostly at tier N is more conservative than Holm's procedure on the N p-values alone. The reverse test of each qualifier runs at the slot's final level and outside the graph. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 9.5 -->

*Estimated, without tier words or error control.* A1 against D\*, A9, AG (the better of the additive spline models A4 and A5), A2 and A10 at all nine cells, each with a simultaneous 95 % interval. The ladder splits the log ratio of A9 to A1 per condition into the law's share with a non-KAN map and the KAN's share inside the law,

$$ \ln\frac{S_{\mathrm{A9},c}}{S_{\mathrm{A1},c}} = \ln\frac{S_{\mathrm{A9},c}}{S_{\mathrm{AG},c}} + \ln\frac{S_{\mathrm{AG},c}}{S_{\mathrm{A1},c}}, \tag{11} $$

and the two terms are reported as medians and as mean log terms with bias-corrected and accelerated intervals. Medians do not add, so Eq. (11) is never read as a decomposition of the median. Also estimated are the window-information gain, per-fold medians, per-laboratory counts, medians and intervals (the range of the order statistics where no pair attains the level, as for the four SIE conditions), the Test C contrasts and NC-2. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 9.6, 9.1 -->

*Outcome table.* Titles and abstract sentences are fixed in advance for every outcome in one closed table (PREREG_v4 section 11), approved before any data were scored. Its states combine a gate G, the P state (physics law helps at $g_1$, or not shown, or reversed), the K state (closed-form law better than, as good as, or worse than the additive law), the modifiers M1 to M3 and fixed qualifier sentences, evaluated in that order on the tiers after the downgrade rules of Section 5.6. No row requires a choice after the data. "Outperforms" is used only at tier S and "matches" never. Every outcome is reported in the text, including those in which the physics law is not shown to help at $g_1$, the closed-form law is no better than the additive law, or the hard ODE or the dataset authors' model is better than A1. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 11, 9.8 -->

## 5.5 Controls and synthetic recovery

*NC-1, label invariance (gate G).* The control detects any flow of held-out post-window labels into predictions, tuning or selection through the code. Every held-out cell's post-window retention is replaced by values scaled per cell and perturbed, with window points and training cells untouched, and all arms and seeds re-predict. The control passes only if every prediction is identical to the bit and the tuning and margin records are identical. It runs in every confirmatory run, and once in full on a synthetic design at the candidate frozen commit. A failure trips gate G: no title and no claim. The control cannot detect what does not flow from labels through the code, such as human selection after looking, or covariate use such as the design hull. A post-window outlier that the quality-control rule drops shortens the retained record, so the forecast set, and the window of a short cell, depend on post-window labels through that rule; the control holds the retained set fixed and the dependence is shared by every arm. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 10 (NC-1) -->

*NC-2, is the stress dependence needed (interpretive).* A1 is rerun at $g_4$ with the stress vectors permuted among conditions, separately in the training and the test set, each cell keeping its own throughput, times and window. Its condition scores are compared with the real run by the sign test of Section 5.3 (30 of 40 at 0.025). A pass licenses the Discussion sentence that the accuracy of the learned law depends on the stress dependence it learns; a failure removes it. NC-2 is a partial control only: it does not separate the gain of the per-cell latent and window inversion from the gain of the law, which P2 addresses. It changes no tier and is not run on Test C. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 10 (NC-2) -->

*Positive controls.* PC-1 uses a well-specified synthetic generator, G0, built on the real Samsung design: the structured arms (A1, A2, A4) must recover the log-rate surface on a fixed set of 500 Latin-hypercube points of the hull with a normalised RMSE of at most 0.15, and a failure trips gate G. The synthetic generator shares choices (a) (one state modulation) and (b) (per-cell offset on the cell's terms) with the fitted law: recovery on G0 is a pipeline check, not evidence about real cells. <!-- C17: PREREG_v4 13 (circularity sentence) --> Two further checks are reported and are not gate inputs: that the fitted calendar rate rises with temperature at every state of charge inside the storage hull (PC-2), and that A1 beats persistence (R1) at $g_4$ with margin 0 (PC-3). No other synthetic study is run. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 10, 13 -->

## 5.6 Downgrade rows, sensitivity rows and physical checks

Four sensitivity rows have a consequence. They are the laboratory-stratified row (the stratified weighted median of $L_c$ with the laboratory strata of Section 5.1), the laboratory-and-stage-indicator row on Test B (including A10 and D\*), the retain-every-check-up row (outlier rule off) and the random-subset row at $g_1$ (three seeded random nested subsets in place of the max-min subset, for A1 and the D\* candidates). Each recomputes every gated and secondary contrast it covers at $g_1$ and $g_4$, and $(32,2)$ for P3, with the counts of Section 5.3 at the contrast's final local level and without rerunning the graph. The tier is placed on the scale reverse S, none, N, S; the lowest tier over the primary analysis and the four rows is the tier in the abstract and in the states of the outcome table, so a title never carries a stronger tier than the abstract sentence. Whenever the sign of the mean paired difference in points disagrees with the median log ratio of a gated or secondary contrast at $g_1$ or $g_4$, the abstract adds the mean difference with its interval and the worst-condition error. Test C enters the abstract only if the laboratory-indicator row leaves the sign of its median ratio unchanged. <!-- src: PREREG_v4 9.8 rules 1-3 -->

Appendix C reports about twenty further rows that have no consequence for any tier. These include per-laboratory and per-stage differences, leave-one-laboratory-out, two-stage bootstrap, a dependence row that recomputes each gated tier at $\rho_0\in\{0,0.01,0.02,0.03,0.05\}$, A10 and cell-window variants, AT and storage cells 04 to 06 included, oracle throughput, and changes of the margins, the clip, the warm-up and the modulation settings. The Results add one fixed sentence for each gated tier: "The [tier] holds for an assumed average sign correlation up to [value]." <!-- src: PREREG_v4 9.9 (a)-(q) -->

Physical checks are descriptive, with no test and no claim beyond "consistent" or "not consistent". They cover the effective activation energy against the dataset authors' calendar fit on the same data (consistency, not independent validation), the storage rate above 80 % state of charge, calendar monotonicity in temperature and the share of predicted fade attributed to the calendar term. The signs of the cyclic partial dependences are compared with those of the dataset authors' cycle model, except for temperature, for which no direction or verdict is given because temperature is aliased with laboratory. Storage at 10 and 45 C is laboratory-confounded, which is printed with the activation-energy result. [RESULT: Figures 5 and 6] <!-- src: PREREG_v4 11a items 1-5, 7, 9 -->

## 5.7 Limitations that follow from the design

The confirmatory test assumes an average pairwise sign correlation of at most 0.03, and this assumption is not verified. A training-side estimate of the correlation is made after the freeze and before any Samsung test fold is loaded; it is descriptive, its calibration is unknown, and it is not evidence for the assumption. If its intra-fold point estimate exceeds 0.03, we state: "The training-side estimate of the sign correlation exceeded the value assumed by the test; the confirmatory tiers rest on that assumption."
